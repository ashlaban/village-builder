The round goes on without it, and the pair is not free to anyone else meanwhile. A refusal waits for the next round. Why: the matcher marries as it goes, and each marriage changes the homes and heirs the next choice reads (survey, below), so the round cannot be held open in one step. (2) The player's wish for a match is either "not this year" or a partner. A partner is offered before the matcher chooses, if the round's rules allow the pair and its score, with the noise at its mean, reaches `MARRIAGE.minScore`. (3) An offer is made in the man's village's round (VB-242), and nothing is polled between rounds: the day's end reads only the first offer's due time. (4) "Son or daughter of the house" is a child of the holder, of the holder's spouse or of the holder's parent who lives in the household and is not in service. (5) The cost of consent is a memory, `refusedMatch`, -0.2 for 3 years, in the spokesman's record of the pawn that refused.

Cost (Rules for cost, 9). At `medium-ref` (1,005 living, 211 households, 26 villages) one year from the fixture gives 13 marriages, so 13 offers a year, and 1,455 pairs scored; at `small-y40`, 7 marriages. Without a player every offer is answered at once and none is stored, so the round's work is today's plus four O(1) `spokesmanOf` calls a match. With a player, one stored offer for each match that touches the played pawn or a member it speaks for: about 0.06 a household a year, and never more than the played household's unmarried adults. Nothing is polled between rounds. `st.offers` is sorted by due time, and the day's end compares the clock with its first entry only (Rules for cost, 4). VB-241's calendar makes that one daily entry, guarded by an open offer. VB-275 adds two `opinionOf` calls for each scored pair, 1,455 pairs a year at `medium-ref`.
