## Notes
- Kim, 23 November. A chore alone is not interesting: what matters is fresh water access, and second what share of the daily plan the fetching takes. The decision is the player's when placing the farm: how much of the daily chore budget the site is worth.
- Decided by Kim: walked, not charged (the job stays, its length the real walk), and of high priority; a pawn who is really thirsty sees to it. A farm by a river or fresh water has the access of one with a well. Wells are a household's and a village's: a house costs less to found in a village with a well, and a household frees more time by digging its own. A well's cost is flat a well and rises with the depth to the water.
- Open, for Kim: does the daily fetch grow with the household (more people, more water, more trips) or stay one trip a house? What makes the waterside cost something, so that it is not simply the best site (damp ground, flood, less field land, few such tiles)? Does a source freeze or dry?
- Today (sim/chores `water`): half an hour, once a day, one hand, at the village well if there is one, else by the house wherever it stands; distance plays no part. VB-626: 6 to 9% of household-days dry, nearly all with a hand free.
- Revises a Decided point (11 November), by Kim's word. VB-1484 measures the lone settler's case and feeds the ranking; VB-1486 puts the siting choice into solo.
