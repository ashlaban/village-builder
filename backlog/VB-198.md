# VB-198: The route cache bounded
Status: done
Priority: 2
Size: S
Arch: yardstick
Phase: implement
Design ref: World and scale (Walking and terrain). `docs/roadmap.md`, sections 5.3 (rule 8, bound every cache) and 5.4; VB-097's first criterion, moved here.
Goal: `st.pathCache` keeps one route for every pair of tiles ever walked, and it is saved with the state. In populous small at seed 7 it holds 8,212 routes at year 5 and 25,723 at year 40 (114,874 tiles, 1 MB of the save). On every day a tile's walking cost changes, `refreshRoutes` tests every route: 91 ms over 30 game days at year 40. Bound the cache, as rule 8 asks, and measure what that saves. Blocked until VB-193 (the fixtures) is done.
Acceptance criteria:
- [x] Measure on `small-y40` and `medium-y60` first: entries, MB in the save, `routeHit`, `routeSearch` and `routeRefresh` per game day (VB-192), and ms per game day in `refreshRoutes`.
- [x] Bound the cache at `ROUTE.cacheMax` entries (start at 20,000, VB-097's figure; tune by the hit rate), dropping the least recently used: a hit moves its entry to the end of the Map, and an insert past the bound drops the first. The order is the Map's, so it survives a save; `checkState` asserts the bound.
- [x] Hit rate, searches a day, ms a game day and the save's MB before and after, on both fixtures. The hit rate falls by at most 5 points, or the bound is raised until it does.
- [x] A dropped route may be searched again and come out different from the stale copy, so routes can change: run the golden check, and if it changes, re-record it and list the changed entries (its runs are short, so it may not change).
- [x] VB-097 points here for its first criterion and keeps HPA* for the day routes cost too much.
- [x] `npm run verify` passes.
Cost: one Map delete and set per hit; `refreshRoutes` then tests at most `cacheMax` routes a day.

## Implementation decisions (6 November 2026)
- Keep the cache in saves, as requested by the user on 5 November. Rebuilding is not guaranteed to choose the same route:
  90 of 25,723 routes in `small-y40` differed from fresh A* results (the first had equal cost but different tiles).
  A one-day empty-cache probe did not diverge, but this does not establish deterministic continuation in general.
- The initial 20,000-entry limit failed the hit-rate criterion on `medium-y60`: 96.189% to 83.824% (12.365 points).
  At 40,000: 91.368% (4.821 points below baseline), but serial total time rose about 8%.
  Final bound: 60,000 on both maps; 93.530% hits (2.659 points below baseline), with about 3% total time overhead.
  This retains the memory bound with fewer repeat searches; it is not claimed as an overall simulation speedup.
- New `routeEvict` work counter counts the bounded insertion and legacy-load trimming at the owner.
  Its tiny-fixture baseline is 0; the focused route tests exercise nonzero eviction, while tiny scaling checks assert none.
  All previous work-counter baselines stay unchanged.
- Golden changes are only `pathCache` (Map order) in founders/heirs, seeds 7/42, years 1/2/3: 12 entries.
  World, pawn, household, farm, job, RNG, event and other fingerprints are unchanged; no public Sim API change.
- Focused tests cover direct/reverse hits, least-recent eviction, re-search, reverse fallbacks, the bound invariant,
  saved LRU order, the same next eviction after reload, and trimming a pre-bound save immediately on load.

Their golden stamps are stale; exactly the same saved states were used before and after. Each window advances 30 days in daily calls.
The baseline is unchanged source `1fbac6b`, extracted separately under `/tmp`; the production checkout was not reset.
The cache stays in format-1 saves. Old saves lack access history, so their existing Map order supplies the initial eviction order.

Timing is diagnostic, not a hard guarantee. Serial medium runs measured refresh 19.07 -> 13.34 ms/day
and total simulation 654.13 -> 674.47 ms/day. Refresh work and serialized cache size fall about 40%;
refresh time falls about 30%, but the extra searches add about 3% to total simulation time in this sample.
Smaller trial caps of 20,000 and 40,000 caused 2,066.37 and 1,101.80 searches/day respectively;
60,000 was chosen to avoid most of that cost while still bounding history growth.

Households, not only averages: on small-y40 all 183 households' hungry-day deltas match (163 household-days total).
On medium-y60 the total is 134 -> 133, but benefits are mixed: 7 households improve by 10 days in total,
6 worsen by 9 days; the other households are unchanged. Worst increase is household 526, 0 -> 4 hungry days;
these are changed routes and work timing, not a hunger-balancing improvement. The per-household differences
are retained in the measurement artifact. A bounded cache can choose a different route after eviction; this
was explicitly allowed by the task. Saving and loading a run made with the new bound still must match its unbroken continuation.

Final quiet small-y40 timing: refresh 2.41 -> 4.25 ms/day; total simulation 144.76 -> 168.57 ms/day.
A repeated quiet baseline measured refresh 2.52 ms/day and total 136.22 ms/day. Thus the small-map total
sample is 16-24% higher with LRU. Its working set is below the bound, so it gets no entry-count reduction
and pays LRU bookkeeping overhead; this is a material tradeoff, retained in the report rather than hidden.
Wall timings vary and are diagnostic; this is a bounded-memory change, not a claim of faster simulation.

Reproduce: fetch fixtures, then `node tools/route-bench.js small-y40` and `node tools/route-bench.js medium-y60`.
An optional third positional argument overrides cacheMax for a trial, e.g. `node tools/route-bench.js medium-y60 30 40000`.
