# VB-462: Days owed: work one household owes another, called and worked on the creditor's plan
Status: blocked
Waits for: VB-310
Priority: 3
Size: S
Arch: debt
Phase: implement
Design ref: design.md, Loans, obligations and cases (Obligations; Remedies); Households and other bodies (The alderman's keep); backlog/VB-404.md
Goal: Three of slice 5's mechanisms take work, not grain: a debt's remedy (VB-315), the alderman's keep and a patron's due. Build the days once, as an obligation kind and a basis of eligibility, so that each source only says how many days and why.
Acceptance criteria:
- [ ] An obligation of kind `days` in `sim/obligations` (VB-310): debtor and creditor households, days left, the year's end it runs to, and its source (`debt`, `keep`, `patron`); live ones in the due-queue (rule 4), ended ones out of the live index (rule 1).
- [ ] Calling: on its planning day the creditor's holder calls days it has work for, for a day at least `DAYS.notice` (3) ahead, at most one adult of an owing household a day; a household below its floor, or with its own harvest task waiting that day, sends nobody and the day stays owed. A verb in `src/sim/commands.js` (`callDays`), so the player as holder calls by it and is told why not.
- [ ] Working: the called adult takes tasks of the creditor's plan on the basis `owed` (the table of bases), for 8 work hours; he walks there and home and carries his meal (nothing teleports). A compiled per-pawn index (pawn to the creditor he works for today) keeps a task's check one lookup (rule 6).
- [ ] Each field keeps the year's work hours given by its user household's own members and by others (two numbers a field, zeroed after the class is reckoned; in the `@state` line, with invariants), written where work hours are booked: the class (VB-318) reads them. Hired hands (VB-319) book to the same pair.
- [ ] At the year's end: days of a debt stand (VB-315 says what follows); days of the keep and of a patron lapse, told once in the record.
- [ ] A test on a constructed state (`tests/tiny.js`): a day called is worked and booked to the field as others' hours; a household with its own harvest waiting is not called away; an uncalled keep day lapses and a debt's does not; the worker is on the creditor's field at noon and home at night.
- [ ] Cost (rule 9): obligations of this kind at `medium-ref`, at most one a pair of households a source (some hundreds); calls on the creditor's planning day; one index lookup a task check. `daysCalled` as a work counter; measured at `small-y40`.
Notes:
- 15 Nov 2026: written by VB-404's plan, to be built once and used by three sources: VB-315 (a debt's remedy, which asked for a basis `debtor`: it is this basis, with the source `debt`), VB-463 (the keep) and VB-464 (a patron's due).
- 15 Nov 2026: Decision (Deciding without Kim, 7 and 8): the days are called when the creditor has work, not fixed to the harvest, because the measured hours say the peaks have little to give (walking and carrying take 30 to 47% outside winter; farmers are idle 16 to 33% in summer and autumn) and Kim's Idle labour point makes idle hours the source; an owing household's own harvest comes first. A close call: history's day-works fell at the lord's harvest.
