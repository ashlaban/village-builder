- 16 Nov 2026 (phase three; Kim, present, on phase two's two choices and on tin; the plan's sections 1, 13 and 14). (A) Skill and quality: "A shoddily made spade will break more easily, and can have a worse edge; a worker's making skill can affect all aspects of quality, and production speed." Five grades of workmanship: "shoddy (malus), sound (neutral), good (small bonus, quite common), fine (medium bonus, rare), masterwork (large bonus, rarer still)"; a piece's grade is drawn at its making from odds that shift with the maker's skill; it acts on everything the kind's properties do, and on worth; counted, no record a piece. This moves the decision of 11 Nov (skill sets only the rate): for made goods, quality too. Assumed by the orchestrator and not contradicted by Kim: skill never multiplies a crop's yield. (B) Millstones: granite too, a fifth slower; this moves the decision of 11 Nov (sandstone). (C) Tin only: copper's chance is not raised; the trial's 8 of 21 small maps is the expected result, and D16's licence for VB-734 is withdrawn.

- Decided without Kim, with reasons in the plan (D19 to D30) and as `decision` notes: graded are tools (their ironwork), whetstone, grindstone, quern, millstones, pot, kettle, cloth and dress, bell, memorial stone; not graded are materials (yarn among them), food and ale, the thing of worth (by weight: a close call against the orchestrator's list) and what is built (houses, facilities, boats, carts); a farm's tools have one grade, drawn when the set is forged; a grade scales life and working but not what a thing holds; a head keeps the best, owes in the least and gives for standing in the best; the five are called workmanship, since a house has a grade.
