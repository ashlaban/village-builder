Kim, in the session, on the draft's eight choices: (1) the island's own metals enter with the mill, slice 6's second half, as goods of place; the bell's church use and coin wait for the church and the Crown; this moves the decision of 11 Nov (metals with the ship). (2) Bronze is household goods and the bell: a copper kettle that wears, a cast bronze pot as dowry and heirloom, a village bell the meeting decides and the alderman carries out; iron first. (3) Tin doubled (0.25 to about 0.5) and in granite hills, so that bronze by land trade is possible on most maps (against the draft's "keep it scarce"). (4) Silver before coin is a good of worth only: kept, given, worn, buried; at a swap at customary worth; barter until coin stands; the lost hoard is its sink. (5) Any household digs a found vein by hand, as clay, and owns the ore; a mine in shares later. (7) Gold as silver, at about ten times the worth. (6, 8) Answered by a principle, now `AGENTS.md`, Deciding without Kim, 17: a good is what it is for and is made of a class of material; the kind makes a better or worse one; quality is the kind's physical properties, with no grade number; a whetstone is one good of slate (the keener) or sandstone, which is the only grindstone.
