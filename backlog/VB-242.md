# VB-242: The meetings and the moving round, a village a step: survey and tasks
Status: done (2026-11-10)
Priority: 2
Size: S
Design ref: Inheritance, expansion and other bodies (Leaving home); Customs and culture (the meetings). `docs/architecture/target-v2.md`, section 3.3. Kim, 10 Nov 2026 (code review).
Goal: `movingRound` (`src/sim/lifecycle.js`) handles every village in one six-minute step, and to stay near the 60 ms limit it keeps a dependency-tracked cache of heir rankings that is invalidated by reading the event record. Kim decided on 10 November that each village holds its own meeting and its own round, on the same days as today, a village a step: the game is about locally supported community, and a village's step is small enough to rank heirs afresh. Survey what the split touches and write the implement tasks.

What a lone farm is: every farmstead has a village (`settlementIdOf`, `src/sim/records.js:176-180`), and a lone farm is a village of one farm (`design.md`, Every farm in a village), so it holds its own step like any other. At `medium-ref` 4 of 26 villages hold one household, at `small-y40` 1 of 25.

The order of villages (decision 1). Today the round asks every unmarried man of the island in one list, those with a place (holders and first heirs) first, then the oldest. By villages, every man of village 0 asks before any man of village 1: a young landless man of village 0 asks before an old holder of village 25. A woman of a later village may be chosen by an earlier village's man before her own village's men ask; a woman of an earlier village is chosen by a later village's man only if no man of her own village took her. Lower ids are the older and larger villages (the first third of ids held 154 of the 333 men who asked, below).

Measured with a static matcher (scratch, not committed) on 38 states: populous small at seeds 1 to 6, years 5, 10, 15, 20, 25 and 30, plus `small-y40` and `medium-ref`. Homes, places and means are as at the start, nobody is married as it goes, and each pair's noise is a hash of the pair and the year, so the three orders see the same men, scores and draws. Of 486 unmarried men, 333 would ask (best score at least `minScore`, the go-ahead draw passed). "Lost" means the woman he scores best was taken before his turn.

In id order the first third's men lose their best-scored woman about half as often (21% against 38%) and marry more often (44% against 36%); the middle and last thirds lose more. The losses move to the villages that ask later. Married in all: 131 today, 133 by village id, 134 turned. Being first measurably favours a village, so VB-280 proposes turning the start by the year. The study's shares are not the game's, since no marriage changes the next choice; VB-277's real runs report the same measures.

What stays of the heir cache (decision 2). `roundHeirs` (`heirs.js:103-147`), `watch`, `family` (`heirs.js:32-36`, `60-62`), `changedSince`, `homes` and the sweep of `handed` go: VB-277 removes the round's use, and VB-279 the rest. Two of the four parameters the task named stay:
- `nested` is not a cache. It is the direct-heirs-only ranking that `ownFirstOf` (`heirs.js:93-100`) needs for the rule "an heir to their own parents' farm" (`heirs.js:73-89`).
- `ownFirstSet` has no dependencies to track: a pass that ranks many households while nothing changes computes the island's first direct heirs once (`countDemand`, `firstHeirs`, `placeIfMarried`, `refreshInterests`). `tests/lib/heir-rankings.js` (VB-224) checks the candidate-lines shortcut against it. On cost it is neutral. Ranking every household of `medium-ref` without it took 2.6 to 4.3 ms (574 rankings), against 5.5 to 19 ms with it (422 rankings, the set included). At `small-y40` it took 16 to 23 ms (1,109) against 12 to 27 (338).

Cost (Rules for cost, 9). Measured with a prototype of the village step (scratch, not committed). It is `marriageRound` for one village's men, with a memo of `heirRanking(st, hh)` cleared after every marriage or handover, and handovers for the village's farms and the homes its marriages chose; it leaves out kin pressure and the village store. It ran on each fixture advanced to 11.9 h of the round's day, three runs each, while other agents ran on the machine, so single times are noisy.

The longest village step is expected at 15 to 30 ms against the 60 ms target. Most of a step is fixed: building the pool from the living, ages, the widowed, and roles. The rankings come next, 229 at most. The round costs more in all (about 3.6 times the rankings), but it is spread over 26 steps once a year: a few ms a game day over the year's mean. The meetings' village steps are expected under 15 ms: the largest village holds 11% of the living, and the island-wide stages took 33, 52, 16 and 13 ms at `medium-ref` and 45, 15, 10 and 17 ms at `small-y40` (cold, one call each, stages 0 to 3).
