# VB-824: Succession is offered and taken up: the holder hands on, the heir answers
Status: ready
Priority: 1
Size: M
Arch: acts
Phase: implement
Design ref: design.md, Inheritance, expansion and other bodies (Succession: Handed on, and taken up; Handover and shares); docs/plans/VB-682.md, section 4.6
Goal: A handover in life is the holder's offer, which the first in line answers; an inheritance at a death is taken up or renounced by the heir; no farm changes hands by the calendar.
Acceptance criteria:
- [x] In life: at the time the timing custom gives (the heir's marriage; the holder at 60), the holder offers `handover` (VB-690) to the first of `heirRanking`, due at the round, giving the holding and wanting its keep on the farm for life (retired there, as built). The round's `handover` loop over a village's farms is replaced by the offers due. A played holder may offer at any time, by the same verb.
- [x] The heir answers: yes, unless it holds a farm, lives on a spouse's, or is betrothed to a place (VB-825, if landed) with more fields; a no passes the offer to the next in line at once, and books no memory. The passing-over rules of the ranking (`ownFirst`, "keeps their place") are unchanged, and a test shows the answers give the same heir as the ranking does today in each of its cases.
- [x] At a death: the inheritance falls at once to the first in line (a right), as `succession` does today. The heir takes it up by `move`, reason `heir` (VB-822), with spouse and children, or renounces it when it holds a place with more fields, and it falls to the next; with nobody to take it the farm stands empty, as today. A minor heir is not asked: the guardian rule stands (VB-695).
- [x] Shares, the reserve and what is owed are untouched (Handover and shares).
- [x] Numbers before and after, populous small, 30 years, seeds 3, 7, 11, 42, and founders small, 50 years, seeds 7 and 42: handovers a decade by reason (marriage, age, death), offers refused and renounced inheritances, farms left empty, holders of 60 or more still holding. With no played pawn the same heirs are expected; differences are listed with their cause.
- [x] `tests/commands.js` and `tests/record.js` pass; tests on a constructed state: an heir with a larger farm refuses and the next takes it; a played holder hands on early; a death with a renouncing heir.

Noted by VB-682, 16 November 2026.
- 18 Nov 2026 (Kim, in the session on the playtests of 16 November; `QUESTION_ARCHIVE.md`, "VB-1122"; raised to priority 1): there is no automatic handing over of a farm. The elder chooses to hand on, the player for its pawn as every holder for itself; the custom's time (the heir's marriage; the holder at 60) is when the village expects it, not when it happens, and a holder who holds out pays in the heir's opinion. So the first criterion reads: the holder offers `handover` when it chooses to, by a rule of its own for a pawn nobody plays (VB-1122's plan gives it its numbers; until then, at the custom's time unless its opinion of the heir is ill), and nothing offers for it. Handing on does not change whom the player plays: succession, for the player, is at death alone (VB-1121). Background: historically the handover was a contract the holder made (undantag, sytning; flatföring in the provincial laws, offered to the heirs first), with a named keep, and many held until death.

## Implementation (18 November 2026)
- **The line and its answers** (`src/sim/heirs.js`). `heirRanking` takes a last argument `asked`, an array that gets the whole line in
  the order asked, `{id, no}`: `no` is why that one would not take the farm (`holds X`, `lives at X`, or, under `ownHeir: keeps`,
  `is heir to X`), else null. The ranking is the line's yes-sayers in that order; nothing in the passing over changed. No new export
  (the module is at its width baseline).
- **In life** (`src/sim/marriage.js`). The round's loop over a village's farms is gone. `handover(st, hh, rank, due)` is the unplayed
  holder's own rule: at the custom's time (the one it expects to take the farm is grown and, under `marriage`, married and living there;
  under `age60`, the holder is 60), unless its opinion of that heir is at `SOCIAL.enemy` or below, it offers the basis `handover` to the
  first grown one in line. It considers this at its heir's wedding into the house (the offer due at once, as the handover followed the
  marriage before) and on its birthday (`sim/lifecycle` tells a holder's birthday as the event `holderYear`, `sim/asking` hears it; the
  offer due at the next moving round, where the village's step answers it first, as `ASK.order` has it). Nothing offers for a played
  holder: it offers by the verb `offer` when it chooses, to the first in line only (the check refuses anyone else, and `gives.passed`
  from anyone but a refusal). Handing on leaves `st.player` alone.
- **The answer.** The one asked answers alone (the holder's yes is the offer): yes unless the line says no. A no is told
  (`offerRefused`, "X would not take over F: holds G"), books no memory, and the holder offers the next in line at once
  (`gives.passed` carries who has said no). A played heir answers itself, with its pawn's judgement as advice and as the answer if it
  gives none. A yes is the `handOn` verb as before; farms are never combined, whoever says yes (the settle refuses it).
- **At a death** (`succession`). Unchanged in who takes it (`heirFor`, the widow's rules, the guardian for a minor); each grown one in
  line ahead of the taker who holds a place is told as renouncing (the new event kind `renounced`, kept for good, under "Land and
  farms"). The taker moves by `move`, reason `heir`, with spouse and children, as `transfer` already did.
- **Shares, the reserve and what is owed**: `transfer` is untouched.
- No new state. `tests/lib/invariants.js`: a life offer is known by its basis's `where` (a handover has no subject), and two feasts
  entered by one household in the same hour (two weddings of one house) are summed, where the second used to overwrite the first: the
  check failed on `tests/occasions.js` once both weddings' feasts were laid out.

## Decisions (Deciding without Kim)
- **Any farm held is a no, whatever its size.** The second criterion asks that the passing over stay as it is and the answers give
  the same heir; the design's Proposed line says "only for a place with more fields". Weighing the fields moves heirs between farms
  and needs a rule for the farm left: noted as VB-1141. Betrothal (VB-825) has not landed, so it is not weighed.
- **When an unplayed holder considers it**: the heir's wedding, and its own birthday (one call a holder a year, no pass over farms:
  Rules for cost, 4 and 5). A holding whose time comes between the birthday and the round waits up to a year longer than before.
- **The offer is nobody's `pawn.ask`.** VB-692 held it as the heir's; open from the birthday to the round that would keep an unmarried
  heir out of the round's matching. Held as the holder's it kept a widowed holder who asks a match each season from offering. A holder
  has one handover open at a time.
- **Ill opinion** is `SOCIAL.enemy` (-0.5), the line an unplayed pawn refuses a match at, until VB-1122's plan gives the rule its
  numbers. A parent starts at +0.3 towards a child, so it takes repeated breaches; at the end of the six runs one holder (founders 42)
  stood at enmity with its heir.
- **A played heir at a death** takes up or renounces by its pawn's judgement, as every heir does; asking it is VB-1142. The page has
  no control yet for a played holder's offer: VB-1140.
