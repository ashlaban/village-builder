# VB-404: Plan: where the elite comes from
Status: needs-review
Priority: 2
Size: S
Arch: debt
Phase: plan
Design ref: Balance loops and targets (The balance review, 12 November); docs/balance/cards.md; docs/balance/findings.md; Land and rights (slice 5); Loans, obligations and cases (slice 5)
Goal: Slice 5 is designed around one road to the landed elite: a lean year lends grain, default passes a field, the old family pays rent. The balance review tested it in four mock models. At the food ratio the game has (1.64 to 1.86 times need, under 1% of households hungry) that road gives at most one elite household in 150 years; rent and interest come to 6 to 10% of output; it is won by hard lenders, not generous households; and it starts only once the map is full (about year 150). An elite of the target's size (75 of 1,000, 12 to 15 households) came from office and a tithe, which no loop opposes. Kim's own road, from hunger through gifts and goodwill to land and prestige, moved 70 fields in 250 years. Decide the roads to the 75 with Kim, then rewrite `design.md` (Land and rights; Loans, obligations and cases; Households and other bodies: Class) and slice 5's tasks to fit.

- Why two mocks disagreed: the aggregate model never ran short, so nobody borrowed; the household model assumed a full island from year 1. With both in one model, land concentrates only when the map is full and the food ratio after that is low (a land Gini of 0.20 at year 150 at a ratio of 1.7; 0.53 with tenancy 32% at 1.24).
- A tithe is a tenth of everything every field produces (12.7% of net output); rent is a quarter of the grain on tenant fields only (3 to 4% of output at a tenancy of 15 to 19%). Living on rent alone needs about 89% of all fields under tenancy.
- A silver tax whose arrears become debt was the strongest concentrator in the mock (tenancy 58% by year 250); it belongs to slice 8 and is noted for VB-188.
- Kim agreed patronage on 12 November (large one-sided goodwill worked off as days on the giver's farm, and weighed in the debtor's vote). As worded it was negligible in the mock, because the owed work is cleared within a year or two: question 4.
