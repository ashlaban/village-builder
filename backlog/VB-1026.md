# VB-1026: Page: plan mode, the head's review as a place
Status: blocked
Waits for: Kim
Priority: 2
Size: L
Design ref: docs/mocks/plan-mode.html; backlog/VB-256.md; design.md section 7 (The plan panel); AGENTS.md, Deciding without Kim, 14 to 16
Goal: At its review the head sees what the plan costs in hands and days, what it changes against the custom's, and what came of the last one, and changes it on the map.
Acceptance criteria:
- [ ] A moment. On the played head's planning day and at each season's first review the game reminds the head that the plan is due; an option turns these reminders on or off (Kim, 17 Nov), and with them on the game pauses and offers the mode. The head may open it at any other time. Leaving it goes on with the plan as it stands. A pawn that is not a head can open it read-only, as the Plan tab is today.
- [ ] The constraint is shown, member by member (Kim, 17 Nov). The season as its days, each routine with its window, who is put to it, and the days it is estimated to take in those hands; for each member the days there are, those the daily chores take, and those the plan claims. Putting the right pawn on the right job is the planning: the head sees what each member's skill makes of a job (faster, a better grade) and what the job makes of the skill (what it would grow to by the season's end), and moves a routine from one member to another. A change to the order, a target or a project redraws this before it is confirmed, and what no longer fits is named (which routine slips, to when).
- [ ] The custom's plan is the proposal; the head's is a difference. The mode opens on what the head would do with nothing changed. Each change the head has made is listed, with its estimated cost and gain, and can be undone one at a time. Both plans can be looked at, one against the other.
- [ ] The map is the surface. With the mode open, the tiles that would take the next field or meadow carry both the tile's fertility and the head's estimate of its yield (Kim, 17 Nov: the first is the land's, the second an estimate with its range), and the walk to it; each field and meadow carries its next step and window; the tile named and a project's tile are marked. A tile is chosen and changed there. When no tile would do, the reason is shown at once, not after a refused click (VB-1027 holds the Plan tab's case).
- [ ] The loop is closed. The mode shows, for the plan the last review made, what the head estimated beside what came of it (stores, the windows kept or missed, the hands), so that a player learns whether a choice mattered.
- [ ] The household is planned as an enterprise (Kim, 17 Nov), so its people are part of the plan: who works this season, who left or joins and when, and where a hand could be asked for (the moving round, a match); each links to where that is done.
- [ ] Every estimate is the head's (principle 14): it says whose it is, it has a range where the head's judgement gives one, and a head of poorer judgement sees a poorer estimate. What has happened is shown as it was.
- [ ] Every change goes through a verb any head has (principle 16, `Sim.head`): the mode gives the player no choice the automatic head lacks. The room for the player is in judgement, not in verbs (Kim, 17 Nov): the automatic head plans by custom and its own reckoning, well enough to reach subsistence and a little more in most years; a player who reads the mode and works the rules does better and makes a surplus. The mode must make that difference findable. Where a requirement above needs a choice no verb covers, the task that builds it adds the verb for every head, or leaves the choice out and says so.
- [ ] Usability comes first (Kim, 17 Nov), and is what the task is judged by: a player who has not read the design finds, within the mode and without a tooltip, what will not fit this season, why, and what to change. The plan for it names how this is checked (a playthrough as a new holder, written up, before and after).
- [ ] As little text as will do (principles 15 and `design.md`'s icons before words): a routine, a good, a member, a skill, a window and a state are shown by the icon and the meter the page already has for them (`src/page/icons.js`, `page/meter`), reused, never a new picture for a thing that has one; a new icon only for a new concept, added to the one set. Words are labels and the reasons for a refusal; numbers sit behind the meter. The mock is wordier than this allows.
- [ ] One presentation (principle 15): a routine, a target, a member and a field look the same here, in the Plan tab and in the timetable (VB-477); the season's strip and the day's are one object at two lengths, and a day of the season opens the day.
- [ ] Cost (rules 7 and 9): the estimates are worked out on demand while the mode is open, never in a step, and the plan names their cost (households, times the season's routines, times the work of one estimate) and measures it on founders and on a late save.
- [ ] The mode's functional test in the page's tests; a screenshot in the PR; `node tools/same.js --base REF` prints `same` if no verb was added.

What this leaves open, for the task that plans it:
- Whether the mode is a screen of its own, the farm's card grown large, or a layer over the map; the mock is one answer, not the requirement.
- How the days a routine takes in a member's hands are estimated (the timetable's ranges of VB-476 and VB-477 are the likely source), and how skill enters before VB-360 (skills) is built: the per-member strip can come first, by role and age, and skill join it.
- Whether the Plan tab stays as the read-only summary, or goes.
- Whether this is one task or a plan task with implement tasks under it (size L says the second).
