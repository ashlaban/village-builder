Goal: A household under any levier answers a hard day by the same judgement, and the difference between leviers is in their terms
Acceptance criteria:
- [ ] Verbs `substitute`, `name` (another day), `ask` with the bases `release` and `transfer` (answered by the levier's agent by its need and opinion), `commute` (goods for the day at the terms' rate, from the household's store through sim/stores with a reason) and `breach` on the levied side; `release` and `transfer` on the levier's (the alderman lends a day of his keep to a household in need); each allowed only where the terms allow it, its check saying why not.
- [ ] The default author tries them in that order; breach is the head's judgement by its own estimate: what keeping the day costs (the windowed work it displaces, in rations) against what this levier does (`onBreach`: a memory, the meeting's fine; a case, an estate's penalty and eviction named, not built); no threshold of its own.
- [ ] A levied adult dead or ill on its day is substituted, or the ladder tried, at once; a levy about to lapse unworked is the levier's to carry over (where `carry`) or let lapse; a debt's days unworked within their year go to the meeting as Remedies says.
- [ ] Decision points added to VB-1757's list: an outer levy laid and a day to name, a named day that cannot be kept, a levied adult dead or ill on its day, a levy about to lapse, a breach to answer, an ask for release to answer.
- [ ] Q11: breaches by household size with the crop at 1.0, 0.8 and 0.6 (the weather forced), and with onBreach a memory against a fine; the share settled by release or transfer first: reported.
