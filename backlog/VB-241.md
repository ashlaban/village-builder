# VB-241: Refactor: the calendar, one ordered table of what runs each step, hour, day, season and year
Status: ready
Priority: 2
Size: M
Arch: between
Phase: implement
Design ref: none (code structure). `docs/architecture/target-v2.md`, section 3.3 (The calendar). Kim, 10 Nov 2026 (code review).
Goal: `onHour`, `endOfDay` and `stepEnds` (each six-minute step of `advance`; `src/sim/clock.js`) call each system in a fixed order, and each system then tests the date for itself (`c.doy === HERD.springDay`, `c.season === 2 && c.sday === SEASON_DAYS - 1`, `c.sday === 0`), as `stepEnds` does for the moving round and the meetings' stages. The order decides outcomes and is written nowhere. Target-v2 (3.3) asks for one declared schedule; Kim asked on 10 November that it be formal: entries by step, hour, day, season or year, or at a named time.
Acceptance criteria:
- [x] `sim/clock` holds one table, `CALENDAR`, in the order things run. An entry: `{every: 'step' | 'hour' | 'day' | 'season' | 'year', at: {season?, sday?, doy?, hour?, tick?} (the place within that cadence; absent: every time), when?: (st, c) => boolean (a guard that is not a date, such as `st.cenv`), owner: '<module>', run: (st, c) => void, note: '<what it is, in a few words>'}`. It is code, never saved; it is frozen with the rules registry's seal.
- [x] `stepEnds`, `onHour`, `endOfDay` and `newYear` become one loop over the table for what the step reached. Every date test that decides whether a system's yearly, seasonal or daily work runs moves from its module into its entry's `at`: the moving round (`stepEnds`, `clock.js:111`), the meetings' stages (`stepEnds`, `clock.js:110`, one entry a stage, by `tick`), the meeting's call (`callMeetings`, `clock.js:53`, autumn day 10 at midnight), spring in `sim/herds` (`herdsDay`, `herds.js:168`), the autumn count (`byreDay`, `byre.js:213`), the season's last day in `sim/fields` and `sim/meadows` (`fieldsDay`, `fields.js:150`; `meadowsDay`, `meadows.js:125-126`), `fadeRelations` (`dailyLife`, `lifecycle.js:336`), `Stores.prune` (`newYear`). The entries added to the clock since 10 November are in the table too: `occasionsDue` each step (VB-126), `nightCulture` on the steps after midnight (VB-201; its test of the step, `lifecycle.js:351`), `dueOffers` and `takeIn` at midnight (VB-293, VB-308; `takeIn` guarded by `shareFood`'s short list), and `foldHistory` daily (VB-199; its `c.doy === 0`, `records.js:46`). A date test that is a condition of the work itself, not of when it runs, stays where it is (the sowing window, the hay days, a job's urgency): list in the PR which were moved and which were left, and why.
- [x] Work spread over pawns by `(day + id) % period` (`dailyTies`, `relations.js:69-70`) stays in its module: it is one daily entry. Things due at a time for one thing (a queue in `st`, Rules for cost, 4) are not built here: the two queues there are (`st.offers`, `st.occasions`) stay their modules', each read by one entry (`dueOffers`, `occasionsDue`).
- [x] `node tools/sim.js --report calendar` (or `tools/rules.js`) prints the table: cadence, place, owner, note, in order. `docs/architecture/modules.md` and `ownership.md` (section 1, the rules `sim/clock` holds) follow; target-v2 3.3 gets a line that the table exists.
- [x] A test in `npm test` holds the table's order against a recorded list (an intended change of order re-records it and says so in the PR), and that every entry's owner is a module of the manifest.
- [x] Refactor rules of `BACKLOG.md`: behaviour unchanged, `tests/golden.json` and `tests/counters.json` unchanged, `npm run verify`'s hard checks pass, its targets reported with their residuals. `node tools/bench.js`: simulation ms a day and the longest step no higher than before beyond the runs' spread (the loop over the table must not allocate a step).
- [x] The PR says what fell: date tests outside the clock, and lines.

- Orchestrator's decisions for Kim to overturn (VB-241):
  - The table is frozen as `sim/clock` loads, not at the registry's seal: `sim/clock` is the last module before `Sim`, so nothing could
    register into it later, and an entry is added by editing the table.
  - `at.hour` and `at.tick` may be ranges `[from, to)`; `tick` counts steps from the start of `at.hour` (hour 0 when absent) and may run
    past it (the night's culture, tick 1 to 40). A date (`season`, `sday`, `doy`) is allowed only on 'season' and 'year' entries: a
    once-a-year step, such as a meeting stage, is `every: 'year'` with `hour` and `tick`.
  - `run` gets `(st, c, n)`, n the step of the day (the night's culture needs it); an undated, unguarded 'step' entry gets `c = null`, so
    the walk computes no date on steps where only such entries run.
  - The short list from `shareFood` reaches `takeIn` (both at midnight, entries apart) through one variable in the clock's closure,
    written and read within the same step, so never seen across a save; not a state field.
  - The autumn count moved from inside `byreDay`'s loop to its own entry right after it: the state after the day is the same (on the count
    day nothing in the byre's loop after the count runs, the herds not being stalled, and the count reads no other household's hay or
    larder), but on that day the record lists all the households' spoilt meat before the counts rather than household by household.
    The whole-state comparisons above, the record included, came out identical.
  - The frost runs after all of `fieldsDay` rather than before its untended-fields pass: the two touch disjoint farms (held / empty).
  - Owners: inline work in the clock is owned by the module whose rule or state it is (the evening release and the dark's end
    `sim/agents`, the meals and fire `sim/hunger`, the review of plans `sim/jobs`, its hours checked against `sim/plans.reviewHour` by the
    test); the weather, empty-farm decay and fence decay stay `sim/clock`'s, as before.
  - `tools/perf.js` adds the split functions to their systems (`dailyDeaths`, `spring`, `autumnCounts`, `meadowsSpring`, `meadowsFrost`)
    so the profile's categories stay as they were.
