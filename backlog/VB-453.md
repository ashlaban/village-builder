# VB-453: A job's urgency is worked out once a round, not once a bid
Status: needs-review
Priority: 3
Size: S
Design ref: none (cost; AGENTS.md, Rules for cost 6). src/sim/jobs.js: score, urgency, bid, planRound, lookUp, offered
Goal: The planning round scores each open job for each idle pawn that may take it (`bid`, `score`), and a pawn looking up from its work scores its household's jobs again (`lookUp`). A score's dearest part is the job's urgency (`urgency(st, j, c)`), which takes no pawn: the same job is weighed again for every pawn that bids on it. Work a job's urgency out once a round and read it for each bid.
Acceptance criteria:
- [x] Within one allotment of a household's day (`allot`, sim/jobs) and one round of help (`helpRound`, `offered`), a job's urgency is worked out at most once; nothing is kept past the call, in `st` or beside it. (Reworded 15 Nov: `planRound`, `bid` for a household's own work and `lookUp` are gone since VB-466.)
- [x] Behaviour unchanged: `node tools/same.js --base REF --scenario founders,heirs,populous` prints `same`. If an urgency turns out to change within a round (an assignment earlier in the round moving a store it reads), the task says which, and keeps those worked out each time.
- [ ] Measured before and after on populous tiny, seed 7, 4 years: sampled time under `allot`, `score` and `helpRound`, and the year lines.
Notes:
- 15 Nov 2026: opened from the investigation of the simulation's slowdown (Kim's report). About 3,000 bids a game day (`matchBid` 22 a living pawn-day).

- Not looked at here: the route searched for each assignment (about 2.8 ms a game day under `assign`), which Kim set aside on 15 November.

Criteria 1 and 3 reworded to that; the saving is smaller than measured in the note above (after the merge, populous small, seed 7: `urgency` 1.5% and `score` 2.4% of `Sim.advance`).

It lives in the call and is dropped with it; nothing in `st` or the closure. No urgency was seen to change within an allotment or a round (the `work` commands of `allot` come after its pairs are scored; `assign` after all bids), and `same.js` agrees.
