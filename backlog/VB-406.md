# VB-406: The village store: why most of its grain rots
Status: done (2026-11-13)
Priority: 2
Size: S
Design ref: Balance loops and targets (The balance review, 12 November); docs/balance/cards.md; docs/balance/findings.md; Goods and the farming year (Help in hunger)
Goal: Each year a household gives the village store a quarter of its surplus (`HELP.storeShare`, `fillStores`, `src/sim/help.js`), and the store loses a fifth a year (`ECON.villageStoreLoss`, `villageDay`, `src/sim/stores.js`). Measured on populous small (seeds 3, 7, 11; 50 years), 10 to 11% of the grain harvest goes into village stores, 68 to 85% of that rots there, and 10 to 22% is given back out: the store takes in far more than hunger asks of it, since hungry households are under 1%. Measure its flows, say why, and propose the rule that would make it a store and not a sink. This task changes no behaviour: its proposal goes to Kim as a question.

- The commons' share. The store does not tell its grain apart, so the rot is shared by what came in: the common fields' harvest is 13 to 18% of the inflow on populous over 50 years (seed 7: 13%, about 52,000 of the 399,000 rations rotted; 18 to 21% over the first 15 years, 10 to 18% in the last ten) and 17 to 19% on founders; net of the seed the store gives back to them, 9 to 12%. The store's growth in years 2 to 4 (seed 7: 21,207 in) is 73% filling and 27% commons. So the commons add about a sixth to a sink that was already there; but their harvest alone (seed 7: 63,622, or 44,547 net of seed) is ten times what is ever given out (4,634). A common field is begun when the store holds under 10 days (`COMMONS.storeDays`) and is then sown every year whatever the store holds: 10 to 13 of 22 to 27 villages still harvest one in years 41 to 50, at stores of a year and a half of need.
- The cause, with numbers (populous seed 7, the 43 years every run reached):
  - Little is asked: 1% of what comes in is given out (4,076 of 392,931), 2.1 households a year, 45 rations each, since under 1% of households are hungry, kin are asked first, and an ask is once a season. That is the root: about 95 rations a year are wanted of a store that takes in 9,100.
  - The filling rule is the whole of the loss in the long run: it takes a quarter of every surplus every year whatever the store holds, so the store rises until its rot equals its inflow (inflow over the rate: five years of inflow at a fifth a year). Capped at half a year of the village's need, the rot falls 74% (326,725 to 84,649) with the same hunger; at a share of 0.1, 44%.
  - The rate is none of it in the long run. At 5% a year (a household's first year) the window's rot falls 35% (to 211,139), but only because the store is still growing: it stands at 165,025 rations, 5.4 years of need, and climbs towards twenty years of inflow, where everything not asked for rots all the same. The rate sets the store's size, not the loss.
  - Kept at home, the same grain would have lost 5% in its first year and 20% in a second (`ECON.storeLoss`): 21,000 to 84,000 of seed 7's 419,000 filled, against 83% in the store. Not measured: what the households would then have done with it (eaten, sown, fed to animals, or kept into the 40% years).

No rule moved hunger (0 to 3 hungry household-days a year in every run), and under the cap as many households were helped as before, or more on seed 3; households are neither better nor worse off, the grain stays with those who grew it. Under a cap the common fields become the main inflow (47% at half a year, 85% at a quarter, where a low store starts more of them) and what rots is then mostly theirs: a cap on the filling alone leaves 71% of a smaller inflow rotting. - Decision (Deciding without Kim): nothing is changed. The filling rule is a Decided point of `design.md` (Goods and the farming year, Help in hunger), so the proposal is a question (`QUESTIONS.md`, VB-406), and the numbers a row of `docs/tuning.md`. The proposal: fill only up to a target (`HELP.storeTarget`, half a year of the village's need) and sow a common field only while the store is under it; the rate stays. It meets VB-404's answer 3 (the alderman keeps a quarter of what households give the store): under the cap the filling falls from about 430 to 85 rations a village-year, so that keep falls from about 108 to 21 rations a year; the question says so.
