Orchestrator's decisions for Kim to overturn:
- Drying is fishing for the rack (a trip of its own, its catch carried home and laid by the rack), then 1 hour of splitting and hanging
  at the rack beside the house. The weeks the fish hangs are not modelled: from the hanging it is dried fish in the larder, food in store
  that may be eaten. Rain does not spoil fish on the rack (the weather branch could).
- The season: spring day 3 (the ice gone) to summer day 6, the drying winds of spring and early summer in which stockfish and klippfisk
  were dried. It keeps a year (the good's 48 days), so spring's fish lasts to the next spring, "to the next summer" in the criterion.
- A ration of fresh fish gives a ration of dried fish: drying takes out water, not food.
- How much: a quarter of the household's need over the 14 days without fresh fish (a quarter of the pot, above VB-119's fifth for a
  source), rounded up to whole lots of 10 rations, about 20 rations a household. Lots of 10 are the good's quantum when it changes
  hands; the larder holds rations.
- The cook puts dried fish in for a quarter of the meals only out of the fishing season, so that it is kept for the winter; otherwise it
  is eaten as larder food (when soon to spoil, or when meals and grain fall short).
- Urgency: fishing for the rack 0.5 (a side craft's, as ale and digging; above calm fishing's 0.2); hanging 1.6 (salting's: the catch
  does not wait). A catch waiting by the rack does not spoil (it waits hours, not days).
- Who builds a boat: every household by the sea or a large lake with two working hands, no boat for its house and no project plan, at
  the start of the drying season, where its village has a wood; so most shore households have one within five years. Its place: the
  nearest bank by the sea or the large lake within 6 steps of the house. A household that moves leaves it behind. The boat serves every
  fishing trip of its household (for food and for the rack), at twice the bank's catch. A possible follow-up: fewer boats (a need or a
  chance), as the boats free more hours than the craft fills.
- 6 loads of timber (two trees) a boat, felled by VB-328's timber trips in the village's wood and carried to the shore; 2 hands on its
  20 hours.
- A boat plan blocks a land project while it stands (one project plan a household, as VB-123); a boat whose village loses its wood
  would wait for timber until the plan is dropped (not seen in the runs).
