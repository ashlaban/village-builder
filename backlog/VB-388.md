Decisions (13 Nov 2026, Deciding without Kim):
- "The winter's fish share of the pot" is read as the most fish a pot of two sources holds with grain still a source: `WINTER_FISH`
  = 1 - `ECON.potShare` = 0.8 (`sim/pot`), derived, not a new tunable. The pot's winter cap on dried fish (a fifth, VB-119) becomes that
  share, and the drying want (`Drying.wanted`) is that share of the need over the 14 days without fresh fish (a household of 3 rations:
  20 -> 40). A fifth would have shrunk the want (0.25 -> 0.2) against the criterion's "grows", and a larger store eaten at a fifth would
  only rot into the next year. `DRY.share` and `DRY.until` are gone; the drying season is that of fishing for the pot (spring day 3 to
  the end of autumn).
- What a household "will eat before it spoils": its need over the fish's keeps (3 days). On each catch for the pot (`Drying.potCatch`,
  called by the fish kind's deliver) the fresh fish above that goes to `hh.rack`, the oldest first, up to the room (dried fish wanted, less
  that held and that waiting by the rack), in the drying season; the `dry` job hangs it as it hangs a rack catch. Fresh fish moved to the
  rack is booked `drying` out of the fresh store and into the rack (no new reason).
- Fishing for the pot waits while fresh fish held is at least `FISH_WAIT` (2, `sim/pot`) days of need (`Pot.fishCovered`); `rackfish`
  is not held. The fish job's urgency already counted fresh fish (`spareFood`, VB-119); unchanged.
- The seed: `Pot.cookMeal(st, hh, need)` (the cook's done) computes the seed the coming meal would eat as `feedHousehold` would (the
  meals missing less the grain above the reserve, fresh food and the larder; at most the seed held) and the pot takes it after the spare
  grain, with the reason `seedMeal` (each crop as held, as `eatSeed`). `Pot.cookable` posts a cook for it. The once-a-season log of the
  seed eaten moved from `sim/hunger` to `Pot.seedTold`, which both call (`sim/hunger` is over 15 exports with no split task: no export added there).
- Rule 9: `fishCovered` is one comparison when the fish job is asked; `potCatch` one when a catch is counted; `seedForMeal` sums the
  larder's lots (a few) when the cook is asked of a household with no spare grain and no fresh food. No new loop over a collection.
