# VB-332: Stone is used: a house on a stone foundation, and stone picked from stony meadows or quarried in the hills
Status: done (2026-11-11)
Priority: 3
Size: M
Design ref: Land, ecology and rights (Land and ecology: Stones, Stone); Goods, Goods of place and side crafts (A well-kept house). QUESTION_ARCHIVE.md, Stocks audit (11 Nov)
Goal: Stone is gathered and never used. Clearing a field or a meadow puts its stones on the household's heap (`hh.stone`, 25 stones a load; `src/sim/fields.js:72`, `meadows.js:66`), the farm card shows the loads, and nothing reads them: 182 loads lay on 93 households' heaps after 15 years of populous small, seed 7 (median 0, the most 14), though the design says the stones cleared become building material. Kim, 11 Nov: stone is used at least for the prestigious house; a cheap house can be built and later upgraded; stone can be picked from stony meadows or mined in hills and mountains.
Acceptance criteria:
- [x] Two grades of house. The cottage as it is built today (timber, VB-328) is the cheap one, on the bare ground. A household may raise it onto a stone foundation (*stengrund*): long work at the house, `BUILD.foundationStone` (12) loads of stone from its heap and `BUILD.foundationHours` (40) of work, in the slack of early summer or winter, the house lived in meanwhile; offered as upkeep-type work once the stone lies on the heap. `f.grade` ('ground' or 'stone') is state with an owner and an invariant; a save without it loads as 'ground'. Generated farms start on the ground.
- [x] What it brings: the well-kept house's prestige (VB-306) is 0.20 times the condition on stone against 0.15 on the ground, and the house wears 0.08 a year against 0.1; tar's gain (0.03 on the term, a third less wear) adds on either. Nothing else yet: no warmth, no room for more people. The pawn panel names the grade among prestige's parts, and the page draws the stone sill.
- [x] Getting stone, three ways, all into `hh.stone` through a stores operation with a reason: clearing a field or meadow, as today; picking on a stony meadow tile of the household's own land or the village's outland (a job kind `pick`, 2 hours a load, within 12 walking steps, which lowers `w.stones[tile]` as clearing does and is offered while the household wants stone and a tile within reach holds more than a meadow keeps, 80 stones); and quarrying on a hills or mountain tile within reach (a job kind `quarry`, 4 hours a load, within the same reach, never exhausted). A trip carries a load home, as firewood. A household wants stone only while it means to build with it (a plan's step, not a standing target), so heaps do not grow without use.
- [x] The village's well (VB-058) takes 6 loads from its households' heaps; fences, the green and the cottage itself take none. A new cottage is always built on the ground and raised later.
- [x] Cost named (rule 9): the two job kinds are posted for the households with a building step open, a handful at a time; the reach search for a stony or hill tile is made when the step opens and kept on the plan. Numbers before and after (populous small, seeds 7 and 42, 30 years): houses raised onto stone, by year and by household size; stone picked and quarried, hours spent; prestige by household and whether voices at the meeting shift; work hours idle before and after. The golden file is re-recorded and its changed entries listed.
