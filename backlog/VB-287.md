# VB-287: Journeys: a pawn who moves to another village walks there, a leg a day
Status: done (2026-11-13)
Priority: 2
Size: M
Arch: between
Phase: implement
Design ref: Inheritance, expansion and other bodies (Between villages: A journey, Provisions and the season, Who travels). Survey: backlog/VB-281.md
Goal: A pawn who changes household is placed at its new home at once (`moveTo`, `src/sim/heirs.js:150-157`, calls `placeAtHome`), wherever the home lies: a bride married into a village 60 hours away is at her husband's door in the same six-minute step. Kim decided on 10 November that the pawn should physically travel. Make every move between two villages a journey: the pawn walks the route from where it stands to its new home, from 6:00 to 18:00 each day, sleeps where it stops, and eats food carried from the household it leaves. Membership changes as today, at the act; only where the pawn is, and what it does, changes until it arrives.
Acceptance criteria:
- [x] Which moves. `moveTo` (and `foundHousehold`'s moves, `src/sim/membership.js:19-20`) start a journey instead of `placeAtHome` when the pawn's old household lay in another village than its new one (`settlementIdOf` of the two farms), or, for a pawn with no household, when the pawn stands more than 12 walking hours from the new home's green (`hoursBetween` of the nearest village). A move within a village is walked too (Kim, 10 Nov, `QUESTION_ARCHIVE.md`, "Consistency review", pawns placed, question 1): the household changes at the act and the pawn, released from its job but keeping the load it carries, walks home to the new door (`goHome`), with no journey entry; only a pawn already indoors at its old home at night walks at 6:00. The consistency review's probe counted 64 such moves in 40 years of populous small, seed 7 (median 2 tiles, the longest 6.6). Measured by VB-281, a year from `medium-ref` moves 26 pawns between villages (12 by marriage) and from `small-y40` 10 (5 by marriage): the spouse who moves, a servant (`service.js`, `takeServants`), a servant where labour is short (`serveWhereShort`, `service.js:140`, VB-090), kin or a servant who comes to a household short of people (`takeIn`, `service.js:173`, VB-308), a guardian and its family (`careForOrphans`), children who go to kin or service (`heirs.js`, `kinHomeFor`, `serviceHome`), a claimant's household moving into a mended farm (`farmclaims.js`, `moveIn`), and a settlers' party walking to its booth or a small village (`settle.js`, `found`). The counts predate VB-090's and VB-308's movers: the task measures them again.
- [x] The journey: `st.journeys`, the open journeys sorted by due time, each `{id, pawn, to (tile: the new home's house), why ('move'), due}`, with ids from a counter in `st` and never reused. Owner `sim/agents` (`src/owners.json`), `@state` line, saved with the run; a save without it loads with none. A journey starts at once if it is between 6:00 and 16:00, else at the next 6:00 (its first entry's due). Each due entry starts a leg: the agent walks its route (`route`, then `walkTo`) towards `to`, and at 18:00 a pawn still on the road stops where it stands, outdoors, and its next leg is due at 6:00. A route is found once, at the first leg, and kept on the agent (`a.path`, `a.seg`) between legs, so a journey costs one route search. Arrival ends the journey; the pawn is then at home as `placeAtHome` would have put it.
- [x] Nothing polls (Rules for cost, 4): the day's 6:00 looks only at `st.journeys[0].due`, as `dueOffers` reads only the first offer; in VB-241's calendar this is one hourly entry at 6:00, guarded by an open journey. Between legs the pawn is not awake (`awake`, `src/sim/agents.js:242`); while it walks it is, like any walking agent.
- [x] On the road: the pawn takes no job and bids for none, is not sent home at 18:00 (`onHour`'s `goHome` loop skips it as it skips a commanded pawn, and as VB-126 skips a pawn at an occasion, `clock.js:45`, `a.occasion !== null`: the pattern to follow), and is not drawn as company (`companyFor`); it may talk with those it passes, as anyone may. It sleeps where it stops. A pawn who dies on the road dies there (`stillAtDeath`), and its journey is dropped (`killPawn`). A pawn whose household changes again while on the road (a death at home, a handover) has its journey's `to` set to the new home, and if that lies in the village it walks from, it turns back.
- [x] Provisions (design: Provisions and the season). At the start, the household the pawn leaves gives it food for the days on the road, `ceil(h / TRAVEL.dayWalk)` days for h hours of walking (`TRAVEL`, a new tunables block in `sim/rules.js`: `dayWalk` 12, the walking hours of a day, 6:00 to 18:00), one ration a day for a pawn of 14 or more and half below, as a store movement with the reason `journey` (`src/sim/stores.js`), from its stored food above its seed, or, where that falls short, from the new household's. The pawn eats it on the road: while on a journey it is left out of its household's meal share (`src/sim/hunger.js`), and its hunger is fed from what it carries. Settlers keep `SETTLE.provisionDays` of stakes as today, and walk on them. A household with no food to give sends the pawn unprovisioned: it goes hungry on the road, which shows in its hunger log.
- [x] Events (`tests/record.js`): `setOut` when a journey starts, in the pawn's record, naming the farm it leaves and the one it goes to and the hours; `arrived` at the end, kept out of the village log (a routine event, folded with the year's routine events, VB-199); `diedOnRoad` is the `died` event with the place where it fell. The village log keeps today's line for the act (the marriage, the service), which now says "and sets out for", where the home is in another village.
- [x] Rule 7: the walk is the simulation's, whether or not anyone sees it; the page draws a pawn on the road as it draws any walking pawn, and a sleeping one by its stop (no new art: the sleeping pose of the night). A test steps a journey of a bride across two villages with the page closed and checks she is on the route at each 18:00 and at the home at arrival.
- [x] Tests (a new `tests/journeys.js` in `npm test`): a move within a village is placed at once, as today; a move between villages walks, legs at 6:00, stops at 18:00, arrives at the new home in `ceil(h / 12)` days or fewer; the provisions leave the old household's store and the pawn eats nothing from the new household's meals while on the road; a death on the road drops the journey; a save made with a pawn on the road loads and plays as the unbroken run; invariants (`tests/lib/invariants.js`): sorted by due, ids unique and below the counter, each pawn living and in at most one journey, `to` its household's house.
- [x] Behaviour changes, so report before and after (targets with residuals): the golden file re-recorded with the changed entries listed (the movers' positions, jobs and meals); `tests/counters.json` re-recorded, `walkStep` and `routeSearch` listed; over populous small at seeds 7 and 42 for 20 years, `tools/sim.js`'s yearly line and `tools/balance.js` (rations against need), and the hungry-households band (`node tests/bands.js`), with a word on whether the movers' households are worse off. Journeys a year, days on the road and rations carried, at `medium-ref` and `small-y40` (one year, `--load`).
- [x] Cost (Rules for cost, 9): about 26 journeys a year at `medium-ref` and 10 at `small-y40` (before VB-090's and VB-308's movers), each one route search (bounded at `ROUTE.reach`), one due entry a leg (about 3 legs a journey: 80 entries a year), and the agent's walk, about 12 h × 10 steps a leg (under 1,000 walk steps a journey, against millions of `walkStep` a year). Measured: `node tools/sim.js --load` on each fixture `--years 1 --report perf`, before and after.

  `TRAVEL.dayWalk` hours from the new village by `hoursTo`: the nearest village's row of the hours table) it calls `setOut`; within one it
  is released and walks home (`goHome`); indoors at night it stays where it is and walks at 6:00 as anyone does (the matcher), since
  `placeByDoor` at the dark's end now leaves a pawn indoors at another house where it is. A pawn on the road whose household changes
  again is `retarget`ed (its route dropped, found again). Phases: `journey` while a leg is walked, `road` before the first leg and
  between legs (out of `awake`, route kept on `a.path`). Calendar: 6:00 `dueJourneys` (reads `st.journeys[0].due` only), 18:00
  `stopJourneys` (before the go-home loop, which skips a travelling pawn as it skips one at an occasion), and after the households' meals
  `feedTravellers`; all three guarded by an open journey. The `work`, `walkTo`, `goHome`, `idle` and `attend` verbs refuse a pawn on
  the road; occasions' `canGo` and the cook's call pass it over; its household's meal leaves it out (`sim/hunger`, `onRoad`). Arrival:
  `endJourney` in `arrived`, the pawn placed as `placeAtHome` would, the food left over into the new home's meals (reason `journey`).

  journey (`food`; `from` too, the farm left), since a pawn holds only a stake as a store holder; settlers (`carry` false) take theirs
  from the new household, which holds their stakes. A journey whose act falls between 16:00 and 6:00 waits where the pawn stands (indoors
  at its old home, or outdoors). The died event's place is a new record field `tile` (the record's fields are fixed), and its log line
  ends "on the road". `setOut` is notable, `arrived` routine (GENERATION), both on the pawn's record only. The act's log line says ", and
  sets out for it" for the marriage, `enterService` and an orphan's service (`extinct`); the other acts' lines are unchanged. No
  sleeping art exists outdoors: a pawn stopped on the road is drawn idle, as `describe` says "Resting on the road to X". Within the
  route search's reach (200 meadow steps, about 17 hours) the walk follows the route; beyond it, as any walk, it goes straight.

- 10 Nov 2026 (VB-281's plan): written. Why the journey is the agent's own walk with due legs, not a teleport after a delay: Kim's "the pawn should physically travel", and Rules for cost 7 (the camera never changes what the simulation does), so the walk must be the simulation's. Why a due entry a leg and not a walking agent through the night: pawns sleep in the dark (design, Night and sleep) and work hours are 6:00 to 18:00; a leg a day gives "a day's walk" a meaning the design's distances use (Near and far).
- Why membership changes at the act and not at arrival: the round marries as it goes, and each marriage changes the homes and heirs the next man's choice reads (VB-233's survey); moving the marriage to the arrival would hold the round open for days. The pawn belongs to its new household on the road, as a bride did once the wedding was held.
- Prefer what exists: the due list is `st.offers`'s pattern (VB-293); the walk is `walkTo`; provisions are a `stores.js` movement as settlers' provisions are. No new job kind: a journey is not work a household posts and anyone may take, but a move of one pawn; a visit (VB-289) is a journey with a way back.

Independent of VB-277's logic (it changes where a mover stands, not who moves), but both edit the round's day: one at a time with VB-277, VB-278 and VB-288.
