Goal: Make the budget a target the game can be held to. The review of the work since 3 November found three things, and Kim answered on 7 November:
- the late reference is the year populous medium reaches 1,000 living (VB-202 makes it), not year 60;
- the basis of the mean budget, "30× at 10% of a core" (160 ms a game day), was an agent's proposal, not Kim's, and may be changed;
- late in a run, cost grows mostly because there are more people, not because of history.
Re-derive each budget line from what play needs, and state which fixture each is measured on. Write a closure table: for every system, its cost today, its share of the budget, and the task that closes the gap. Then carry the result into `design.md`, `AGENTS.md`, the roadmap and the yardstick's remaining tasks.

- From year 5 to year 60 a game day's cost grows 3.71 times: 2.83 times the people and 1.32 times the cost per living pawn-day. On a log scale, 79% of the growth is people. Most of the rise per pawn comes early: from seeding, populous medium goes from 0.16 ms a living pawn-day in year 1 to 0.23 in year 12 and 0.26 in year 16 (VB-202's notes), about where year 60 stands. VB-195, which takes the dead out of the loops, saved 8% on `small-y40`.
- At the new reference (1,000 living, year 13 today), a game day costs about 230 ms (whole years from seeding, round and meetings included), over today's 160.
- By system at year 60, in ms a game day:
  - agents and walking 223 (34%); no open task covered it, so VB-205 is written for it;
  - talk and contacts 176 (27%), VB-127;
  - daily life and births 132 (20%);
  - jobs 96 (15%);
  - paths and routes 16;
  - hunger and meals 8;
  - every other system under 1.
- The meetings' step was over the step limit with no task to cover it; VB-206 is written for it. medium-y5's longest step (87 ms) is over 60 with no allowance.
- These fixtures predate VB-174, whose ledgers add about half to a save; VB-204 folds them.
