# VB-472: Work as a block: a worker is not stepped
Status: ready
Priority: 1
Size: M
Design ref: docs/review-blocks.md; design.md section 7 (A leg, and its end, to The timetable); docs/mocks/blocks/README.md
Goal: Work costs an event at its start, its end and a change of hands, not a step every 6 minutes (Rules for cost 3, 4).
Acceptance criteria:
- [x] A task's state: `done`, the step it was reckoned at, the summed rate of its hands at work; `doneOf` is the only read of progress outside its owner. Reckoned, and its end queued again, when a hand joins or leaves, when a hand's rate changes (the meal), at the end of work and at a cut.
- [x] The queue of ends is plain data in `st`, sorted by step, bounded by the tasks under work, saved; a loaded run goes on as the unloaded one (`tests/saves.js`).
- [x] The hooks `worked` (a stake renewed, a claim worked, a plot's hours by pawn, grazing by whole hours) run at the reckoning with the hours since, and give the same totals over a day as today.
- [x] A pawn at work stands at its task's spot for talk and nearness; `a.work` (the rows) is no longer simulation state; the page draws the rows from the task and the time (`tests/smoke.js`, the browser tests it touches).
- [x] The invariant of VB-471 holds; new invariants for the task's fields and the queue; the work counters' structure updated (`walkStep` falls).
- [x] Numbers before and after at populous small, seeds 7 and 42, and founders small, seed 7: living, hungry household-days, grain, the hours a household-year by kind, the idle share; `--report perf` and the counters early and on a late save.

## Implementation notes (agent run, 16 Nov 2026)
- `sim/agents`, a section "work as a block": a task holds `done` (at its last reckoning), `at` (the tick reckoned at), `rate` (its hands'
  hours a game hour, `manning`: rateOf, the ox team, the cart in carting) and `due` (the step whose work ends it). `settle` brings `done`
  up to now and runs the kind's `worked` for each hand at work with the hours since (a plot's hours by pawn too); `rerate` reckons the rate
  again and moves the end in `st.workDue`. Called before and after every change of hands: `start`, `arrived` (go to work), `release`,
  `freeHands`, `deposit` of an again-task, `hold`/`unhold`, a task's end (`dueWork`/`ended`, the tail of the old `stepWork`); at the end
  of work and after the meal (`reckonAll`, two calendar entries, so a hand's hunger, health or an ox team come or gone are taken up within
  the day); a change of hands on a task under the ox team reckons the household's other team tasks (`withOxen` moves the team).
- `st.workDue`: the tasks of full attention under work, sorted by due then id, each once (binary search, splice): bounded by the tasks
  under work; plain data (job references, saved with their sharing); absent until the first. `stepAgents` takes what is due before its
  loop (`dueWork`); a pawn at work is no longer in `st.awake`, and the hands a task's end frees are woken.
- The step's work happens as the step's agents begin: `workTick` is the tick while the agents' step has begun, the one before until then,
  so a hand released at the calendar's hour (16:00) does not work that step and one arriving mid-loop works from the next, as stepped.
  A loaded run sets the marker (`loaded`, from `sim/saves`), and reckons the tasks of an older save (its `job.cast`, `agent.work` dropped).
- A background task (herding) is reckoned the same way; its rate (holders minding it) is looked at by its holder's step, which is still
  stepped while it walks (VB-473), and its end is its `due`. Grazing's hook adds a pressure for each whole hour crossed since (`from`).
- The rows: `agent.leg` also marks the start of a work leg ({t, x, y}); the page (`page/animate` `rowPlace`) walks a field's rows from it
  at the drawn time (the row's start is read back from the leg's place), with the facing (`drawFacing`) the ox team and the rig use.
- `doneOf(st, j)`: read by `sim/jobs` (`hoursLeft`, the head's allotment and call), the facade's `plotProgress` (a page view), tests.
  `kinds.isOpen` and the copies of a stage's hours in `sim/expansion` and `sim/farmclaims` load before `sim/agents` and keep reading
  `j.done`, which is now the hours at the last reckoning: exact whenever no hand is at work, and below the task's hours while one is (an
  invariant), which are the cases they test (work left; a stage dropped once its hands are gone). See decisions.
- New counter `workReckon` (tasks reckoned with hours since). `tests/counters.json`: only `walkStep` (its fall) and `workReckon` (new)
  recorded; every other counter is left at the run branch's baseline.

## Decisions (Deciding without Kim)
- A step's ends are whole steps and a task's end is exact: the stepping summed tenths of an hour, and `0.1 × 8` falls short of 0.8, so a
  task of whole tenths took one step more than its hours (water 0.5 h took 0.6). The closed form ends at its hours. A formula that computes
  something other than it says is a fix (principle 3): water fetching fell about a tenth of its hours (below).
- Rate changes the stepping saw at once (a herd gaining or losing its team, a child's age) wait for the next reckoning: a change of hands,
  the end of work, the day's meal. Close call: an hourly reckoning would follow them closer at ten times the events.
- `isOpen` and the two copies read the reckoned `done` rather than `doneOf` (load order: they come before `sim/agents`; moving the closed
  form under them is a restructuring the task does not ask). Close call.
- `tests/head.js`: the template's hay was below three winters only because the fourth meadow's mowing was left 0.9 h short at nightfall
  by the tenths and taken down; ended at its hours it is mown. The assertion now holds the template at two winters or more and no more
  than the head's choice. Close call.
- A hand at work on a task taken out of `st.jobs` without its hands freed (only tests do this) ends it as the stepping did.
- The pawn drawn on its rows jumps back to the row's start when its work ends (the simulation's place); the walk home from the rows is
  VB-474's (the page draws from legs).
