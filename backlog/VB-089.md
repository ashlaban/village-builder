# VB-089: Landless adults take service where labour is short
Status: needs-review
Priority: 2
Size: M
Arch: expansion
Phase: implement
Design ref: Inheritance and expansion, Leaving home (Service: a landless adult may take service where labour is short: Agreed); People (Household and family are separate: servants live in households not their own: Agreed); Roles (farmhand, *dräng*; maid, *piga*).
Goal: Only the founders' farmhand and maid, and members of a farm that dies out (VB-065), are servants today. A landless adult waits in `st.demand` at home however many adults the household already has, while a couple on a three-field farm next door works alone. Life-cycle service was the normal path for young adults in the Northwestern European pattern (Hajnal 1965); it moves labour to where the fields are and widens whom a young adult meets.
Acceptance criteria (draft, pending `QUESTIONS.md`, VB-089):
- [ ] At the moving round, a household is short of labour when its working adults (16 to 59, not retired) are fewer than its fields, or fewer than 2, and its grain above the seed reserve (VB-049) covers a servant's food until the next harvest. At most 2 servants per household.
- [ ] A household has labour to spare when its working adults outnumber its fields. Its unmarried adults of 18 or more in `st.demand` may go into service, oldest first: to a short household of their own village first, then of the nearest village by `hexSteps` between centres.
- [ ] A servant moves (`moveTo`) with a "moved" life event naming the farm, takes the role farmhand or maid, and keeps their place in `st.demand` and in every heir ranking; service ends when they marry into a place or get a farm or plot.
- [ ] No wages until money exists: a servant eats with the household and works its jobs as a member (as today's farmhand and maid).
- [ ] The settlers list and the household panel show servants with the farm they came from.
- [ ] A test on a constructed village: a couple on a three-field farm takes a servant from a household with three grown children; a farm without grain for a servant does not.
- [ ] Founders over 50 years and populous medium over 5 years at seeds 7 and 42 report servants per year and hungry household-days, before and after (reported, not asserted). Re-record the golden file and list the changed entries in the PR.
Notes / questions:
- VB-065 sends members of a dying farm into service in their village; both should use one helper (`takeService(st, pawnId, hh)`), whichever is done first.
- Food matters: without VB-080 a servant can tip a farm into hunger; the grain gate in the first criterion guards against it.
