  - The cap of 32 never binds (at most 12 counterparts); a forgotten balance is rare within a year (under 2% of the year's first
    balances), since a typical one, 12 to 23 rations at a 4-year half-life, takes 14 to 18 years to fall under a ration. Decay takes
    17 to 19% of the stock a year (16% of a constant stock, plus the year's bookings); handovers pass 1 to 4% and deaths end 1 to 5%.
    The rest of the difference (70 to 260 rations a year) is netting: gifts both ways between the same holders, and a balance with
    the heir halved at a handover (question 5).
  - By kinship, balances "through a spouse" are the largest group (35 to 51% of balances, 55 to 67% of the year's bookings): help asks
    the households of every member's parents and children first, and a young household's hungry member is often the wife, whose
    father's holder then gives to her husband. Parents and children hold 11 to 23% of balances, a little larger than siblings' (median
    17 to 26 rations against 12 to 23), as asking parents and children first predicts.
  - For VB-220: `limitDays` of 30 days of the debtor's need lies near one full ask (12 to 36 days) and above 90 to 96% of balances, so

- Design against code, The gift (`design.md`, section 9), row by row, and the rows of section 9 and of Help in hunger that the slice built:
  - Lumps and occasions: help asks at most once a season (`household.askedIn`), for enough to reach the harvest (`askDaysAt`); amounts are
    not yet whole quanta, which the row leaves to slice 4. Matches.
  - Goodwill is one decaying number: `pawn.goodwill`, `{v, t}` mirrored on both pawns, read as `v * 0.5 ** (age / halfLife)`. Matches.
  - The gift: help's gifts go through the verb `give` and `sim/exchange`, the grain through `sim/stores`, and the worth (grain 1 a ration)
    is booked to the giver's credit. A household without a holder gives too (actor `{household}`, keeping 60 days), which Who books
    implies. Matches.
  - Acceptance: `assessGift` needs the floor kept and `n` within the allowance (to 1e-9); a village store has neither, and gives what it
    holds, which the design does not say but nothing contradicts. Matches. With goodwill's term off, help proposes exactly the allowance,
    so no gift of help is refused today.
  - The floor: `floorOf`, the seed reserve and `HELP.floorDays` (20) days of need. Matches.
  - Allowance: `slack` (grain above the floor, at least 0), `reserve` (minus the holder's buffer beyond 20 days, 0 to 40 days of need),
    `goodwill` (0 while `GOODWILL.acts` is false; written as the row says, against 30 days of the receiving household's need), clamped at 0.
    Matches.
  - Unit and decay: rations, a 4-year half-life, forgotten under a ration and dropped when either pawn next books, at the cap (the
    smallest goes) or at a death; 32 counterparts. Matches. A share passed at a handover from a balance of 1 to 2 rations is born under a
    ration and reads as forgotten at once, which the row's rule covers.
  - Goodwill and opinion: not switched on, as the row says (VB-220).
  - Who books: the two holders; the store, its filling and a household without a holder book nothing; half passes at a retirement or a
    holder's death (and a widow's succession), the retired holder keeps the rest, other deaths end a pawn's balances. Matches, but for
    one case the design does not name: the half of a balance with the heir himself is cancelled (question 5).
  - What the player sees: the panel lists up to 5 balances by size, in whole rations, with "would give up to" and the allowance's parts
    on hover on a holder's panel (VB-218). Matches.
  - The player's gift: source `player` passes the floor and the allowance in `assessGift`; the page's side is VB-221. Matches.
  - Section 9's Goodwill at retirement says the share is "set by custom"; the code and The gift pass a fixed half (question 4). Help in
    hunger (section 8) says "each adult keeps a buffer"; the code reads only the holder's, as The gift's Allowance says (question 6).
