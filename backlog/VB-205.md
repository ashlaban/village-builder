# VB-205: Agents and walking: name the largest cost, then cut it
Status: ready
Priority: 2
Size: M
Arch: yardstick
Phase: implement
Design ref: none (code structure). `docs/roadmap.md`, section 5.3 (rules 4, 5 and 6).
Goal: The largest line of the budget has no task. On 9 November, `tools/bench.js` reported "agents and walking (the rest of Sim.advance)" at 70 ms a game day at `medium-ref` (a whole game year, the mean of three runs, 68.5 to 71.9; 30% of the day), against its share of 60 ms (VB-203, `docs/bench/closure.md`); 57 ms at `medium-y5`. Spring and winter cost most (85 and 73 ms a day in one run). It is not one system: it is what is left of `Sim.advance` once the named systems are timed. It holds the 6-minute agent step (every living agent, every step: the `walkStep` counter) and whatever else is not wrapped. Name its parts, then cut the ones over the share VB-203 gives them.
Acceptance criteria:
- [ ] Measure first, on `medium-ref` and `small-y40`, a whole game year each. A CPU profile (`node --cpu-prof`) splits the remainder into named parts. Each part over 5% of a game day gets a system name in `tools/perf.js`, so the bench reports it from then on, and the unnamed rest falls below 10% of a day.
- [ ] Work counters for the per-step loops the profile finds (beside `walkStep`), asserted at tiny scale in `tests/counters.json`.
- [ ] Agents and walking at most 60 ms a game day at `medium-ref` (`tools/bench.js`, whole year): for each part over it, a change by the rules for cost. For example: an agent with nothing to do until a known time is not stepped until then (rule 4); a per-tile or per-agent value read every step is compiled (rule 6); work that can wait is spread over steps (rule 5). An order-preserving change leaves the golden file unchanged. A change of behaviour re-records it and reports `tools/sim.js` before and after, at seeds 7 and 42.
- [ ] ms a game day before and after on both fixtures (paired runs, as VB-195 measured), and the counters.
- [ ] `npm run verify` passes.
