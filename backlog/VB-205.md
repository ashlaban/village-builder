# VB-205: Agents and walking: name the largest cost, then cut it
Status: done (2026-11-09)
Priority: 2
Size: M
Arch: yardstick
Phase: implement
Design ref: none (code structure). `docs/roadmap.md`, section 5.3 (rules 4, 5 and 6).
Goal: The largest line of the budget has no task. On 9 November, `tools/bench.js` reported "agents and walking (the rest of Sim.advance)" at 70 ms a game day at `medium-ref` (a whole game year, the mean of three runs, 68.5 to 71.9; 30% of the day), against its share of 60 ms (VB-203, `docs/bench/closure.md`); 57 ms at `medium-y5`. Spring and winter cost most (85 and 73 ms a day in one run). It is not one system: it is what is left of `Sim.advance` once the named systems are timed. It holds the 6-minute agent step (every living agent, every step: the `walkStep` counter) and whatever else is not wrapped. Name its parts, then cut the ones over the share VB-203 gives them.
Acceptance criteria:
- [x] Measure first, on `medium-ref` and `small-y40`, a whole game year each. A CPU profile (`node --cpu-prof`) splits the remainder into named parts. Each part over 5% of a game day gets a system name in `tools/perf.js`, so the bench reports it from then on, and the unnamed rest falls below 10% of a day.
- [x] Work counters for the per-step loops the profile finds (beside `walkStep`), asserted at tiny scale in `tests/counters.json`.
- [x] Agents and walking at most 60 ms a game day at `medium-ref` (`tools/bench.js`, whole year): for each part over it, a change by the rules for cost. For example: an agent with nothing to do until a known time is not stepped until then (rule 4); a per-tile or per-agent value read every step is compiled (rule 6); work that can wait is spread over steps (rule 5). An order-preserving change leaves the golden file unchanged. A change of behaviour re-records it and reports `tools/sim.js` before and after, at seeds 7 and 42.
- [x] ms a game day before and after on both fixtures (paired runs, as VB-195 measured), and the counters.
- [x] `npm run verify` passes.

- 9 Nov 2026, implementation:
  - Rule 4: `sim/agents` owns the six-minute step (`stepAgents`, moved from `sim/clock`) and a cache, `st.awake` (non-enumerable like
    `st.idx`, so never saved; `src/owners.json`): the ids, in order, of the agents that may act this hour, rebuilt at the hour's first step
    after `onHour`: those walking or working, and in working hours those with no task whose pause ends by the hour's end. Between rebuilds only
    `walkTo` and `idle` can make an agent act (VB-177 left every movement write in `sim/agents`), and both wake it; `stillAtDeath` takes it
    out. An agent left out is one the old loop would have passed over, so the order of acts is the same. About half the living are awake.
    The loop no longer reads the pawn record (no living pawn is ever dead there: deaths take the id out of `st.living` at once).
  - Rule 6: `withOxen` asks `herds.hasTeam` (count the oxen, allocate nothing) instead of building the team; `moveAgent` indexes a path point
    instead of destructuring it.
  - `fadeRelations` copies the memories left to a new object instead of deleting keys, which left the record a dictionary that `opinionOf`
    walks with `for...in`.
  - `sim/saves` `decode` makes each object with all its keys at once (`{...d}`, or `Object.fromEntries` for a shared or escaped one) and then
    fills in its values: loaded objects stay fast. Keys keep their order.
  - `tools/perf.js` names `agents: idle strolls` (`idleAbout`) and `agents: walking and working` (`stepAgents`, timed once a step);
    `tools/bench.js` splits the 60 ms share: 33, 23, and 4 for the rest of `Sim.advance`.
  - Counters (`VB.COUNTERS`, `tests/counters.json`): `walkAwake` (agents walked by the hourly rebuild), `idleStroll`, `companyWeighed` (idle
    pawns drawn as company), `walkPoint` (path points walked past); `walkStep` now counts the awake agents. Invariant: `st.awake` holds
    increasing living ids and, for its hour, every agent that may act (`checkState`).
