# VB-850: Nothing vanishes: a gift's grain on the back of a carrier or fetcher who dies on the road is laid down where he fell
Status: ready
Priority: 2
Size: S
Arch: physical
Phase: implement
Design ref: AGENTS.md, Deciding without Kim (the hard rule: nothing vanishes but by a process); backlog/VB-461.md; src/sim/lying.js
Goal: sim/agents dropJourney (stillAtDeath) ends the journey of a pawn who dies on the road; the grain of a gift on his back (j.gift.n of a carry, j.gift.held of a fetch) vanishes with it. sim/lying already lays down a hand's load where its carrier fell, for its owner to fetch.
Acceptance criteria:
- [x] A carrier or fetcher who dies on the road with a gift's grain on his back puts it down where he fell, as a load lying (`sim/lying` lay), owned by the receiving household (or, for a carrier who has not delivered, the receiver: its owner since the ask), and fetched by its owner's next hand or lost to the autumn frost as any lying load.
- [x] The grain still set aside at a fetch's stops goes back to its giver's store, as now (VB-461 unfetched).
- [x] `tests/fetch.js` and `tests/journeys.js`: a death on the way home with a load leaves a lying load of that size.
- [x] Numbers: see Notes below.

Noted by VB-461, 16 November 2026.

Implementation (17 Nov 2026):
- `sim/agents` `dropJourney` calls `layGift` for a fetcher or hauler with `gift.held > 0` and for a carrier who has not delivered
  (`gift.n`): `Lying.leave(st, 'leftGrain', n, tile, owner, {x, y, of: {parts}})` where he fell (`placeOf`). `sim/lying` `leave` takes an
  optional position and `of`; its frost log names 'grain'. The kind `leftGrain` (`sim/building`, beside `leftTimber`, as it is never posted,
  only fetched) has a fetch kind made by `sim/lying`; its delivery puts the load, by crop, in the store of the hand that fetches it (the
  village's when the owner is a village). What lies at a fetch's stops still goes back (`unfetched`).
- Owner: the receiving household of a carry or fetch (its village when it is gone); a haul (a quarter on its way to the green) is
  still its giver's until it is in the store, so the giver's household owns the load (its village when it is gone).
- Tests: `tests/fetch.js` (death on the way home with a load: a lying load of that size, the receiver's, by crop; the rest at the stop
  back to the giver; then fetched home), `tests/journeys.js` (a carrier's death on the road).
- Numbers: populous small seeds 3, 7, 11, 42, 30 years: 0 loads of grain laid down on a death in each (deaths on short walks are rare; the tests build the case).

Decisions: a haul's load is its giver's, not the village's (the village has no hand of its own to fetch grain home to a household store).
