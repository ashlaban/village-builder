# VB-094: Forest spreads into open land unless grazing or work keeps it open
Status: needs-review
Priority: 3
Size: M
Arch: animals
Phase: implement
Design ref: Land and ecology (Forest spread: Decided; Grazing: pressure feeds forest spread: Agreed; Making a hay meadow: a meadow left unmown and ungrazed falls under the forest-spread rule); Systems overview (Forest, wood and stones).
Goal: Trees regrow on wooded tiles (VB-005) and felled tiles become meadow, but open land never turns back to forest; only empty farms' fields go to scrub, by a fixed rule (VB-067). With grazing pressure (the animals arch), the design's loop closes: land kept open by herds, mowing and farming, and lost to woodland where it is not. Depends on the grazing-pressure task that VB-047 writes and on VB-067; if either is not done, mark this blocked.
Acceptance criteria (draft, pending `QUESTIONS.md`, VB-094):
- [ ] Once a game year, at the new year: a meadow, heath or fallow tile next to forest (1 step) that was not worked, mown or grazed above a threshold that year counts one more idle year; worked, mown or grazed, its count is reset.
- [ ] After the answered number of idle years (proposed 8) the tile becomes `scrub`; after the answered number more (proposed 8) it becomes woodland of the neighbouring forest type with 10% of a full tile's trees, and then grows by the tree model of VB-005 (about 30 years to full).
- [ ] Spread advances ring by ring, since each new wooded tile makes its neighbours eligible. Fields of an occupied farm, the green, house tiles and claimed tiles being worked never spread.
- [ ] Clearing scrub takes the scrub hours of VB-033 (20 h); clearing young woodland is felling (VB-031).
- [ ] The VB-067 rule for empty farms becomes a case of this one.
- [ ] Cost: the yearly pass over all land tiles takes under 20 ms on a Large map (reported).
- [ ] Founders and populous runs of 50 years report open land, scrub and woodland per decade, with and without herds (reported). Re-record the golden file and list the changed entries in the PR.
