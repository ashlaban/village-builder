# VB-1615: Independent review: a fresh reviewer checks a task's diff against its task and design before the PR, against a held-out control
Status: ready
Priority: 2
Size: L
Design ref: none (agent workflow). AGENTS.md, Taking a task and Surfacing; .claude/agents/vb-task.md; docs/review-agent-workflow.md, sections 6 and 8; docs/agents/README.md; tools/brief.js, tools/same.js, tools/agent-usage.js, tools/usage-page.js; backlog/VB-254.md, VB-255.md, VB-485.md (the reviews of 16 November)
Goal: An implementing agent's code and tests can agree with each other and still rest on one misreading of the task or the design. The hard checks cannot catch that, because the tests were written from the same reading. Before a single-task session opens its PR, an eligible task gets one review by a `vb-review` sub-agent with a fresh context. The reviewer never sees the implementer's conversation or reasoning. It reads the task as it stood before the work, the design sections that are its authority, the architecture's rules, the diff and the evidence that tools have already computed. It reports where the change is wrong, in three grades: it finds semantic defects; it does not repeat checks and does not rewrite. The cycle is bounded at one review and one revision. Whether review pays is measured against a held-out control: a third of eligible tasks, chosen by a hash of the ID, get no review. The run files record each task's arm, the review's cost and what it found, and escaped defects are traced through a new optional `Cause:` header.
Acceptance criteria:
- [ ] **The reviewer**, `.claude/agents/vb-review.md` (tools: Bash, Read, Grep, Glob; no Edit or Write). Its one responsibility is to find where the change is wrong against its authorities, not to fix or rewrite it. Its prompt is fixed, `Review VB-NNN; base <sha>; head <sha>`, and carries nothing from the implementer; it is started as a fresh sub-agent, never as a fork of the session. It reads `node tools/review.js packet ID` first and writes its draft findings to `work/review/VB-NNN.json`. Only after that does it read `node tools/review.js claims ID` (the implementer's ticks and notes) and check each claim against the code. It does not read the implementer's commit messages or transcript, does not rerun what the packet reports (`npm test`, verify, lint, ownership, counters, `same`), and edits nothing in the repository. It may write probes under `work/review/` and run them, and run single named test scripts. It looks for, in this order: criteria unmet, or met only in letter; the change against a Decided or Agreed point of the design sections its refs name; behaviour the task did not ask for (on other households or systems, on saves, on cost); counterexamples the tests miss (boundaries, the dead and the dissolved, the empty, the first and last day of a season, a loaded save); tests that would pass without the change, or that assert the implementation rather than the contract; complexity or coupling the task did not need (a new module, owner, export, read between modules or special case), judged against target-v2 section 2 and `AGENTS.md`, Conventions. Ceiling: 40 model calls and 10 findings. `tests/context-size.js` counts its definition as a target of its own, at most 6,000 B.
- [ ] **Grades**, written into the reviewer and checked by `review.js` when it reads the findings file. A `blocking` finding needs both an authority, quoted with its place (a criterion of the task; a section of `design.md` with its status, Decided or Agreed; a rule of `AGENTS.md`, `BACKLOG.md` or target-v2 section 2), and a concrete failing scenario (a constructed state or input, and the wrong outcome). The scenario is demonstrated by a probe (`tests/tiny.js`, `tests/lib/probe.js`) where one can be built in a few calls. A finding that rests on a Proposed or "Under revision" point, on no authority, or on a scenario not shown is `advisory` at most. A matter of taste is a `preference`: at most three, never acted on, counted only. Each finding carries an id, grade, category (one of the six above), authority, place (`file:line`), scenario, and the check that would catch it. Why: the review of VB-254's diff on 16 November led to VB-485, whose first criterion was withdrawn as "built on a misdiagnosis" of a Decided point; the review of VB-255 found a real blocker (a plot open for ever). Requiring a blocking finding to quote its authority and show its scenario guards against the first case without losing the second.
- [ ] **The packet**, `node tools/review.js packet ID [--base REF] [--head REF]`, in one call as `brief.js` is (the base defaults to `git merge-base HEAD origin/main`, the head to `HEAD`; a `tools/help.js` header). It prints: (a) the brief of the task as it stood at the base: `tools/brief.js` on the base's task file, with goal, criteria, open questions, the design sections of its refs with their status markers, and the rules for its kind, and none of the implementer's ticks or notes; (b) for a plan task, the diff of `design.md` and the brief of each task it creates or changes; (c) the diff from base to head, with its stat, leaving out derived files and the task's own file, cut at 60 KB (beyond that, the stat, with `git diff BASE HEAD -- FILE` named for the rest); (d) evidence computed by tools rather than asked of the reviewer:
  - `npm test` at the head;
  - each test script the diff adds or changes, run with the head's tests against the base's `src/` in a temporary worktree (as `tools/same.js` makes one), reported as "fails on base" or "passes on base: does not detect the change" (for a task that is not a refactor);
  - for a refactor, the line of `node tools/same.js --base BASE`;
  - the work counters changed (`tests/counters.js`);
  - what changed in structure: modules added (`src/manifest.json`), `@state` lines added or changed, `src/owners.json` entries, verbs registered, job kinds, counters, exports past `tests/width-baseline.json`, and reads `VB['…']` of one module by another that the base did not have (new edges in `tools/dependencies.js`);
  - the criteria ticked and unticked at the head.
  `review.js claims ID` prints the task file's changes since the base, and refuses until the draft findings file exists. Tests on a constructed repository with two commits: a test that passes on the base is flagged, and `claims` refuses before the draft.
- [ ] **When to review.** `node tools/review.js eligible ID` decides from the task file and the diff alone, and prints the arm (`review`, `control` or `ineligible`) with the rule that decided it. A task is eligible when any of these holds:
  - its phase is plan;
  - its size is M or L;
  - the diff adds a module, an `@state` field, an owner entry, a verb, a job kind, a work counter, or a read between modules;
  - it changes behaviour (not a refactor showing `same`) in three or more modules of `src/`;
  - the task file gains a `Flag:` line.
  Otherwise the task is ineligible: an S task in one module with no new structure, a change to tests, tools or documents alone, or a refactor showing `same`. An eligible task is `control` when a fixed hash of its ID (FNV-1a of `VB-NNN`, mod 3) is 0. Nobody chooses it; it is printed with the arm, and it holds until the evaluation task records Kim's decision. The PR reports the shares the rule makes eligible and control over the tasks done from 17 to 24 November, each reconstructed from the commits that name it, as `tools/usage-page.js` attributes them (140 of those 313 were M or L, so about half eligible is expected).
- [ ] **The cycle, bounded**, in `AGENTS.md`, Taking a task, as a step between 4 and 5 of at most 600 bytes; the rest is in the reviewer's definition and `review.js --help`. After the last verify, the session commits and runs `review.js eligible`. For `review`, it starts the `vb-review` sub-agent with the fixed prompt: Sonnet for an S task, Opus for M and L, and the strongest model offered for a plan. If the verdict is `revise` (one or more blocking findings), the session makes one revision:
  - each blocking finding is mended with a test that fails at the reviewed commit and passes at the head, or rebutted in the findings file with its own authority or a probe's output;
  - each advisory finding is mended or declined in a line;
  - preferences are not acted on.
  The revision does only that, in at most 30 model calls. There is no second review. `node tools/review.js confirm ID --reviewed SHA` checks each blocking finding deterministically: the test named fails at SHA and passes at the head, or the rebuttal cites an authority. For each finding left unresolved it opens a priority 1 task with `Cause: VB-NNN`. The task is merged and stays done: hard checks alone decide a merge, and review does not use `needs-review` (decided 24 November, when this task was written). A task gets one review, also when it runs over several sessions. The PR gets a "Review" section: the arm and its rule, the verdict, and the findings by grade with their outcomes. Unresolved findings go under "For Kim" (`AGENTS.md`, Surfacing, with no orchestrator).
- [ ] **Measured with the existing instruments.** `tools/agent-usage.js` tells a reviewer sub-agent from an implementer (its prompt begins `Review VB-NNN`, or by its agent type) and keeps a `role` for each. Reviewers are left out of `firstPass`, `tasks` and the implementing cost by size: today a reviewer would be joined to the implementer's result by task and counted as a second attempt. `--review work/review/VB-NNN.json` keeps a `review` record in the run file: the arm and its rule, the verdict, the findings by grade and category with each one's outcome, the reviewer's calls and weighted cost, and the revision's weighted cost and minutes (the session's, after the reviewer returned). `--arm control|ineligible` does the same for a session with no review, so every measured session states its arm. `tools/usage-page.js` shows a task's review and revision apart from its implementation. Tests on constructed transcripts, in `tests/agent-usage.js` and `tests/usage-page.js`.
- [ ] **Escapes traced.** An optional `Cause: VB-NNN` header line, after `Status:` and any `Waits for:`, names the task whose change introduced the defect a task mends, where that is known. `tests/backlog.js` checks that it names an existing task; `tools/backlog.js new|set cause=` and `tools/inbox.js note ID task cause=` write it; `BACKLOG.md`, How this backlog works, says so in one sentence. Older tasks are not back-filled.
- [ ] **The evaluation, as a tool and a task.** `node tools/review.js eval [--dir docs/agents/usage] [--days 14]` prints, per arm (review and control, with ineligible beside them for scale):
  - eligible tasks done;
  - escapes: tasks whose `Cause:` names one of them, created (the first commit of their file) within 14 days after its done date, and not opened by its own review's `confirm`;
  - escapes per task, with an exact 90% interval of the ratio between arms;
  - tasks merged with a known unresolved finding;
  - the median weighted cost of a task, with and without review and revision, and its median minutes.
  For the reviewed arm it also prints:
  - findings by grade;
  - confirmed catches: blocking findings mended with a test that fails at the reviewed commit;
  - blocking findings rebutted (false blockers);
  - the cost per confirmed catch;
  - the break-even: confirmed catches × the median weighted cost of a task with a `Cause:` line, against the total cost of reviews and revisions.
  A test on constructed run files and task files. This task also opens the evaluation task (`blocked`, `Waits for: Kim`, its criteria the method below), so that the method is fixed before the data come in.

**Evaluation method** (fixed now; the evaluation task copies it):
- Units: eligible tasks done in single-task sessions from this task's merge on, by arm. Window: until 60 control tasks are done (about ten days at the rate of 17 to 24 November, when about 17 tasks a day were M or L), then 14 days of follow-up for escapes. The eligibility rule does not change during the window.
- Primary outcome: escapes per task, reviewed against control (the rate ratio and its 90% interval). Co-primary, for the decision: the break-even. Secondary: cost and minutes per task by arm; the share done at the first attempt; the false-blocker share; catches by kind (plan, M or L behaviour, structural S), so that eligibility can be narrowed where catches concentrate.
- Power, stated now: with about 60 control and 120 reviewed tasks, halving an escape rate of 15% is detected at α 0.05 one-sided with a power of about 0.4. The escape comparison therefore guards against harm and against claims of large effects. The break-even, which does not wait for rare escapes, carries most of the decision.
- Threats: whoever finds a defect writes its `Cause:` line without seeing the arm, which is in the run file and the PR, not in the task file. An implementer who knows a review is coming may work differently; the cost comparison by arm shows whether that happens.
- Proposed decisions for Kim: keep review for all eligible tasks if the break-even holds and the escape ratio's upper bound is at most 1.25; narrow it to the kinds where catches concentrate if it holds only there; stop it if more than half of blocking findings are false blockers, or if the break-even fails in every kind.
