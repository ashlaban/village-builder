# VB-407: Plan: outlets for the surplus: feasts that book goodwill, and keeping
Status: needs-review
Priority: 2
Size: S
Design ref: Balance loops and targets (The balance review, 12 November); docs/balance/cards.md; docs/balance/findings.md; The gift (slice 1); Goods and the farming year
Goal: A farm's surplus can be eaten, taken (rent, tithe, tax), feasted, sold, or left to rot (`docs/balance/cards.md`, card 4). Today only eating, a little brewing and rot exist, and about a fifth of the grain harvest rots. Kim agreed on 12 November that feasting and good-year gifts should be a driver of the goodwill economy (generosity as a strategy, not only an answer to hunger), that coin opens trade with strangers and does not replace goodwill, and suggested longer keeping as a late-game way to raise the effective yield. Design these outlets with numbers, then write the tasks. Rent, tithe and tax are VB-404's and slice 8's; sale is slice 4's (the fair).
Acceptance criteria:
- [ ] Kim's answers to `QUESTIONS.md`, "VB-407: feasts and keeping", are recorded and archived.
- [ ] `design.md`, Exchange, goodwill and obligations (The gift) gains the feast and the good-year gift: when a household holds one (its stores above a threshold, an occasion: a wedding, a funeral ale, the harvest home), whom it asks (by tie and kinship, a cap), what it costs (grain, ale, an animal), what each guest then owes (goodwill booked by the gift's rule, in customary rations) and what the host gains in standing (ale's rule, VB-306, extended or replaced). The loop is stated with its brake: goodwill halves in 4 years.
- [ ] The surplus split is written as a property with its measure (VB-403's report) and what each outlet feeds: feasted to goodwill and standing, sold to the town, taken to the elite, rot to nothing.
- [ ] Keeping: what lengthens a food's life and when it arrives (a granary as a facility of slice 6 with lower `ECON.storeLoss`; drying, salting and smoking, of which dried fish is built), with the side effect the review named: less rot means larger stores and more to lend.
- [ ] Animals as stored wealth are placed: whether a herd counts as an outlet of the surplus (fodder into a cow), and where that is designed (VB-141, or a card of the balance map).
- [ ] Implement tasks are written with numbers and criteria, each naming its cost (rule 9: a feast is an occasion, a few a village a year) and the golden file's change.
- [ ] Checked against the mock: feasting mostly replaced rot (with feasting off, rot took 41% of output at a food ratio of 1.67) and slightly sped land concentration (18 estates against 15); the plan says whether that is acceptable.
