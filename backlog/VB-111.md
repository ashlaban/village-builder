# VB-111: Rivers in the generated world, and villages that seek them
Status: done (2026-10-30)
Priority: 2
Size: L
Design ref: World and scale (Rivers: Decided; River courses: Proposed); Settlements and look (Generated villages: fitted to the land, villages follow rivers); Long work and processing (water mills need rivers). Questions answered 30 Oct 2026 when this task was set (`QUESTION_ARCHIVE.md`, "VB-111: rivers").
Goal: The world has no rivers, so nothing in the design that needs them (water mills, villages that follow rivers, wet banks) can be built. Generate rivers with the world, draw them on the map, and let villages prefer river land, whose fields are watered from the river.
Acceptance criteria:
- [x] A module `src/gen/rivers.js` (`@sys gen/rivers`, listed before `gen.js`, which calls it) traces rivers from the elevation `w.he`: every tile drains to a neighbour on a path to the sea (priority flood from the sea, Barnes, Lehman and Mulla 2014); a lake fills to its lowest shore and drains on from there, so a river that enters a lake leaves it and runs on to the sea.
- [x] Rivers rise in hills, mountains and marshes, at least 3 steps from water. Each island gets `round(land tiles / 700)` sources (none on islands under 350 tiles); sources lie at least 10 steps apart, and a new river runs at least 3 land tiles before it meets water or another river. Long courses go first (a course of 15 land tiles or more counts as long), with a random term of up to 10 so sources spread over the island instead of crowding its longest drainage. Rivers merge where they meet.
- [x] `w.river` (Uint16Array per tile): the catchment of a land tile a river runs through, in tiles weighted by moisture (0.5 + `hm`), 0 elsewhere; `w.riverTo` (Int32Array per tile): the next tile down the course (a river tile, a lake on the course or the sea), -1 off every course. Both have `@state` tags. The rivers use their own random stream, so every other field of `w` is unchanged.
- [x] The map draws each river as a smooth curve through the centres of its tiles, from a narrow brook at its source to most of a tile wide (width 2.5 + 0.9 × √catchment map units, at most 0.75 of a hex); on every level of detail, the overview included. Trees, shrubs, stones and flowers stand clear of the water. The tile card names a brook, stream or river.
- [x] No house, field or green on a river tile (`farmable` is false there; new fields and plots avoid them).
- [x] Irrigation: a field next to a river tile has 20% more fertility (`RIVER.irrigation` in `src/sim/rules.js`), wherever fertility is used: placing and choosing fields and the field's `fert`.
- [x] Village sites prefer rivers: `siteScore` adds `RIVER.site` by steps to the nearest river tile (10 at 1 or 2 steps, 5 at 3), so village centres and lone farms move to river land. Report the share of village centres within 3 steps of a river before and after (populous, medium, seeds 7 and 42).
- [x] A test (`tests/rivers.js`, in `npm test`): rivers are deterministic; every course ends in the sea; catchment never falls downstream; sources are hills, mountains or marsh; no house or field on a river tile; fields next to a river carry the bonus.
- [x] Re-record the golden file and list the changed entries in the PR; the jsdom smoke test passes.
Notes / questions:
- Walking ignores rivers for now: wading anywhere or only at fords is Open in the design; fords and bridges are a later task.
- Not in this task: water mills (VB-093 builds a windmill first), wet banks for hay (the animals arch), fetching water from the river, farm names from rivers.
- Done 2026-10-30. Rivers (medium seed 7 / 42): 26 / 29 sources, 19 / 18 mouths, 649 / 509 river tiles (3.2% / 2.5% of land); large: 49 / 52 sources. Catchment runs from 2 at a source to 1,656 (seed 7). World generation takes 6% longer (medium 914 to 973 ms, large 1,375 to 1,446 ms).
- Village centres within 3 steps of a river, populous medium (meadow sites within 3 steps of one: 23% at seed 7, 12% at seed 42): seed 7 from 40% to 68%, seed 42 from 14% to 38%; populous small 43% to 75% and 20% to 22%. Founders and heirs, seeds 7 and 42: 1 of 8 villages before, 7 of 8 after. Before, 44 (seed 7) and 7 (seed 42) houses and fields of the populous scenario stood on river tiles; now none. A site bonus of 6 (first proposed) gave 50% and 28%; 15 gave 79% and 43%.
- `gen.js` stays at its lint baseline of 672 lines: the trace is one line, the thinning call replaces the sort of the decoration items, and `items` joined the declaration above it. The river drawing is called from the page's `chunkJob`, between the bake and the decorations.
- The look (asked for after the first version, which read like pipes): each river is drawn as lines sampled through its tiles and bent by seeded noise at two scales (meanders, weaker on wide rivers); width runs smoothly from a thread at a small spring pool to 1.6 times the river where it opens into a lake or the sea; each layer (damp ground, a rough mud edge, light shallows, a darker channel and its deepest part) is one fill of all outlines, so overlaps do not build up; the paint takes only on land already in the chunk, so mouths end at the painted coastline. At close zoom, glints run with the current of streams and rivers, and reeds grow on lowland banks. Trees whose crowns would hide the water are left out.
- `tests/populous.js` checked the mean household size of each map against fixed bounds of 5.5 to 6.6. On a Tiny map of about 20 households that is only 1.3 standard errors above the drawn mean of 6.17 (sd 1.48), so about one run in ten failed by chance; with rivers, Tiny seed 7 drew 19 households of mean 6.84 and failed. Over 28 maps (7 seeds, 4 sizes) the mean is 6.18 before and 6.22 after. The check now asks for the mean within 3 standard errors of the drawn mean: about ±1.0 on Tiny maps, ±0.4 on Medium ones (5.79 to 6.57), close to the old bounds there.
- Golden entries changed: `world/7` and `world/42` gain `river` and `riverTo` (no other world key changes); every `founders` and `heirs` entry at seeds 7 and 42; `apiKeys/Sim` (`RIVER`, `byRiver`). `calibrate` is unchanged.
