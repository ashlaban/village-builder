# VB-519: Traits: twelve numbers a pawn, passed on at birth and set at 15, and a culture's values
Status: ready
Priority: 2
Size: M
Arch: talk
Phase: implement
Design ref: design.md, People, households and family records (Traits, talk and stories: The twelve traits; How a trait is passed on); Customs and culture (A culture's values); docs/architecture/target-v2.md, section 4.4 (Persons); backlog/VB-186.md
Goal: The twelve traits Kim decided on 3 November replace the five uniform stand-ins: values from those who raised a child and from its culture, temperament and aptitudes partly from its parents by birth, a hungry childhood paid for in body and wits. Each culture holds the mean of its people's values, read from them and never set. No rule reads a new trait yet: this task is the state, the passing on, the labels and the door.
Acceptance criteria:
- [x] `sim/traits` (made by VB-254 for its five stand-ins) owns `pawn.traits`: twelve numbers in 0.02 to 0.98, in the order of `TRAITS.names` (generosity, piety, ambition, honour; sociability, temper, diligence, openness; body, hands, husbandry, wits), read only through `Traits.of(st, pawn, name)`. The five stand-ins and `pawn.diligence` become these (one home; an old save keeps the values it has and draws the rest). `@state`, `src/owners.json`, invariants in `tests/lib/invariants.js` (twelve finite numbers within the bounds, for the living and the dead), the save.
- [x] One equation passes every trait on (`design.md`, How a trait is passed on): `mean + b × (the two parents' mean − mean) + chance`, the chance normal with spread `TRAITS.sd × √(1 − b²/2)`, drawn from the stream `traits`. `TRAITS` in `src/sim/rules.js`, each with its meaning: `sd` 0.15, `b` 0.45 (temperament), 0.5 (aptitudes), 0.6 (values), `setAge` 15, `lean` { body: 0.2, wits: 0.1, days: 96 }.
- [x] At birth: temperament and aptitudes from the father and mother by birth around 0.5, for good; values from them around the culture's mean, as a child's value. A parent not on record (a founder's) counts as the mean.
- [x] At 15, once, where the pawn's age already rolls over (no new pass over pawns): the values are drawn again from those who raised it, the holder and the mistress of the household it lives in (one of them alone if there is one; the culture's mean if none), around its culture's mean; and a hungry childhood takes from body and wits: `lean.body` and `lean.wits` times min(1, the child's hungry days / `lean.days`). The hungry days are one number a child, `pawn.leanDays`, counted where its household's hunger is already written for its members (hunger of 0.5 or more), and dropped at 15.
- [x] A culture's values (`design.md`, Customs and culture, A culture's values): `culture.values`, four means, 0.5 but honour 0.6 for Old Nordic; founders and placed pawns draw from it with spread `sd`. At the culture's yearly reckoning it becomes the mean of its living adults' values, in the walk the reckoning already makes; a culture that splits takes its own adults' mean at once. `sim/saves.js` keeps it; an old save starts at the archetype's.
- [x] Labels: `Traits.labelsOf(st, pawn)` gives the words of `design.md`'s table for traits under 0.345 or over 0.655 (`TRAITS.label`); the pawn panel shows them and no number. The page reads through the `Sim` API. A screenshot under `docs/img/`.
- [x] Neutral on average: no rule reads a new trait in this task (VB-520 does), so `node tools/same.js --base REF` differs only by the draws of the stream `traits`; say which leaves differ. Where VB-254's stand-ins were uniform and are now normal, report its numbers (hungry household-days by the head's skill in thirds, populous small, 30 years, seeds 3, 7, 11, 42) before and after.
- [x] Tests on a constructed state (`tests/tiny.js`, `tests/lib/probe.js`), with `fix` on the stream: a child of two high parents lies between them and the mean; the spread of 2,000 children of random pairs is within 0.14 to 0.16 for each group; a child raised in another house takes its values from that house and its temperament from its birth parents; a hungry childhood lowers body and wits and nothing else; a split culture's mean is its members'.
- [x] Cost (rule 9): written at birth and at 15, read as an index; no daily or yearly pass but the reckoning's own walk. Twelve numbers a pawn in the save: name the bytes against the save's line (report the JSON's size on a 30-year populous small save before and after; the bench is the night's).

One function passes a trait on (`Traits.pass`: mean + b × (parents' mean − mean) + sd × √(1 − b²/2) × z); z is the inverse normal of one uniform of the stream `traits` (Acklam), so a birth always draws twelve, a 15th birthday four, a placed pawn twelve, whatever the values (and `fix` 0.5 is no chance). `Traits.born` (from `sim/births`, after the child joins its household): from its father and mother by birth, values around its culture's means; a parent not on record counts as the mean. `Traits.setAtAge` (from `sim/lifecycle`'s `dailyLife`, in the walk that already rolls the age over: one comparison a living pawn): the values again from the holder and the holder's spouse of the household it lives in (one alone, or the culture's means if none), and body and wits lowered by `lean` × min(1, leanDays / 96); `pawn.leanDays` is then null. `Traits.hungryDay` (from `sim/hunger`'s loop that writes each member's hunger): one more day where the child's own hunger is 0.5 or more. `seedTraits` (at seed, and at every load) gives every pawn without the twelve, living or dead, a draw from its culture's distribution in id order, keeping an old save's five stand-ins (by their old order) or VB-249's diligence, clamped; and `leanDays` 0 to a living child, null otherwise. A culture's values: `culture.values` {generosity, piety, ambition, honour}, made by `Culture.makeCulture` (the archetype's, given by `sim/society`'s `initCulture`, or the parent's); `cultureYear` sets them to the mean of its living adults (age 15 and over) in its walk of each village's members, now over every village of the culture (those whose meeting did not sit too); `split` takes the new culture's own adults' at once; both through the hook `addValues` (`sim/traits`, given by `sim/society`'s hooks), so `culture.js` stays trait-blind. `sim/saves`: an old save's cultures start at Old Nordic's. `Traits.labelsOf(st, id)` gives the words of design.md's table (honour and openness by sex); `Sim.traitLabels`; the pawn card shows them as one line, no number (`src/page/words.js`). `@state` lines (pawn.traits, pawn.leanDays, culture.values), `src/owners.json` (pawn.leanDays: sim/traits; culture.values: culture), invariants (every pawn's twelve within the bounds, leanDays null or a whole number; every culture's four values within the bounds).
