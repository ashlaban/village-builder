# VB-119: Mixed meals go further, and fresh food spoils
Status: needs-review
Priority: 2
Size: M
Arch: year
Phase: implement
Design ref: Economy and jobs (Goods and the farming year: Mixed meals, Meals; Long work and processing: Milling at home). Job review, second pass, 30 Oct 2026: rations made from several food sources are more nutritious, combined with the flour mechanism (VB-009).
Goal: Fish and gathered food only matter if they change what a household needs. A ration is the unit and the cook makes meals from foods (Kim, 10 November; VB-259): a pot of several foods goes further, which rewards a household that fishes and gathers beside its fields; fresh food spoils, which keeps a summer's catch from feeding a winter; and a household with nothing cooked eats its grain raw, at a loss.
Decided (Kim, 10 November; `QUESTION_ARCHIVE.md`, "VB-119"): variety is judged in the pot, at cooking; uncooked grain at a penalty.
Acceptance criteria:
- [x] Foods in `FOODS` (VB-259): fish (VB-117) and gathered food (VB-118) with a shelf life of 3 days, each its own source; grain and flour one source; milk and meat as their rows say.
- [x] Fresh food is held in batches with the day it came in (`hh.fresh`, `@state`, invariants; at most 4 batches a food, since older ones are lost); a batch older than its shelf life is lost and noted in the ledger, through `sim/stores` with a reason. No daily pass over every household to spoil: a batch's loss is read from its day when the store is next touched, or falls due in the due-queue (Rules for cost, 3 and 4).
- [x] The pot: the cook makes the next day's meals from the household's foods, what spoils first going in first (oldest batch first), then grain up to the need. A food is a source of the pot when it gives at least a fifth of the pot's rations. A pot of 2 sources makes 1 / 0.9 times its rations in meals, of 3 or more 1 / 0.8 (so the day's need is met with 10% or 20% less food, as decided on 30 October).
- [x] People eat meals. A household with no meals eats grain uncooked at a penalty: 1.25 rations of grain for a ration of need (`ECON.rawGrain`; the number is the agent's starting value, to be confirmed in the PR). Fresh food that is marked eaten uncooked in `FOODS` (gathered food) is eaten before raw grain, at no penalty.
- [x] Tests: a household with grain only, and the same with grain and fish at a quarter of the pot, over 12 days: the second uses 10% less food. A household that misses its cooking for 3 days uses 25% more grain on the days it has no meals.

Implementation notes (11 Nov 2026):
- `FOODS` (`src/sim/rules.js`): rows `fish` (keeps 3, source `fish`, cooked) and `gathered` (keeps 3, source `gathered`, `raw`: eaten as they
  are on a day without meals), and a new column `held` (`grain`, `larder` or `fresh`: where a household keeps the food); `raw` now means "may
  be eaten uncooked at no penalty". Grain is one source (flour will be a row with source `grain`, VB-009); milk and meat stay larder foods
  eaten as they are, as their rows say, and are not put in the pot. Tunables in `ECON`: `pot: [1, 1, 0.9, 0.8]` (the food a pot of k sources
  needs), `potShare: 0.2`, `rawGrain: 1.25`.
- Fresh food (`src/sim/stores.js`): `hh.fresh`, batches `{food, day, qty}` by day (`@state`; owner `sim/stores` in `src/owners.json`;
  `fresh[]` in the household's `@state` of `sim/scenarios`); `putFresh` (a day's trips merge into one batch; past `FRESH_CAP`, 4 batches of a
  food, the oldest is lost), `takeFresh` (oldest first), `freshHeld` (reads only; a batch past its day does not count), `settle` (drops the
  batches older than their food's keeps, each noted as `spoilage` of its food in the yearly totals). No pass over households: the store is
  settled when it is touched (the catch, the pot, the day's meal), and `freshHeld` reads a stale batch as gone before then. The batch's
  amount is `qty`, not `n`: a resolved write to `fresh[].n` made `n` a state field's name to `tools/writes.js`, which then listed four older
  writes of `.n` on scratch records as unplaced. `tools/writes.js` gets a hint, `units` in `sim/stores` is a local (the pot's scratch).
- The pot (`Stores.pot`): the cook job (`src/sim/kinds.js`) cooks the meals still missing for the coming day; fresh food goes in oldest
  first, then grain above the seed reserve. The cook fills the smallest pot that makes the meals wanted: it tries a pot of 3 sources (0.8 of
  the want), then of 2 (0.9), then a full pot of any; short of food it puts in all there is. A food is a source at a fifth of the pot's
  rations. `cookWanted` is true with fresh food and no spare grain too. `Stores.cook` (one food, 1 to 1) is kept for its test.
- The meal (`src/sim/hunger.js` `feedHousehold`): larder food about to spoil, then meals, then (no meals) gathered food as it is, then
  grain above the seed reserve uncooked at `rawGrain` (a new routine reason `raw`, so the yearly totals count the days and the grain), then
  the rest of the larder, then the seed, uncooked too; on a short day all of it goes by the feed order. `foodHeld` (the need rule),
  `sourceHeld` (a food the pot lacks) and fishing's and gathering's `spareDays` count fresh food. The catch and the baskets go into `hh.fresh`
  (reasons `catch` and `gathering`).
- Saves: an older save's households get an empty fresh store at load (`Stores.openFresh`). Invariants (`tests/lib/invariants.js`): batches of
  fresh foods only, amount above 0, day an integer not after today, in day order, at most `FRESH_CAP` a food; larder lots are of `held:
  'larder'` foods; ledger and totals goods include `fish` and `gathered`.
- Tests: new `tests/pot.js` (in `npm test`): the table; grain only against grain with fish at a quarter of the pot over 12 days (12 days of
  need against 0.9 of it); 3 sources at 0.8; a food under a fifth is no source; batches merge by day, the oldest goes first, a batch is good on
  its third day, read as gone on its fourth and noted when the meal touches the store; the cap of 4; 3 days without cooking use 1.25 times
  the grain, and cooked again 1 to 1; gathered food eaten before raw grain, fish not eaten uncooked; a short day; 36 days of founders 7 with
  mixed pots and the invariants. `tests/meals.js` (a missed cook eats grain at 1.25), `tests/fish.js` and `tests/forage.js` (the catch and
  the baskets are fresh food) follow.

Orchestrator's decisions for Kim to overturn:
- "Older than its shelf life": a batch that came in on day d is lost from day d + keeps + 1 (fish caught on day 0 is good through day 3); with
  a day's trips in one batch a food never holds more than 4 batches, and the cap of 4 only bites if a shelf life grows.
- Milk and meat are not put in the pot: their rows say they are eaten as they are, from the larder (VB-134), so they add no variety yet.
- The cook makes the meals wanted with the least food: a 3-source pot is tried first, then 2, then 1 (a full pot). Fresh food beyond the
  day's want stays in its batch (and may spoil); the cook does not cook it all.
- Gathered food is `raw` (eaten as it is on a day without meals); fish is not, so a household without meals or grain goes hungry beside its fish.
- The seed, eaten on a short day, is uncooked grain too, at `rawGrain`.
- `rawGrain` 1.25 is the starting value, to be confirmed: the penalty costs about 2,300 more rations of grain over 5 years at seed 7 (about
  6 a household a year) and adds 4 hungry days at one seed of four.
- Numbers to watch, and a follow-up worth a task: the cook is missed on about a sixth of a household's days even with a wife at home (seed 7),
  so the penalty bites far more often than "one missed cooking uses the buffer" (design.md, Meals) supposes. Why the cook is missed (the
