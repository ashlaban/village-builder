What the evidence supports, and what it does not:
- No yield trend from selection on a game's timescale: the gains of selection are measured over millennia (landraces about 50% over wild ancestors; "significantly, but not radically" higher yields in AD 1500 than in 1500 BC). So no rule by which a farm's seed improves with the years it is kept.
- Seed as a strain fitted to a climate band: Nordic landraces were the locally available genotypes over the long term, and barley from one latitude is more alike across countries than across latitudes within Sweden; seed from elsewhere ripens late and fails more often in a cold year. A risk, not a yield, fading over a few generations of the crop on its new ground.

- [ ] Slice 10's plan decides whether this is built, once regions have a climate: a household's seed of a crop carries the climate band it was grown in (one small value with the seed, not a variety a field); seed sown in a colder band than its own ripens later by a named number of days a band and so meets the frost more often (the crops' existing reading of the season and the frost day, VB-354), and each crop grown there moves the seed a named share of the way to the band it is grown in.
- [ ] If built: nothing teleports (seed bought or begged from another region is carried); after a failure a household seeks seed first from neighbours of its own band (the gift and loan rules as they are); the head's choice of where to get seed is its own estimate (principle "The future is someone's estimate"); place matters (principle "Place matters": a northern village's seed is worth more to its neighbours than a southern trader's).
- [ ] Sources read in full before any number is set: the size of the delay a band and how fast a strain settles are not in the evidence gathered (Leino, Hagenblad et al. have no yield data and call local adaptation speculative; Suneson 1956 is a mixed population under trial, not a farm). Without them the numbers are named tunables marked as estimates, with rows of `docs/tuning.md`.
- [ ] Unchecked and worth checking: Walter of Henley's advice to change seed yearly (from memory), which would pull the other way (fresh seed from elsewhere as a gain).
- [ ] Numbers before and after, if built, at the seeds and maps slice 10's plan names: failed harvests by seed's origin, hungry days a household (the distribution), growth; cost named (households × crops, one value read at sowing; no pass).
