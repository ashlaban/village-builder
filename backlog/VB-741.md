Goal: Before coin, silver and gold are goods of worth only (Kim, 16 Nov): a household can turn a surplus into worth that waits, and give it.
Acceptance criteria:
- [ ] A good `worth` with kinds silver and gold, by the öre, and amber, by the string of beads (VB-733): worth rows, Proposed, 20, 200 and 10 rations (gold at ten times silver: Kim). Got only from someone who has it or from the ground; at a swap it is a good like any other (barter until coin: Decided, 10 Nov), never change.
- [ ] Gold is dug as metal (VB-736). Amber is gathered on a beach tile that holds it, as clay is dug (3 hours a trip, a load), and bored into beads at home (4 hours a string); a fiftieth of the strings held are lost a year (brittle), taken on the planning day.
- [ ] Kept: the head's target for worth (VB-698) is what it means to give (a child's dowry or morning gift ahead, a fine owed, a follower to bind), never "all it can": a head does not farm for silver. Reported: whether silver draws surplus away from feasts (principle 1).
- [ ] Given: VB-276's dowry and morning gift may be paid in worth, carried with the bride; a gift to a follower (VB-708) and a fine (VB-315) likewise where those are built; each is a journey.
- [ ] Worn: an icon on the pawn's row (principle 15) and a term of standing for the holder and spouse, 0.02 for each 20 rations' worth held, at most 0.15 (Proposed; a deed of VB-705).
- [ ] Invariants: silver and gold conserved (dug, smelted, held, buried: nothing vanishes); `tools/stocks.js` lines.
- [ ] Numbers, populous small, seeds 3, 7, 11, 42, 15, 30 years: worth held by the richest tenth of households and by the median, by kind; dowries paid in worth; barrels poured at feasts before and after (does worth compete with the feast); villages with silver, gold or amber within 30 steps against villages holding any.
- [ ] The numbers that move are reported before and after at the seeds and maps named (Working rules); `npm run verify` passes its hard checks.
