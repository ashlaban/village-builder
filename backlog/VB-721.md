- [ ] A source for the size before any number is set: what ditching gave a wet field in the North (Gadd 2000 for the 18th and 19th centuries, from memory; a medieval or early modern figure for ridging and open ditches if one exists). `docs/review-yields.md`, 2.5, has only an estimate ("a quarter or more", not to be built on). Without a source the size is a named tunable marked as an estimate, with a row of `docs/tuning.md`.
- [ ] The wetness of a place, from slice 10's plan (VB-190, 16 Nov 2026; `design.md`, Climate and travel beyond walking, Climate): it is the tile's moisture, `w.hm` (0 to 1: the rain the seed's wind brings and the water of its valley, VB-342), which exists; nothing new is stored. A field is wet above a named threshold, `WET.from`, set where about a tenth of today's fields lie above it (the share measured and written here), and loses fertility in proportion beyond it.
- [ ] The mechanism, not a multiplier (principle 8): a wet field (a marsh's edge, a wet hollow, by the wetness slice 10 gives a tile) loses fertility to its wetness; a ditch takes away a share of that loss. Marsh stays 0.5 by its biome unless slice 10's plan moves the wet part of that into wetness.
- [ ] A project a head may start (principle 16: every act is a pawn's; a choice the player has is one every head makes): a verb in `src/sim/commands.js`, a project plan of `sim/plans` as making a field is, with its hours a tile (a named tunable, sourced or marked), dug by pawns who walk there; the head chooses it by its own estimate of the gain against the hours (principle 14), as it chooses to break a field.
- [ ] Upkeep: a ditch silts up; the gain fades unless it is cleared every few years (stored as a value and the time it was set, read in closed form: Rules for cost 3), so drainage is kept by work, as tilth is by dung.
- [ ] State, invariants, saves, `tools/stocks.js` where it applies; the field's card shows wetness and the ditch on the one meter (principle 15).
- [ ] Numbers before and after at the seeds and maps slice 10's plan names: fields made on wet ground, grain a field there, hours spent ditching, hungry days a household (the distribution), growth; whether place matters more for it (principle 9: a village on wet clay with labour to spare gains land others cannot use).
- [ ] Cost named in the plan (Rules for cost 9): drained fields × one closed-form read at reaping; the project's hours as any project's.
