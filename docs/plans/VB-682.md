# VB-682: the moving round as offers pawns make and answer (16 November 2026)

The plan of VB-682 (arch `acts`; `AGENTS.md`, Deciding without Kim, 16: every act is a pawn's). Today the moving round is the actor:
its matcher pairs the unmarried, its steps hand out farms, plots, places in service and new land, and three rules outside it move
people with no pawn choosing (a guardian, kin taken in, the people of a dissolved household). This plan turns each into an offer one
pawn makes and another answers (VB-690's verb pair), says what each of them weighs, names the cost, and writes the tasks. The
measure is `docs/plans/VB-682-pools.js`, a throwaway script: `node docs/plans/VB-682-pools.js $PWD populous small 7,42 20`.

Written without Kim in the room (the orchestrated run of 16 November). What moves a Decided point is decided here by the best reading
of the design and of history, marked **Proposed** in `design.md`, and listed in section 9 for Kim to strike.

## 1. What the plan starts from: Kim's four answers (16 November)

1. Offers are made all the year, and the round is their due date.
2. A move needs the pawn's yes and the receiving head's; the head left is asked only for a marriage or one under age.
3. Heads arrange matches and the member may refuse; acceptance weighs opinion between the two and between the heads, and has
   consequences in opinion.
4. A pawn pursues one rung at a time, a rung being one answer to "where do I live next year": stay (as heir, or at home), marry onto
   a holding, a plot or an empty farm, a place in service, new land.

Left to this plan by Kim: succession (the holder hands on, the heir answers), and which of the nine ways a pawn changes household
become one `move` act.

## 2. The offer about a life: its shape and its times

An offer is VB-690's: plain data `{id, from, to, basis, gives, wants, due}` in the due-queue. This plan adds six bases to its table,
and nothing to its verbs.

| Basis | From, to | Gives and wants | Due | A yes books |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `match` | a spokesman (or the one to marry, who speaks for itself), to the other side's spokesman | the two, and the home they would live in | the round; at once at a gathering both attend (VB-298) and for a child on the way (VB-555, a servant excepted: VB-634) | the marriage (`marry`), the spouse's `move`, the handover the custom brings |
| `place` | a head short of hands to an adult, or the adult to the head | a year's keep (and the portion, VB-637), for a year's work | the round; at once for a household taken in | the `move`, reason `service`, and the year (`servedSince`, VB-634) |
| `care` | the nearest kin in the custom's order, each in turn, to the household in need; or its grown holder to a kin head | a home, or a guardian's coming; the use of the fields for a farm held in trust | at once | the `move`s, the guardian's office or the trust; goodwill, as help does |
| `land` | a landless adult (or a betrothed pair, VB-825) to the alderman of the farm's or plot's village | a named empty farm, or a plot | the round | the claim and its restoration jobs, or the plot (as built) |
| `party` | one who would leave for new land, to those who may come | a share in the new farm, for hands and stakes | the round | the party; its steps, and the `move` at their end, are VB-445's |
| `handover` | a holder to the first in line | the holding, for a keep on the farm for life | the round (the custom's timing); an inheritance at a death is taken up or renounced at once | `handOn`, and the heir's family's `move` |

**Three times.** Kim's first answer separates when an offer is made from when it is answered:

- **The asking day.** A pawn who seeks (unmarried and of age, or grown and landless) reviews its wish (VB-680) once a season, on
  season day `id mod SEASON_DAYS`, at 6:00: a due entry, never a pass over the living (Rules for cost, 4 and 5). If it holds no open
  offer about its own future it makes the one its best open rung asks for; a son or daughter of the house has the match made by its
  spokesman, on the member's asking day. So at most four askings a seeker a year, and one in the usual year, since an open offer
  stands until the round. Occasions make offers too, on their own days: a good talk that starts a courtship (VB-036), a suitor's
  arrival (VB-289), a gathering (VB-298).
- **The due time**, when the one asked answers, once (VB-690): the round for a life's bases, at once for the urgent ones. The played
  pawn is asked when the offer is made and may answer on any day until it is due; unanswered, its pawn's judgement answers, which the
  page has shown it as advice all along (principle 14: the advice is that pawn's estimate, and says so).
- **The round concludes.** It chooses nobody: it answers what is due and makes the moves, each a journey (the hard rule).

**The round lasts three days** (autumn days 11 to 13; `ASK.passes` 3; Proposed, it was one day). On each day every village holds its
step as today (a village a step; the starting village turns by the year, as Decided). A village's step answers the offers due that
are addressed to its people, in this order: `handover`, `match`, `land` (farms, then plots), `party`, `place`. A pawn takes part
only in the step of the one offer it holds, so the order ranks nobody's wishes: it only lets a yes of one basis be known to the next
(a home handed on before a match that needs it; land before the party that would leave for want of it; service last, for those
nothing else took). One refused on a day makes its next offer that evening, due the next day; what is still open after the third
day lapses, and the pawn asks again on its winter asking day. History has this as the servants' free week at the autumn moving day
(*flyttdag*, with its *friveckan*), when hirings were made and unmade face to face.

**Caps** (Rules for cost, 2). A pawn holds one open offer about its own future (`pawn.ask`, the offer's id or null; an invariant).
A head may besides hold one open `place` offer for its household and one `match` offer for each member it speaks for. Nobody is
asked on one basis by more than `ASK.held` (5) open offers: the sixth asker knows it (who is spoken for is news, which moves at
once) and turns to its next best. A spokesman weighs at most `ASK.pool` (40) candidates for a match, nearest villages first.

## 3. The rungs and the wish (what VB-680 is given)

A wish is a ranking of rungs. Each rung is open or shut to a pawn by a fact, and the pawn asks for the best-ranked open one.

| Rung | Open when | The ask | Default rank |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Heir | first in line to the farm it lives on | none: it stays, and answers the handover | 1 |
| Marry onto a holding | unmarried, of age, and a partner with a place (or its own place) within the pool | `match` | 2 |
| An empty farm | a claimable farm within 6 hours that the custom lets it claim | `land`, the farm named | 3 |
| A plot | its village has a plot site; or a small village within reach has, kin there first | `land`, a plot | 4 |
| New land | its village is crowded (no plot site; as built, `v.crowded`) and it has waited `SETTLE.patience` rounds there | `party` | 5 |
| Service | its home keeps `SERVICE.short` hands a field without it, and a head within a day's walk is short | `place` | 6 |
| Stay at home | always | none | 7 |

What shifts the default (each a threshold on something the pawn has, none a draw):

- **Ambition** (the trait; `TRAITS.acts.ambition.settle`, 1.5 at ambition 0 down to 0.5 at 1, as built): the rounds a pawn gives
  its village to find it land before New land opens are `SETTLE.patience` (2) times that factor, rounded up: 3 rounds at ambition
  0, 2 at 0.5, 1 at 1. At ambition 0.75 or more New land ranks above A plot. This replaces the chance to leave (0.3, 0.6, 0.8 by
  rounds waited; Agreed, 2 Nov): the mean wait today is about 2.3 rounds at ambition 0.5.
- **Courting** (VB-036): a courting pawn's match rung is the one it courts, and it asks no other while the courtship lasts.
- **A refusal** (VB-036): a couple whose head refused ranks Service above Stay at home at the next round, whatever the hands at
  home, when each holds the head at an opinion under 0 (it was a chance of 0.5).
- **Not this year**: a pawn ranks Stay above Marry while it or a housemate it keeps is at a hunger of `HUNGER.marryBelow` or more
  (the gate's fourth line, as the pawn's own judgement).
- **The played pawn** sets its ranking with the `wish` verb; `st.player.wishes` goes into the pawn's wish (VB-680, VB-692).

A match may ride on a place rung: a pair agreed on a match whose home is a place still asked for is betrothed, asks for land as a
couple, and marries at the round after the place is theirs (section 4.1, and VB-825).

## 4. Each crossing: who offers, who answers, what each weighs

"Today" names the present rule and its numbers, so that each term of the present score has a place in what a pawn weighs.

### 4.1 Marriage (VB-036, VB-693, VB-825, VB-826)

- **Today.** A village's round walks its unmarried men (those with a place first, then the oldest); each takes the best-scoring free
  woman of any village whose kin degree and home the custom allows. The score (`matchScore`): `-|gap - 3| x 0.3` (0.15 for the
  widowed) `+ 2` in one village (else twice the lesser tie to the culture's centre; VB-288 makes it `2 x exp(-hours / 8)`)
  `+ 1.5 x noise + 1 x` the mean of the two's opinions; at least `minScore` -1.5; then a go-ahead roll of 0.75, the means of the
  home (`affords`), and an offer the two and their spokesmen answer, an unplayed pawn saying no only at enmity (-0.5).
- **Who offers.** The spokesman of one who wishes to marry (the head of the house for a son or daughter of it; itself otherwise:
  Agreed, 9 Nov), on the member's asking day. The man's side asks, as the suitor's side did; from the summer asking day a woman's
  side that holds no offer asks too, and one who holds a farm and speaks for herself asks from the first (Proposed: the verb is any
  pawn's, principle 16; who customarily asks is the custom of VB-826). A courting pair's offer is their own and comes first: the
  spokesman of a courting member arranges nothing else.
- **Whom.** The best-scoring partner the gate allows (age, kin limit, a home under the residence custom; the means are read at the
  round) among the unmarried of the villages within a day's walk, of a village he visited since the last round (VB-289) and of
  those at his last gathering (VB-298): VB-288's pool, narrowed by VB-289. The score is today's, with two changes: the noise is
  drawn once, at the asking, and is that spokesman's fancy; and `MARRIAGE.headsLiking` (0.5) times the asking spokesman's opinion of
  the other spokesman is added (0 between strangers, so the default score is today's). It must reach `minScore`.
- **Who answers, at the round.** The four of today (`answersOf`): the two, and each one's spokesman where another speaks for it.
  Her side answers the best offer it holds and refuses the rest. Each weighs:
  - a spokesman: the score as his side reads it (the same terms, his own opinion of the other spokesman, the noise at its mean),
    at least `minScore`; the means of the home (`marriage.means`, VB-631), read now; the terms, when VB-276 has landed;
  - one to marry: no at enmity towards the other, as today; no while it courts another, unless it holds its spokesman dearer than
    the one it courts (opinion against opinion), in which case it yields and remembers; no under "not this year".
- **The go-ahead roll goes** (VB-405's plan kept it "until the heads answer by opinion and courting has landed"). It delayed a
  marriage by a third of a year on average and answered to nothing.
- **What the custom allows.** The residence custom (where the home is), the kin limit, the least ages, and, new, Who makes a match
  (VB-826; Proposed; the design named it a candidate on 9 Nov): *the heads* (a member's no costs it twice the memory), *the heads
  with consent* (the default: as above), *the couple* (each speaks for itself; a head is not asked).
- **Consequences in opinion** (`SOCIAL.memories`; Kim's third answer). `refusedMatch` -0.2 to -0.4, 3 years (as built: a head of the
  member who refused his match). `refusedUs` -0.2 to -0.4, 3 years (the courting two, of the head who refused them; VB-036).
  `refusedSuit` -0.1 to -0.3, 2 years (a spokesman, of the spokesman who refused his offer; not booked when hers took a better
  offer, which is no slight). `marriedAgainst` -0.3 to -0.3, 5 years (a member who yielded, of its spokesman).
- **Betrothal** (VB-825; Proposed). A match whose home is a place asked for (a farm claimed, a plot, new land) is answered as any;
  agreed, the two are betrothed: held for each other, counted as a couple in the custom's order for land (couples before singles,
  as built), and married at the first round after the place is theirs, if the gate still holds. It lapses at the third round, or
  when either dies. The gate is not moved (Decided, 13 Nov): it is read at the marriage.

### 4.2 Service (VB-634, VB-694)

- **Today.** Three rules: a holder who lives alone takes the unmarried landless adult (18 to 50, in `st.demand`) of its village,
  else of the nearest within 12 hours, whose home keeps 2 hands, the home with most hands first, then the oldest; then, oldest
  first, adults whose home has more than 1.5 hands a field and keeps 1 without them go to the household under 1 hand a field with
  the fewest, nearest village first, never to a parent's; and a household taken in before it starves gets a servant at once.
- **Who offers.** A head whose household is short (under `SERVICE.short` hands a field, or living alone) offers a place, on his
  asking day, to one adult: kin first, then by his opinion of them, then the nearer, among those of his village and within a
  day's walk whose wish has Service open. Or the adult whose best open rung is Service asks such a head, by the same order read
  from its side (kin, opinion, nearness, then the fewest hands a field).
- **Who answers, and what each weighs.** The adult: no at enmity towards the head; no when its home would be left under
  `SERVICE.short` hands a field (its own judgement that it is needed, in place of the round's test); no when the master's house is
  hungrier than its own (the mean hunger of the two households, read now); of several places offered it takes the best by its
  order. The head asked: yes when still short at the round and not at enmity. The head of the home left is not asked (Kim's
  second answer): an adult leaves for service without leave.
- **The year** (VB-634, unchanged and landed first). Service runs from round to round. At the round a servant stays without
  asking, unless a better rung is open to it; a servant in a house no longer short has Service open elsewhere, as today. Nobody is
  dismissed: no verb is added for it.
- **Memory.** `refusedPlace` -0.05 to -0.15, 1 year: an everyday no.
- **The custom.** None today; the hands a field (`SERVICE.short`, `spare`) stay what decides who serves, and no share or count of
  years is set (VB-634).

### 4.3 Kin care and the guardian (VB-695)

- **Today.** Checked when a household's membership changes (VB-323) or its ask for food falls short (VB-308), with no pawn acting:
  the ward's nearest landless grown relative (kinship, then the oldest; within walking reach first) moves in as guardian with its
  family; a household of at most one grown member is taken in by such a relative, else a servant; with nobody to come, its people
  go to the nearest kin household within reach, which holds the farm in trust (`goToKin`, the only one of these with a verb); a
  dissolved household's people are placed with their nearest kin, or in service in the village.
- **Who offers.** Kin offer, since news of a death or of a house in want moves at once and a child cannot ask: the relatives in
  today's order, each in turn and each due at once, so the whole chain is one call, as today. A grown holder in need asks instead,
  down the same order. The people of a dissolved household each ask for themselves (`care` to kin, else `place`), a child through
  the grown member who keeps it, or through the kin who offer.
- **Who answers, and what each weighs.** A relative asked to come as guardian or to move in: free to move (as built: unmarried,
  landless, 18 to 50, its home keeping a grown member), and not at enmity with the household's grown members. A kin head asked to
  take people in or hold a farm in trust: yes unless his own household is hungry (mean hunger at `HUNGER.marryBelow` or more: he
  cannot feed his own), or he is at enmity with the asker and they are kin beyond the second degree (parents, children and
  siblings do not turn each other away). A child's yes is not asked.
- **A refusal is remembered**: `refusedKin` -0.4 to -0.4, 5 years, by each grown member of the household refused, and by a ward
  from 12. A yes books `tookUsIn` +0.3 to +0.3, 5 years, beside the goodwill help books.
- **The guardian acts as head** of the ward's farm until the heir is 18: sim/plans, sim/aims and the head's verbs read him so.
- **The custom.** The order of kin is the inheritance customs', as today.

### 4.4 An empty farm or a plot (VB-697)

- **Today.** After the villages' rounds: each claimable farm goes to the first of `st.demand` in its village's custom's order (kin
  of the old family while the birthright stands, villagers, anyone, the meeting's support), couples before singles, then the
  oldest, from within 6 hours; then plots are started for those still waiting, where a site is found.
- **Who offers.** The landless adult, or the betrothed pair, whose best open rung it is: for the farm it names (of those it may
  claim, the one with the most fields, then the best kept), else for a plot, in its own village or in a small village within
  reach (1 to 3 farms, kin there first; today a party joins such a village unasked).
- **Who answers.** The alderman of that village (VB-314), by the custom in force: among several asking for one farm the custom's
  order decides, exactly as today; under "the meeting assigns" he carries out what the meeting's support gives. He adds no
  judgement of his own, and the no he gives says which line of the custom stood in the way.
- **Memory.** None against the alderman for a custom's no; the grievance against the rule is the claims review's (as built).

### 4.5 New land (VB-696; the steps are VB-445's)

- **Today.** In a crowded village each waiting adult of 18 to 45, longest waiting first, leaves by a chance (0.3, 0.6, 0.8 by rounds
  felt), with its spouse, their minor children and each waiting unmarried sibling at a chance of 0.5 (at most 2), if the party has
  42 days of rations and seed for a field; to a small village within 30 steps, else a new site.
- **Who offers.** The pawn whose wish reaches New land (section 3) calls a party: a `party` offer to its spouse and to each
  unmarried sibling waiting in the village (at most `SETTLE.maxFollow`, 2, the eldest first).
- **Who answers, and what each weighs.** The spouse: yes unless she, or a child she keeps, is at a hunger of
  `HUNGER.marryBelow` or more (a hungry house does not start a farm); the provisions themselves are counted at the move, by
  VB-445's plan, and are no part of the answer. A sibling:
  yes when it holds no open offer of its own and its opinion of the caller is `SETTLE.followLiking` (0) or more (it was a chance of
  0.5). Minor children are not asked.
- **How settling fits** (VB-445's plan landed the same night: `design.md`, Moving to new land, Settling in steps). Its step 1,
  at the round, is where the call is made and answered: its "the leader is found as before" becomes the wish's threshold, and
  "the siblings who follow" those who said yes. The call and its answers say only who goes. What the party then does (the site, the trail, the booth, the
  move, and when in the year each falls) is VB-445's plan; the party's members change household by `move`, reason `settle`, at the
  step VB-445 names, and until then they are members at home. Joining a small village is the Plot rung (4.4), asked of its
  alderman.
- **The custom.** None; who leaves and the distances are Decided (1 Nov) and stand.

### 4.6 Succession (VB-824; left to this plan by Kim)

- **Today.** `handOn` and `transfer`: by the custom's timing (the heir's marriage, the holder at 60, the death) the farm goes to
  the first in the ranking, who moves in with spouse and children; an heir who holds a farm or lives on a spouse's is passed over.
- **In life.** The holder offers (`handover`), when the custom says he should; the played holder when it chooses. He gives the
  holding and wants his keep on the farm for life (*undantag*). The first in line answers: yes, unless it holds or is betrothed
  to a place with more fields; a no passes the offer to the next in line, with no memory. The custom's timing is thus when an
  unplayed holder offers, a rule the pawns hold.
- **At a death.** An inheritance is a right, and falls at once to the first in line (rights move at once). The heir takes it up
  by walking there (`move`, reason `heir`), or renounces it when it holds a better place, and it falls to the next.

## 5. Whose yes, the nine ways, and one `move`

**Whose yes** is one function, `moveWhy(st, pawn, household, reason)`, read by every basis: the pawn's own yes and the receiving
head's, always; the head left only for a marriage (as spokesman, where the custom gives him a voice) or for one under 18; a minor
moves only with the grown pawn who keeps it, or under a `care` offer its kin answered.

The code changes a pawn's household through `moveTo` (ten call sites) and `foundHousehold`. The ways:

| # | The way | Today | Becomes |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Marriage: the spouse who moves | `marry` (`wed`) | `move`, reason `marriage`, issued by `marry` |
| 2 | Taking up a farm: the heir, spouse and children | `handOn` (`transfer`) | `move`, reason `heir`, issued by `handOn` |
| 3 | A holder marries away: the giver and its minor children leave | `handOn` of sim/heirs, inside the marriage | `move`, reason `marriage` |
| 4 | Service | `serve` (`enterService`) | `move`, reason `service`; the verb `serve` goes |
| 5 | A guardian comes, with family | none: `careForOrphans` moves them | `move`, reason `guardian` |
| 6 | Kin taken in | none: `takeIn` moves them | `move`, reason `care` |
| 7 | To kin, who hold the farm in trust | `goToKin` | `move`, reason `care`, issued by `goToKin` |
| 8 | Back from kin | `goBack` | `move`, reason `home`, issued by `goBack` |
| 9 | A dissolved household's people | none: `extinct` places them | `move`, reason `care` or `service`, each its own |
| - | Founding: a cottage, a restored farm, settlers | `finishCottage`, `foundHousehold`, `leave` | stay apart; their members enter by `move`, reason `found` or `settle` |

**One act:** every change of household is the verb `move(actor, household, {reason, with})`, `with` the minors who follow. It is
the one caller of `moveTo`, so the consent rule, the journey and its provisions (the hard rule), the `moved` event with its reason
and the servant's flag and year (reason `service`) are each written once. Ways 4, 5, 6 and 9 are `move` and nothing more.

**What stays apart, and why:** `marry`, `handOn`, `goToKin`, `goBack` and the three foundings change a right as well (a union, a
holding, a trust, a household's existence). A right moves at once and where it lies; the person walks. Folding them into `move`
would hide a right inside a journey, so each keeps its verb, concludes its offer, and issues `move` for the people. Birth and death
are natural processes and no act.

- **No pass over pairs.** Nobody is scored against the island: a spokesman weighs those within a day's walk, capped at `ASK.pool`.
  Today each village's round reads every unmarried woman of the island for each of its men (`roundPairs`, exponent 2 in
  `tests/scaling.js` against a target of 1.15, and `history.roundPairs` out by 11.9); the asking's work grows with the seekers,
  not with their product, so both targets are expected in. Reported by the tasks, not promised.
- **Scheduled, not polled** (rule 4): an asking is a due entry `{t, pawn}` written when a pawn comes of age to seek, at each
  asking for the next season, and dropped when it marries, takes a place or dies; the round's answers read VB-690's queue.
- **Bounded** (rule 1, 2): an answered or lapsed offer leaves the queue; `pawn.ask` is one id; `ASK.held` and `ASK.pool` cap the
  rest. Nothing reads the dead or the record.
- **Spread** (rule 5): askings over the season's twelve days by id; the round over three days, a village a step.
- **Counters** (`VB.COUNTERS`): `asksMade`, `asksWeighed` (candidates read at an asking), `offersAnswered`; `roundPairs` falls to 0

## 8. How the demography targets are held (principle 2)

- **The gate does not move.** A marriage still needs a place under the residence custom, the kin limit, the means and no hunger
  (Decided, 13 Nov); they are read at the round, by the `marry` verb's side, as today. Offers change who asks whom and when, not
  whether a pair may marry. The valve's setpoint (`marriage.means`) is untouched.
- **Nobody falls through for want of an asker.** Every seeker has an asking day each season and a spokesman who must ask if
  anyone is allowed; a woman's side asks from summer. VB-680's default wish is today's order of the round's steps.
- **Each task is measured alone**, at the seeds and maps its criteria name (populous small, 30 years, seeds 3, 7, 11, 42; founders
  small, 50 years, seeds 7 and 42; `tools/marriage-demography.js` for marriages), against the bounds VB-288 reports against:
  marriages a year and the mean age at first marriage within 5%, the never-married at 45 to 54 within 2 points, and growth by
  principle 2 (the median seed at 1% a year or more).
- **What is expected to move**, so that a task knows a cause from a fault: the end of the go-ahead roll brings first marriages
  about a third of a year earlier (the band is 20.2 to 22.3, today 21.3); three days of asking let a refused suitor marry the same
  autumn; a threshold in place of the settler's chance moves when parties leave (earlier for the ambitious, later for the rest),
  not how many the land takes; `care` refused by a hungry kin head sends some children one household further. Betrothal raises
  marriages under "with whoever holds land" by letting a pair be a couple in the order for land.
- **If a target goes out**, the task reports it (before, after, residual, cause) and may look for a parameterisation in at most
  five short runs over the parameters it adds (`ASK.passes`, `ASK.held`, `MARRIAGE.headsLiking`, `SETTLE.patience`,
  `SETTLE.followLiking`); the rest is a row of `docs/tuning.md`. A median seed that shrinks is a collapse and is mended before the
  task ends. VB-828 measures the whole on long runs and the nightly band.

## 9. Decided here, for Kim to strike (each Proposed in `design.md`, each a `decision` note)

1. **The round lasts three days, and her village's step answers.** Moves "A village's round" (Decided, 10 Nov: one day, the man's
   village matches its men). Why: Kim's fourth answer needs a refused pawn to try its next rung, and one day gives one answer; the
   servants' free week is the history. The year's turning start (Decided, 10 Nov) is kept, though with all offers in before the
   round no village's men ask first any more.
2. **A threshold replaces the three draws** (the go-ahead roll, the chance to leave, the sibling's chance to follow): principle 19,
   and Kim's "in place of the draw" (VB-696). Close call: the rounds waited (`SETTLE.patience`) are a count of years; it stands as
   the pawn's fading hope of land at home, beside crowding, provisions and VB-445's steps, never alone.
3. **A woman's side may ask** from the summer, and a woman who holds a farm at once. Close call: history has the suitor ask;
   principle 16 has the verb any pawn's. Made a custom's matter (VB-826).
4. **Kin may refuse care**: only a hungry household, or an enemy beyond the second degree. Moves "A guardian for an orphaned
   heir" (Decided, 1 and 13 Nov) from a rule to an offer that can meet a no; Kim's answer of 16 Nov asks that a refusal be
   remembered, which needs one to be possible.
5. **`serve` goes into `move`**, and `marry`, `handOn`, the trust's two verbs and the foundings stay (section 5).
6. **Betrothal**: a pair with a place asked for is a couple in the order for land, and marries at the round after it has the place.
7. **Succession at a death is taken up or renounced**, and in life it is the holder's offer.
8. **Joining a small village is asking its alderman for a plot**, not a party arriving unasked (Kim's second answer: the
   receiving side's yes).

Principle candidate (the third decision of one kind, in 2): **a chance that stands for a judgement becomes the judgement.** A
pawn's yes is a threshold on what it weighs (an opinion, a store, a trait); chance is kept for what nobody decides (weather, a
birth, a death, a find, the noise of a fancy drawn once).
