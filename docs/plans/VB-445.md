# VB-445: settling in steps (16 November 2026)

The plan behind `design.md`, Moving to new land (the rows Provisions, Where, The new site, The booth and The new village, and the
subsections Settling in steps and Sleeping away from home). Kim, 15 November: "we need to refine the mechanism for settling a new
farmstead (becoming a new village if far enough away). The establishment would require several steps and the mechanism for sleeping
away from the home"; and: they may build a farm of forest. Written in a night's run with nobody to ask: what would have gone to Kim is
decided here, marked Proposed in the design, and listed in section 9.

## 1. What stands today

- `sim/settle`, at the moving round: a waiting adult of a crowded village leaves by a chance (`SETTLE.chance`, by rounds waited and
  ambition), with a party; the provisions (42 days and seed) are checked and taken; a small village within `SETTLE.join` (30) walking
  steps is joined, else a site is searched 10 to 30 walking steps from the green; the household and, on a new site, the village are
  founded in that step, and the party walks there as a journey. It lives in a booth (`f.booth`, a flag and the cottage's stages, not a
  built thing) and builds its cottage as its household's work.
- **The reach is steps, not cost.** `reachOf` is a breadth-first search over passable tiles, so a tile of thick forest counts as one
  of meadow: the task's note that the search is "by cost today" does not hold. Dear forest therefore does not shorten today's search;
  what stops it on the forested island is that a site must be meadow (below).
- **The lower bound never binds.** A site is at least `centreApart` (12) hex steps from every centre, the home's too, and walking
  steps are never fewer than hex steps: `SETTLE.reach[0]` (10) rules nothing out.
- A journey (`st.journeys`, in `sim/agents`) is the only way a pawn sleeps away: on the road, where its leg ends, eating a carried
  ration a day (`j.food`, taken uncooked from its household's grain above seed), left out of its household's meals by a test of its
  phase (`sim/hunger`, `onRoad`). Nothing lets a pawn sleep at a place and work from it.
- A forest tile is not farmable (`FERTILITY` has no forest), but a plot fells and grubs wooded tiles already (`sim/expansion`: fell 10
  hours, grub 6, the trees the plot's timber, the tile meadow afterwards), and a field broken on it is fresh land.

## 3. Decisions

1. **The far bound is 6 hours along the trail** (`SETTLE.hours`; Proposed, and it moves the Decided 10 to 30 steps). Argued from the
   walker's day, as VB-444's plan kept every bound that is argued in hours: 6 hours is there and back in a day (Between villages,
   Near and far), so a worker fetches its rations and a daughter comes to the mother's gathering without a night on the road, and
   kin help reaches it at once. The 30 steps were 10 hours at the 3 tiles an hour of 1 November, when they were decided ("within a
   day's walk", as the round's log still says), 2.5 at 12 and 3.75 at 8; 6 lies between what they were and what they became.
   Counted along the trail, not the ground as it stands, since the party cuts the trail before it carries anything.
2. **The room stays 12 hex steps** (`centreApart`), by section 2's measure, and answers "when is it a new village": always, at that
   distance. `SETTLE.reach` goes; its lower bound was dead.
3. **The small village is joined within 6 hours by the villages' table** (`SETTLE.join`), the same hours, read from the compiled
   table as it stands: no search.
4. **Forest is a site**, scored as the meadow it becomes. By the hours the design has, a wooded tile costs 16 to fell and grub and
   a meadow tile 10 to 30 to clear, so forest is no dearer; it brings its timber and it is fresh land. That may be too kind to
   forest (a tile holds 60 to 80 trees and is felled in 10 hours): left as a row for the tuning run, not changed here.
5. **The household and the village are founded at the move**, not at the round. The other way, a household founded at the round
   whose members lodge at their old homes, makes a household two places, which the hard rule forbids (a farmstead is one place) and
   every reader of `hh.members` would have to learn. Until the move the party's adults are members of their old households, working
   away: the mother farm feeds the clearing, as a *torp* was cleared.
6. **The trail is always blazed where wood lies between.** A weighing was worked out and dropped: a blazed tile of broadleaf saves
   0.3 hours a walk (4 / 8 against 1.59 / 8) and costs 3, so it pays after 10 walks whatever the length; a settling makes about 12
   (the workers' walks for rations, the move and its loads). The rule would have said yes nearly always.
7. **Sleeping away is a bed**, section 4. A journey becomes a stay with no fixed bed; nothing else is special.
8. **Rations away are eaten uncooked**, in a booth too: one rule with the journey's, and the third more grain is what being away
   costs in food. A hearth and a pot in a booth are left to the outlying booth's plan (VB-790).

State: `st.away[]` (owner `sim/away`, new: the journeys leave `sim/agents`, which VB-422 was to split by this concern), `agent.away`
as its index, `st.booths[]` (owner `sim/building`). Three things read it: the walk home and a job's reach (the bed), the household's
meal (who is away), the day's end (where to lie down). Cost in the design's table. The numbers are tunables with a meaning:
`AWAY.pack` 12 days of rations of the 30 a back carries, the rest of the load being tools and seed; `AWAY.walk` 4 hours there and
back, a third of the working day, over which a pawn lodges; `BOOTH.hours` 12, `BOOTH.wood` 2 loads, `BOOTH.sleeps` 4.

## 5. The steps, with their numbers

In `design.md`, Settling in steps. The arithmetic behind its hours:

- Trail: 3 hours a wooded tile (VB-447's proposal, kept). A route of 6 hours through pine on a path (1.36) is 35 tiles: 105 hours at
  most. Two workers blaze 8 tiles a day.
- Site on forest: booth 12, house tile 16, field 46, cottage 120: 194 hours. On meadow: 12, 10 to 30, 40 to 60, 120: 182 to 222, and
  the trips for 18 loads of timber. Two workers at about 20 hours a day: ten days of the year's 48, with the walks and the seasons
  between; the round is on autumn day 11, so the felling falls in winter, the field and the walls in spring.
- Food: a worker's pack is 12 rations, 16 of grain uncooked; ten days at the site for two is 20 rations, 27 of grain, 7 more than
  the same two would have eaten at home. At the move, to the harvest and 6 days: from mid spring about 22 days where the round's 42
  stood, the other 20 having been eaten at home meanwhile: the mother farm gives about what it gave, later and over time.
- The home loses the workers' hands for a season, the spring's sowing among it: reported by VB-787 (hours of the homes' own jobs).
