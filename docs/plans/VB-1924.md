# VB-1924: how a town grows before the ship, the plan

A design pass, in an orchestrated run with nobody to ask (28 November 2026). Kim decided on 27 November that towns grow on the
map from the villages before any ship comes, and that the ship visits an existing harbour of an existing town (`design.md`,
section 17, Towns before the ship). This plan says what gathers makers before the ship, how large a town is, and what its harbour
is, writes them into `design.md` as **Proposed**, and rewrites the town and ship tasks to them. Every number is a starting value or
arithmetic; the toy models' are good to a factor of two (`docs/cities/mock-town-emergence.md`).

## 1. In brief

- **What gathers: bulk goes by water, and bulk is dealt where the boat lies.** A boat-load (150 rations in the small boat, 900 in
  the large one) cannot be carried inland at a back's 30, so it is dealt within a short carry of the landing it put in at, on the
  day it came, which nobody ashore knew ahead. A maker who lives at that landing serves every boat that comes; a maker an hour
  inland serves none of them. That is the one thing the three mocks found that gathers makers: demand at a point that only those
  who live there can serve (part three, finding 38). Before the ship it comes from the island's own boats: the archipelago's
  exports (fish, butter, firewood, hay; section 17) and the dealing of every village whose makers lie across water.
- **Where: the landing most boats reach in a day**, by the table by boat (slice 10, VB-813), from geography and never by a quota.
  One landing that leads the archipelago makes one town; two that each lead a part of it make two.
- **What holds people there besides makers:** the boat-borne visitor who cannot row home the same day sleeps there and gives for
  his night (lodging, the standing alehouse's beginning), and a household with a boat at the landing carries another's load for a
  share (the boatman). Both are deals between households, on the fair's barter.
- **The size:** before the ship a town of about 150 (some 32 households) at the leading landing; with the ship's custom, which takes
  only of those who live at its harbour, the harbour town grows to about 350 (some 75 households). An island whose archipelago has
  two leading landings ends with two of about 150. Tiles, houses and workshops are in section 4.
- **The harbour the ship calls at:** a landing the ship can lie at, chosen by the master's own estimate, first by the households he
  sees within a short walk of it and then by the worth he dealt there; no rule reads the label. Section 5 says what the harbour does
  for the first ship and what VB-188 and VB-636 take from it.
- **"Town" gets its cut point**, as a label for the eye that no rule reads: 15 households or more at a place and at least half of its
  people's work not farming, at 3 year ends running; dropped under 10 or under 40% (VB-771).

## 2. Tried against the mocks' finding

The mocks found that makers spread (the fullest village holds 10 to 22% of an island's maker households) because every buyer can
walk to a maker and every maker can walk to a gathering: custom is a district's, and the second potter at a place halves the
first one's buyers (part two, finding 15). Each candidate the third mock tried left that alone: a facility (12 to 22%), an elite
household wanting 5 to 20 farms' goods (15%), a ship's buyer on known days whom anyone within half a day can walk to (12 to 17%).
What gathered was a buyer who deals only with those who live at its point: 2,000 rations' worth a year at 130 households gave 26% at
the harbour, 4,000 gave 41%, and all three candidates at one harbour, dealing at home, 36% at year 60 (findings 35 and 38).

Each mechanism of this plan is read against that test:

| Mechanism | A point, or a district? | Of what order, at about 1,000 living (220 households) | Reading |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| A boat-load dealt at its landing | A point: the load lies at the boat, and the day is the weather's | The households of villages with a landing (10 to 20 of 22 to 30 villages, VB-190) whose nearest makers of a craft lie across water or more than 3 hours away on foot: by the VB-190 count about a third to a half of the island's households. The island's farms give its makers about 3,060 rations a year at 130 households (part three, finding 36), so about 5,200 at 220; a third to a half of that is 1,700 to 2,600 a year at the leading landing, if one landing takes most of it | Between the mock's 2,000 (26%) and its 4,000 (41%): a quarter to a third of the island's makers at the landing, by arithmetic on a toy's figures |
| The exports dealt there | A point, for the same reason: the buyer of a load of fish is whoever is at the landing | The landing's own households who let fields go buy food there; the loop is the town's (section 3) | Raises the landing's custom, not the country's |

The boat's walk-on is what differs from finding 36's ship: that buyer could be walked to, carrying cloth for two hours three days a
year; a boat-load has to be carried to a door from where it lies, in 5 to 30 back-loads, so the doors at the landing win by the
carrying hours alone, by the head's own reckoning of the walk to a door (VB-770), and no rule favours them. That is why the plan
expects this to gather where the ship on known days did not; it is a reading of the toy's lever, not a measurement, and the first
run measures it (VB-839's report; the reopening test of `design.md` read in the game). If the leading landing holds under a fifth
of the island's makers at year 60 of a populous medium run with boats, the lever is the carrying cost (the back's 30 against the
boat's load), which a tuning run may weigh; the mechanism stands as a toy's reading until then.

What stops the town, as in the mocks: custom (the makers of one craft at a place share one custom, so a place's makers are bound by
the boats that come), land (fields for those who keep them lie further out as the place fills), and crowding: a house's members' hazard
rises with the people within 2 steps (`CROWD`), so a dense place pays about 1.5 times the farms' deaths, which the growth budget's
"town" line already counts. Nothing locks in by a rule: a landing that the boats stop reaching (a new landing nearer most villages)
loses its makers by the same estimates that brought them.

## 3. The loop, and its brakes

The reinforcing loop: makers at a landing draw boat-loads; the households of the landing who earn by craft, lodging and carrying let
fields go, by their own estimate of their food (Towns and regions, A craftsman's food), and buy food from the boats; the boats come
for that custom and deal the makers' goods home. Its lag is years (a field let go after the craft's poor years stop frightening a
head; a landless maker's plot asked once a year, VB-772). Its brakes: custom shared among the makers of a craft (immediate), the walk
to fields (decades, as the place fills), crowding's deaths (a generation), and the ice and the freeze-up, when no boat comes and the
landing's households eat from their own store (a season each year, VB-190's winter dates).

## 4. The size

Kim's numbers (`design.md`, section 17; World and scale, the sizing table): a main scenario of about 1,000 pawns that starts with 1
or 2 villages and ends with many villages and one town of about 350, or two of about 150. This plan reads them as the end state,
and places them in time: before the ship the leading landing grows to a town of about 150; the ship's custom, taking only of those who
live at its harbour, carries the harbour town to about 350 (part three, finding 38: a point buyer of 4,000 a year gave 41% of the
makers at 130 households). Where two landings each lead a part of the archipelago, both stay near 150. The counts:

| | A town of about 150 | A town of about 350 |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Households, at 4.7 people (servants and lodgers counted in their house) | 32 | 75 |
| Farms of the old village, holding 2 to 4 fields | 10 | 15 |
| Maker households, part-farming (a third of the island's 20 to 25 before the ship; of 40 to 60 with the ship) | 8 | 20 |
| Boat and harbour households (carrying, lodging, the alehouse) | 4 | 10 |
| Fishing households, selling at the landing | 5 | 15 |
| Cottagers living by hired days and service | 5 | 15 |
| Houses, a tile each | 32 | 75 |
| Workshops, a tile each (a maker's facility beside its house: a smithy, a kiln, a loom house; the mill where a river is) | 8 | 20 |
| Yards and storehouses | 12 | 30 |
| The green, the lanes and the landing | 10 | 20 |
| **Built tiles** | **about 60** | **about 145** |
| Field tiles held from the place, at the land's reading | about 40 | about 80 |

The density is set by crowding, not by a layout rule: a hazard factor of about 1.5 (the growth budget's "about 1.5 times") is about
50 people within 2 steps, 19 tiles, or one house in two tiles over the core; the 145 built tiles hold 75 houses at that density
(19 x 75/145 x 4.7 = 46 people within 2 steps, a factor of 1.35, and the densest part up to the cap). Plots are grown as village
houses are (Towns and regions, Plots): no street is laid and the counts are what the place grows to, never targets.

## 5. The harbour

- **Which place the ship calls at, and why.** A ship lies only where it can: a landing on the sea, reached from the map's open water
  by the ship's own water (VB-188 sets the ship's draught; slice 10's "wider water"). Among those, the master's estimate, as a
  household's estimate of a green (Towns and regions): on the first voyage the landing with the most households within an hour's
  walk of it (what is seen from the water), and after it the worth he dealt there, blended 0.7 old to 0.3 new a voyage. So the
  ship calls where the island's own boats made a town, since that is where the houses are, and it never reads the label.
- **What the harbour does for the first ship.** *The landing:* the ship lies off the landing tile and the town's boats carry its
  goods and people ashore, work for the boat households (carrying for hire, below). *The market:* the ship deals only with those
  who live at its harbour (households within an hour's walk of the landing), on the days it lies there, which nobody knows ahead:
  the point demand of finding 38, chosen over the district the third mock warned of. So the ship grows the town it found, and a
  village sells to the ship through the town's households (section 17, Off-map trade goes through a town). *The granted market:*
  the Crown's grant (VB-188) is given to the place whose landing the ship calls at, as an act of the Crown's agent with a record;
  what it grants (a peace on set days, a toll, a day added) is VB-188's, and it is the first fact about a town a rule may read,
  since it is a grant held and not a label.
- **The landing for settling families (VB-636).** A family lands at the harbour with what it brought. It takes, in order, a place
  in the town itself if a plot or a house there is offered to it (a lodging with a household short of hands, the town's service),
  else a village within a day's walk of the harbour with a plot site or an empty farm whose customs let an outsider claim it, walked
  to, or rowed to by a boat household of the town for its share when the village lies across water (a leg by water, VB-812). Where
  none takes it, it does not land.
- **The harbour questions VB-189 handed to the ship.** (1) The granted market: above. (2) Outside demand: a point, by this plan;
  VB-188 sizes it (worth a year by decade). (3) The cut point for "town": given here, before the ship (section 6). (4) 600 / 325 /
  75: the town before the ship adds its boat, lodging and makers' hours, about 35 a 1,000 of the work line (a place of 15% of the
  people with half its work not farming, against an island's 27%), to near 300; the 325 still waits for the ship's custom and slice
  5's elite, and the target stays Kim's. (5) Disease: crowding stands in for it, by household; the ship's sickness lands at the
  harbour first and the disease model reads crowding, never a town flag. (6) Town against country: a custom the Crown presses once a
  grant exists; the standing alehouse is the lodging household that hosts most (no label brings it).

## 6. The label "town"

A town is still a label read from what people do, and no rule reads it (Towns and regions, A market place and a town are labels).
What sets it apart now that something makes a town: the count of households at the place and the share of its people's work that
is not farming, both of which a village does not reach (a village of 5 to 15 farms; at the fullest place of the mock 267 to 396 a
1,000 of work not farming). **Town:** 15 households or more and at least half of its work not farming (VB-765's work line) at 3
year ends running; dropped under 10 households or under 40%, at 3 year ends running. A place is a village and its houses, as today;
the numbers are Proposed and read off this plan's arithmetic, waiting for the first real run (VB-771 prints the histogram).

## 7. Cost, named before building

At about 1,000 living on medium. *Boat-borne dealing:* households of villages with a landing, some 100, times 2 to 6 trips a year,
so about 400 boat journeys a year, each a journey as the walk to a door is (VB-770) with one leg by water (VB-812): constant work a
leg, over 40 game days a year of open water about 10 a day. *Lodging:* a night's host found among the houses within 2 steps of the
landing, at most about 20 looked at, a few hundred times a year. *Carrying for hire:* one offer a load that waits for a boat, at the
head's planning day, a few hundred a year. *The label:* yearly, outside the step. The two build tasks claim 0.2 ms a game day of the
budget, a year's mean, within slice 9's 1 ms; the town's wrap-up (VB-774) measures it on `medium-ref` and `small-y40`.

## 8. The tasks

New (noted for the orchestrator, VB-1984 and VB-1985):

- **VB-1984, A boat-load is dealt at its landing** (town, M; waits for VB-770, VB-812, VB-813). A head whose door partner or green
  is reached cheaper by boat goes by boat with a load; the load is dealt at doors within a short carry of the landing, each door's
  cost read with the carrying of the load from the boat (back-loads of 30, VB-770's weighing, no new weight); a household learns the
  households it saw dealing at a landing as it learns those of a green; the exports (fish, butter, firewood, hay) carried by the
  same reckoning. Reported: boat journeys a year, worth dealt at each landing, the leading landing's share of maker households
  (VB-839).
- **VB-1985, A night's lodging and a load carried for hire at a landing** (town, M; waits for VB-1984, VB-814). A visitor from
  beyond a day there and back sleeps at a house of the landing (the hosting of `sim/hosting`), and gives a ration's worth a night,
  in kind, as a swap; a head whose loads wait for a boat offers a share (a fifth of the load) to a household with a boat at its
  landing to carry it, an offer answered by that head's estimate of its hours.

Rewritten: VB-771 (the label: "town" gets its cut point), VB-839 (the report: the leading landing's share and the reopening test,
now an expectation and not an exception), VB-774 (the wrap-up measures the town: households, built tiles, crowding, the boats'
share), VB-636 (the landing: the town's harbour, the order of section 5), VB-188 (ready: the harbour, the point demand, the master's
estimate and the six handed items above). The landless maker's plot (VB-772) needs no change: its estimate of custom at a place
counts the households it dealt with at a landing as any.

## 9. Decided by the principles here, and surfaced

- **The mechanism is bulk by water, and not the elite's seat or the gathering.** The elite's dues brought to a seat would also be a
  point (finding 35: 25% only at 80 farms' goods dealt at home, outside slice 5's 5 to 20), but it waits on an elite that the roads
  produce late, and dues in kind are only Proposed (section 17); the gathering is a district's three days. Bulk by water rests on
  what is planned (boats, the walk to a door, the exports) and adds no rule that favours a place (principle: an elite and a town are
  what the rules leave over, not a quota). A close call: noted.
- **The 350 is the end state, with the ship; 150 before it.** Kim's numbers are an end state of the scenario; the mock gives a point
  buyer from outside as what reaches a third of the makers, so 350 before the ship would need demand the island's own boats do not
  have (section 2). A close call: noted.
- **"Town" gets a cut point.** The Decided rule was no cut point until something makes one; Kim's decision of 27 November is that
  something does, and the cut is a label no rule reads, so it moves no mechanism.
- **The ship deals only at its harbour.** Finding 38 makes this the choice that grows the harbour town; the third mock's other option
  (a buyer anyone within half a day can walk to) would raise the country's makers and leave the harbour as it was.
- **Not done here:** a mock of bulk by water. The toy of part two has no water, and the plan reads its lever by arithmetic; the
  first run with boats measures it (VB-839).
