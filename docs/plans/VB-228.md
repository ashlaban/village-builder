## 2. The line: a verb lives where its act is carried out

The rule, from target-v2 3.2: a verb's body lives with the owner of the act it wraps, and registers there at load. Where a verb's
`run` calls several modules, its home is the highest of them in load order, so that every import of the body points downward. That
second half is what decides the unclear cases, and it is the reason the table differs from the task's examples in two places
(`give`, `marry`).

## 3. What stays in sim/commands

The dispatcher, and nothing of any domain:

- `register(verb, {check, run})`: at load only (`Rules.atLoad()`, as `sim/events` does), a duplicate throws; sealed with the rules
  registry at the first seed or load.
- `issue(st, cmd)`: unchanged.
- `VERBS`: the table `issue` reads, still exported for `sim/opportunities` (the check on a view) and three tests.
- The three guards every domain's checks share, which are pure reads of `st` and belong to no one domain: `living`, `home`, and
  `free` (5.4).

About 60 lines, 6 exports. It moves up in `src/manifest.json` to load after `sim/registry` and before every module that registers.
The modules that today wrap `issue` in a lazy lookup because commands load after them (`sim/relations`, `sim/exchange`,
`sim/farmclaims`, `sim/help`, `sim/expansion`, `sim/away`) import it at load like the rest.

## 4. The player: a module of its own, `sim/player`

The note of 15 November asks whether the player's queue stays. It cannot: it imports `sim/agents` (`release`), `sim/wishes`,
`sim/relations` and `sim/records`, and the criterion is that `sim/commands` imports no domain. It is also a different concern by
target-v2 4.7: the player is a source of commands, as the planner and the round are; none of the others lives in the dispatcher.

`sim/player` (new, about 200 lines, `@sys` and `@state st.player` move with it; owner of `st.player` in `src/owners.json`): `inhabit`,
`uninhabit`, `queue`, `unqueue`, `setAuto`, `wish`, `clearMatchWish`, `answer`, `head`, `playerStep`, `playerDied`, `goOnAs`,
`commanded`, `unplanned`, `QUEUE_MAX`, `TIES_MAX`, with `LASTS`, `HEAD_VERBS`, `homeAtDark`. Sixteen exports: one over the width
line, so it enters `tests/width-baseline.json` at 16 as `sim/commands` leaves it (the count moves, it does not grow).

Its place in load order is the one real knot, 5.5.

## 5. Choices

Each has a proposal; the first two differ from the task's text, so they are Kim's to confirm.

1. **`give` and `deliver` go to `sim/away`, not `sim/exchange`.** The criterion names `sim/exchange` for `give`. But `give`'s check
   asks `sim/away` who can haul or fetch, and `deliver`'s run calls `sim/away` three times and `settleGift` once. `sim/exchange`
   loads before `sim/away`, so homing them there keeps an upward lookup, which is what this task removes. The alternative is to
   move `haulerOf` and `fetcherOf` down, which is not a verbatim move. Proposed: `sim/away`.
2. **`raiseBooth` goes to `sim/away`, not `sim/building`.** Its check is `free`, and `sim/building` loads before `sim/agents`; a booth
   is raised to lodge in, and `sim/away` already reads the booth. With 5.4's answer (b) it could stay in `sim/building`.
3. **The customs' verbs (`decide`, `argue`, `dropCause`, `rally`) go to `sim/society`.** `culture` owns the fields (`practice`,
   `pawn.advocate`) but loads before the kernel and holds no `st` logic of this kind; `decide` writes `practice` directly today, with
   no act of `culture` to wrap. `sim/society` issues `decide`. VB-231 (the customs' door) is the task that gives `culture` its write
   act; this plan moves the bodies as they are and notes the direct write for it.
4. **`free` needs `travelling`, which is `sim/agents`'.** Three ways: (a) `travelling`, a one-line read of an agent's phase, moves
   to `sim/commands` beside `free`, and `sim/agents` imports it from there; (b) `free` lives in `sim/agents` and `raiseBooth` in
   `sim/away` (5.2), so that every verb using `free` loads after it; (c) the guards get a small module of their own. Proposed: (b):
   it adds nothing to the kernel that knows what a journey is, and no module.
5. **Where `sim/player` loads.** `playerStep` needs `release` (`sim/agents`), and `haltForTalk`'s check, in `sim/agents`, needs
   `commanded`. Three ways: (a) `commanded` and `unplanned`, two pure reads of `st.player`, stay in `sim/commands`, and
   `sim/player` loads after `sim/agents`; (b) `sim/player` loads before `sim/agents`, and the part that drives the pawn
   (`playerStep`, `LASTS`, `homeAtDark`, 50 lines) goes to `sim/agents`, which is already over its line and waits for its own
   split (VB-422); (c) `haltForTalk`'s line on the player's command moves out of the verb to its one issuer, which changes no
   outcome but is not verbatim. Proposed: (a), noted as a wart: the kernel then reads `st.player`, which it does not own.

## 6. What the split shows about the 700-line rule

- **The limit did not find this split; the edges did.** The module was over 700 lines because it held 43 bodies that belong to 18
  modules. The evidence was 74 upward lookups, not 922 lines: at 500 lines it would have been as wrong. For a dispatcher, a
  registry or a facade the useful measure is fan-out (modules imported or looked up), and a rule on that would have fired months
  earlier.
- **Where the limit would force a cut with no concern behind it:** the receiving modules. The move adds about 45 lines to
  `sim/agents` (1,202), 150 to `sim/plans` (1,079), 110 to `sim/away` (674) and 55 to `sim/heirs` (645), for a reason the rule itself
  asks for (code goes where its concern lives). The first two have split tasks open (VB-422, VB-431). `sim/away` crosses the line
  by this move alone, and `sim/heirs` reaches it: by the rule this task opens a split task for each and goes on. That is the rule
  working as written, but it shows what it measures: `sim/away` does not become two concerns by receiving the verbs of the one it
  has. Proposed for the revision: the line asks for a split task only when a module passes it by new code, not by a verbatim move
  of code home; or the line is replaced by a measure of concerns (sections, fan-out).
- **Exports:** registering a verb exports nothing, so the receivers' widths do not grow, except `sim/plans` (`heads`, for
  `sim/aims`; 34 today) and `sim/agents` (`free`, under 5.4 b; 31 today), both in the width baseline and both with split tasks open.

## 7. Order of work

Small commits, each with `npm test` and `node tools/same.js --base cf270f9`:

1. `register` and the seal in `sim/commands`; the existing table registered through it in place. No move yet.
2. `sim/player` out, verbatim; owners, saves, the facade and the three importers follow.
3. The verbs home, one receiving module a commit, in the order of section 2's table from the bottom (latest loaders first, so that
   each step still loads).
4. `sim/commands` up the manifest; the lazy `issue` wrappers and `later()` go; `tests/dependencies.js` drops the exemption;
   `tests/commands.js` reads the verbs from their modules.
5. `tools/writes.js` into `ownership.md` section 4; the baselines; the notes.

Cost: none expected. `issue` does the same lookup in the same table; registration is at load. Counters are reported unchanged.

## 8. Kim's review (18 November 2026)

Decided in the session; where this section and the ones above differ, this one holds.

1. **The guards are lookups on what they read, not the dispatcher's** (5.4). `free` goes to `sim/agents`, beside `travelling`;
   `living` and `home`, lookups on a pawn and a household, go to `sim/records`, the register (they cannot go to `sim/agents`:
   `sim/offers` uses `living` and loads before it). `sim/commands` holds no guard: `register`, `issue` and the table.
   `sim/records`' length baseline rises by the lines moved (Kim: yes); its split is VB-455.
2. **`raiseBooth` registers in `sim/away`** for now (5.2). A booth is a temporary shelter raised by a pawn for a pawn or a group,
   and should be a project: VB-1172, which also drops `free` from its check and puts the verb where the plan lives.
3. **The talk's halt no longer asks whether the pawn is on the player's command** (5.5). The line treated the player's pawn
   differently from every other (principle 16), so it is cut, the one change of behaviour in this task, in a commit of its own: a
   played pawn standing idle outdoors with commands queued is halted by a talk like anyone. Runs without a player are unchanged
   (`tools/same.js`). If the need returns, every pawn gains the means to decline or leave a talk. So `sim/player` loads after
   `sim/agents` and imports it; `commanded` and `unplanned` go with it; no `guard()` is added to `sim/commands`.
4. **The customs' verbs register in `sim/society`** (5.3), the wiring between `culture`, which does not take `st`, and the state.
   `decide`'s direct write of `practice` is noted on VB-231.
5. **`give` and `deliver`** (5.1): proposed after the review, not yet confirmed: a small module `sim/gifts`, loaded after
   `sim/offers`, `sim/exchange` and `sim/away`, for how a gift gets from here to there (the two verbs, `giftOffer`, `fetched`,
   `hauled`, `fetchWhy`); a follow-up moves `carryGift`, `haulGift` and `fetchGift` into it out of `sim/away`. The reason: settling
   needs the carrying and the carrying books through `sim/exchange` on arrival, so neither of the two can hold the verbs without
   an upward call.
6. **A talk does not cut a walk on an errand** (Kim, 18 November, after 3: "the pawn should continue what it was doing"). A talk
   never interrupted work; it stops only a pawn outdoors with no task, and such a pawn's walk is a stroll towards company
   (`sim/jobs` `idleAbout`), so the talk is where it was going. The played pawn's queued walk was caught because state could not
   tell the two apart. Now it can: `agent.errand`, true from the verb `walkTo` to a place (not `near`) until the pawn's next walk
   or its release; `haltForTalk` leaves such a walk alone, for any pawn. A walk let go of (`release` on the way to a task) is still
   cut, as before: marking strolls instead changed unplayed runs, since three such walks met a talk in 200 days at founders seed 7.
   Unplayed runs: `same` against cf270f9. `commanded` and `unplanned` went to `sim/records`, not `sim/player`: `sim/occasions`
   (social layer) reads `unplanned`, and the layers' baseline takes no new upward call; they are lookups on a pawn, as `living` is.
