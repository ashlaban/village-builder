## 2. The line: a kind lives where its work is carried out

The rule, as VB-228's for verbs: a domain module declares its kinds as `KINDS` (or `KIND`), beside the rules their `done`, `deliver`
and `available` call; `sim/kinds` joins them, and keeps the registry and the asking order. Where a record calls several modules,
its home is the one whose state its work changes; if that module loads before another the record needs, see 5.2.

It stays a pull, not VB-228's push (`register` at load): `sim/kinds` loads after the domains and reads their `KINDS`, as it does for
the 20. A push would move the registry before the domains and `ASK` elsewhere, for no line saved here (5.3).

## 5. Choices for Kim, and close calls

1. **The field kinds get a module of their own, `sim/fieldwork`** (the task's note of 15 November foresaw it). `sim/fields` is
   767 lines; with its ten kinds it would be about 1,000. There is a concern behind the cut, not only the length: `sim/fields` is
   the field's state and its year (stages, soil, rotation), `sim/fieldwork` the work a household does on it; and `haul` and `clear`
   call `sim/commons`, which imports `sim/fields`, so they cannot load inside it. The other way: into `sim/fields`, with the two
   calls looked up late, and VB-434 cuts it afterwards.
2. **Six kinds have no domain module: `sim/chores`.** `wood` changes the woodpile (`sim/stores`) by the plan's targets and calls
   `sim/forest` and `sim/carts`; `cook` needs `sim/pot` and `sim/hunger`, which imports `sim/pot`; `gather` needs `sim/forage`, which
   loads before `sim/pot`; `water` and `mend` write a field no module owns (`waterDay`, a stretch's `cond`); `brew` waits for the ale
   module of VB-416. The rule of section 2 would scatter them by load order (`cook` in `sim/hunger`, `wood` in `sim/carts`), each in a
   module that is not its concern. Proposed: one module for the household's daily work at the house, named for what it is; `brew`
   leaves it with VB-416. The other way: they stay in `sim/kinds` (then about 310 lines, and the rule has six exceptions).
3. **Pull or push.** Proposed: pull, as now. A push (`declare(kind, record)` at load, as verbs) would be the same pattern as VB-228,
   but the roles' ranks, the fetch kinds and `ASK` need every kind first, so the registry would need a sealing step it has no use for
   today. A close call another agent could make the other way.
4. **`sim/kindrules`** is the price of the pull: nine one-liners and `landProject` that fields, meadows, plots and three foragers
   share. The other homes considered: `sim/layout` (the rows) and `sim/hunger` (spare food), both over their width baseline.
   The name is open.
5. **`clear`, `break` and `meadow`** are stages of a land project: `clear` and `break` go with the field kinds, `meadow` with the
   meadow's, and what they share (`landProject`, `onProject`, `workStake`) to `sim/kindrules`. The other way: the three together.

## 6. What the split shows about the 700-line rule

- The limit did not cause this split and would not have found it: 29 records sat in `sim/kinds` because the old cap of 400 pushed
  kinds out one at a time, by date, not by concern. What finds the misplacement is the width measure: 35 names exported to one reader.
- Load order decided more homes than length did (5.2, 5.4, the three changed lines): a rule that says "where its concern lives"
  needs a second half for a concern that sits between modules.
- The limit forced one cut, `sim/fieldwork`, and there it helped: the cut has a concern behind it (5.1).
- It will ask for nothing more of `sim/kinds`; `sim/byre` comes within 100 lines of it by receiving its own kinds, which is the
  rule working against a module for being complete. A length that counted records (data) apart from rules would not.
