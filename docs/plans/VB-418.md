## 2. The line: a kind lives where its work is carried out

The rule, as VB-228's for verbs: a domain module declares its kinds as `KINDS` (or `KIND`), beside the rules their `done`, `deliver`
and `available` call; `sim/kinds` joins them, and keeps the registry and the asking order. Where a record calls several modules,
its home is the one whose state its work changes; if that module loads before another the record needs, see 5.2.

It stays a pull, not VB-228's push (`register` at load): `sim/kinds` loads after the domains and reads their `KINDS`, as it does for
the 20. A push would move the registry before the domains and `ASK` elsewhere, for no line saved here (5.3).

## 5. The choices, motivated (revised after Kim's questions, 18 November)

Three things decide a kind's home, in this order. They are target-v2's principles 1 to 3, and each has a check in the repository:

- **Principle 1, domains own truth and the rules about it; processes coordinate.** A kind's record is a rule about a domain's state
  (what finishing the work does, where it can be done). `sim/kinds` belongs to the process that matches hands to work (target-v2's
  `work`), so it should hold no domain's rules. Target-v2 3.1 says where they go: "`herds`, `meadows`, `byre` and `fields` as domain
  steps and job kinds in `production`".
- **Principle 2, one writer per field.** A record's `done` must change state through its owner. Where the record sits in the owner,
  the functions it calls stop being exports (the width measure falls).
- **Principle 3, layers: a module calls its own layer and those below** (`src/layers.json`; `tests/layers-baseline.json` holds the
  13 upward calls left, a count that only falls). A record can live only in a module at or above the layer of everything it calls.

1. **The field kinds: `sim/fieldwork`, in the material layer beside `sim/fields`.** By principle 1 they belong to the fields domain:
   every `done` is one of `sim/fields`' transitions (`hoed`, `sown`, `reaped`, `binned`), and `sim/fields` stays the one writer of a
   field (principle 2). One module or two is then only a question of length, which the task's note of 15 November settled (over 700
   lines: a module of their own beside it); the cut follows target-v2's own wording, the domain's steps (`sim/fields`) and its job
   kinds (`sim/fieldwork`). Principle 3 finds a defect on the way: `haul`, `clear` and `break` each branch on `j.common` and call
   `sim/commons`, a social module, which a material module may not call. The common field's rule belongs to its owner: `sim/commons`
   wraps those three records (`common(K)`), as `sim/lying` already builds a fetch kind from each carrying kind, and `sim/kinds`
   applies it. Behaviour is the same (`tools/same.js`); it is the one place where a record does not move verbatim.
2. **The kinds with no domain module: `sim/chores`, in the material layer, for five of them; `mend` goes to `sim/building`.**
   Principle 3 rules out the resource's own module: `wood` fells in `sim/forest` and `gather` picks in `sim/forage`, both in the
   kernel layer (the world), and both put what they bring into the household's stores, a layer above; the forest may not call the
   stores. Principle 1 says what they are: human work that turns something of the world into something in the house, which is
   target-v2's `production` (4.3: "human transformation only"; cooking and brewing are its recipes). No module of today is that, so
   one is begun, small: `wood`, `water`, `cook`, `brew`, `gather`. `cook` cannot sit in `sim/pot` though the pot is its: it asks
   `sim/hunger` for the day's need, and `sim/hunger` imports `sim/pot`. `mend` and `repair` are upkeep of built things (target-v2's
   facilities: "wear on a facility ... shows as an opportunity to mend"), and today's module for built things is `sim/building`.
   Not chosen: leaving them in `sim/kinds` (the process keeps five domains' rules, against principle 1) or scattering them by load
   order (`wood` in `sim/carts`: no principle behind it).
3. **Pull, not push.** The context: VB-228 made each domain *register* its verbs (a push) because `sim/commands` is in the kernel,
   the lowest layer, and was importing 16 domains above it, against principle 3. `sim/kinds` is in the agency layer, the highest
   that acts; when it reads `KINDS` from a domain it calls downward, which is what principle 3 asks. So there is no violation for a
   push to remove, and principle 18 (registries are code, built at load) holds either way. The 20 kinds already outside
   `sim/kinds` are read this way.
4. **`sim/kindrules`** (a close call): what kinds of several domains share must sit below those domains. By principle 1 each
   helper has an owner (the rows: `sim/layout`; spare food: `sim/hunger`; a stake renewed: `sim/claims`), but all three are at or
   over their width baseline and may not gain an export. So they go to one small material module, and VB-423 (`sim/hunger`) and the
   other width tasks may take them home. The name is open.

## 6. What the split shows about the 700-line rule

- The limit did not cause this split and would not have found it: 29 records sat in `sim/kinds` because the old cap of 400 pushed
  kinds out one at a time, by date, not by concern. What finds the misplacement is the width measure: 35 names exported to one reader.
- Load order decided more homes than length did (5.2, 5.4, the three changed lines): a rule that says "where its concern lives"
  needs a second half for a concern that sits between modules.
- The limit forced one cut, `sim/fieldwork`, and there it helped: the cut has a concern behind it (5.1).
- It will ask for nothing more of `sim/kinds`; `sim/byre` comes within 100 lines of it by receiving its own kinds, which is the
  rule working against a module for being complete. A length that counted records (data) apart from rules would not.
- Found while building: the width measure leaves out an export read only by a registry. Twelve of `sim/fields`' transitions were hidden
  that way; when their reader became `sim/fieldwork`, the count rose from 23 to 38 with nothing new exported. A measure that changes
  when a reader moves, not when an export is added, is a weak guide to where a domain should be cut.

## 7. As built (18 November)

Kim approved the plan with one change: no `sim/kindrules`; each shared helper goes where it belongs, size ignored. So the rows went
to `sim/fields`, `onProject` and `landProject` to `sim/projects`, `workStake` to `sim/claims` (over its width baseline: VB-1176) and
spare food to `sim/hunger`. Two things the plan did not foresee: the order of `KINDS` is part of saved state (the due-queue holds an
index into the windowed kinds), so `sim/kinds` names every kind in its old position, one line each; and `mend` and `repair` are a
second table of `sim/building` (`UPKEEP`) to keep that order. `sim/kinds` ended at 150 lines and 3 exports. The task file has the rest.
