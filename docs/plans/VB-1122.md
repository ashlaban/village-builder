# VB-1122: the retired holder's life, and handing on by choice

Plan of 19 November 2026. Design refs: `design.md`, Inheritance (Succession; Handover and shares; The retired, new); The player and
what they see. Built on VB-824 (succession offered and taken up, done 18 November) and beside VB-1121 (the player is the line).

## 1. Where it stands

- A farm passes in life by the holder's offer `handover` (VB-690's basis), made by an unplayed holder at the custom's time (the heir's
  wedding into the house; the holder at 60) unless its opinion of the heir is at `SOCIAL.enemy` or below; a played holder offers by the
  verb `offer` when it chooses (`src/sim/marriage.js`, `handover`). The old holder and its spouse become `retired` (`src/sim/heirs.js`).
- The retired eat at the household's table, work by the role `retired`'s weights (`src/sim/rules.js`: workday 8, herding 4, household 3,
  husbandry 3, foraging 3; no field work beyond threshing and tending), do not count as hands, are barred from the round's matching and
  from service, and may take up a cause (`argue`, any living pawn). Under "Who speaks at the meeting: landholders" (the default) they have
  no voice. Nothing they own is apart from the household's stores.
- The playtest of 16 November: 14 years retired with nothing to decide.
- Kim, 18 November: no automatic handing over; the elder chooses, the player for its pawn as every holder for itself; the custom's time
  is what the village expects; holding out costs the heir's opinion; handing on does not change whom the player plays.

## 2. Grounding (principle 5)

The Nordic retirement contract (*undantag*, *sytning*; *födoråd* in Swedish law, *kår* in Norway, *aftægt* in Denmark) was a written or
witnessed contract between the old holder and the new one: a room or a cottage (*undantagsstuga*), yearly grain, firewood, a cow's milk or
a cow of its own fed with the farm's, sometimes a strip of land, and the right to be cared for when sick. Many held until death; the
contract was made when the heir married or the holder could no longer work the farm. Disputes over a keep not given were common at the
district court (*häradsting*), and the old could reclaim the farm where the contract was broken. The old kept a say in the house,
counselled the young holder, and had a voice in their grandchildren's matches where the family arranged them.

## 3. The retired, for every pawn (principle 16)

Each is an existing verb opened to the retired, or one new basis of `offer`, with its check. Nothing here is the player's alone.

## 4. The player as a retired pawn

Against the playtest's 14 years (Ingeborg, retired at 60, died at 74):

| When | Decision | Every year? |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Spring, the head's planning day | Advise the plan: a rotation, a store's target, a crop out of turn. The head's answer is shown, and why. | Yes |
| A grandchild of age, at the asking day | Wish a match for the grandchild, under the custom that gives the heads the match | When one is of age (from about year 5 of the 14) |
| Autumn meeting | Argue a cause; vote where the custom gives the retired a voice | Yes (a cause is held `causeEvery` years) |
| The keep short | Press the keep, bring the case to the meeting | When it happens |
| Any time | Give from the keep to kin: a calf, saved grain | When the keep has something to give |

So every year has at least the advice and the meeting. A year in which the player takes none of them is skipped as any year is (`skip`,
VB-244): the page offers "On to the next thing asked of you" (the next spring planning day, a grandchild's asking day, a keep pressed), and
says whose plan it is. Nothing is decided for the player while it skips: unanswered, an advice is not given, and a case not brought.

## 5. Handing on by choice

The unplayed holder's own rule replaces VB-824's "at the custom's time unless its opinion of the heir is ill". It is considered at the same
two moments (the heir's wedding into the house; the holder's birthday), so its cost is as built: one weighing a holder a year.

The holder hands on when

`w = HANDOVER.custom × due + HANDOVER.years × max(0, age − HANDOVER.from) + HANDOVER.weak × (1 − hand) + HANDOVER.wish × wish + HANDOVER.opinion × op − HANDOVER.keep × keepShare`

reaches `HANDOVER.at` (1), where `due` is 1 once the custom's time has come (what the village expects: the heir married and living there,
or the holder at 60), `hand` is 1 while the holder counts as a working hand and 0 after, `wish` is 1 when the heir is married and living in
the house, or courting with no place to marry onto (the means to marry needs a place), `op` the holder's opinion of the heir (−1 to 1), and
`keepShare` the keep's share of what the farm made last year (the keep of the holder and its spouse, in rations, over the household's
harvest and milk), which a poor farm cannot bear.

| Parameter | First value | Meaning |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `HANDOVER.custom` | 0.8 | The pull of what the village expects |
| `HANDOVER.from`, `HANDOVER.years` | 55, 0.05 | From 55, each year of age adds 0.05 (a holder of 65 is at 0.5 from age alone) |
| `HANDOVER.weak` | 0.3 | A holder who no longer counts as a hand |
| `HANDOVER.wish` | 0.3 | The heir married in the house, or waiting on a place to marry |
| `HANDOVER.opinion` | 0.4 | The holder's opinion of the heir, either way |
| `HANDOVER.keep` | 1.0 | Times the keep's share of the farm's yield |
| `HANDOVER.grudge`, `HANDOVER.grudgeMax` | 0.05 a year, 0.3 | The heir's opinion of a holder who holds out past the custom's time |

With a keep of a fifth of the farm's yield (0.2): under "when the heir marries", a holder of 52 who thinks well of its heir (0.3) hands
on at the wedding (0.8 + 0.3 + 0.12 − 0.2 = 1.02); one at odds with it (−0.5) holds until 60 (0.8 + 0.3 − 0.2 − 0.2 = 0.7; at 60, with
the years and no longer a hand, 1.25). Under "the holder at 60", a holder of 60 with an unmarried heir it thinks well of hands on at 60
(0.8 + 0.25 + 0.3 + 0.12 − 0.2 = 1.27), at odds with it at 61. Before the custom's time a holder of 50 with a married heir does not
(0.22), and an old holder whose time never comes (an heir who never marries, under "when the heir marries") hands on at about 71 from age
alone. A poor farm whose keep is half its yield holds about six years longer. Holding out past the custom's time costs the heir's opinion of the holder `HANDOVER.grudge` a year, up to `HANDOVER.grudgeMax`, derived
(never stored as a memory: Rules for cost, 3) from one field on the farm, `farmstead.expected` = `{heir, since}`, set when the custom's time
comes and the holder does not offer, and cleared at the handover or when the heir changes. The opinion's derivation reads it for a pair
whose second is a holder: one lookup.

A played holder is never weighed for: it offers when it chooses, and pays the same grudge.

The numbers before and after, by the implement task (VB-1122 named them; VB-824 measured them as built): founders small 50 years seeds 7
and 42, populous small 30 years seeds 3, 7, 11, 42: marriages and handovers a decade (by marriage, age, death), holders of 60 and over
still holding, heirs waiting married on a farm not theirs, with the median years from the custom's time to the handover.

## 6. Cost (Rules for cost, 9)

- The choice: one weighing a holder a year and one at an heir's wedding: households × 1 a year, O(1) each (the heir is VB-824's
  `heirRanking`, called there already). No new pass.
- The grudge: one field read per opinion derivation of a pair whose second holds a farm: O(1), no allocation.
- The keep: one obligation per retired pawn (a few per village); its short days counted in the hunger step's walk of members: O(1) more per
  member-day. Its case: as a pressed debt, on the meeting's agenda.
- Advice: one offer per retired pawn a year at most, answered at the head's review: retired × 1 a year.
- Matches: a retired grandparent's wish at the asking day, over its grandchildren of age in the household: retired × a few, once a year.
- State: `farmstead.expected`, an obligation of kind `keep`, a reservation of the keep's goods; all plain data, saved.

   nothing to spare has nothing to give.

## 8. Decisions (Deciding without Kim)

- **The keep is met at the table** (the hard rule: a farmstead is one place). Booking it as goods moved to the retired each day would move
  nothing anywhere; what the contract adds is a claim the retired can press.
- **Reclaiming the farm** after a keep pressed for years is the provincial laws' (principle 5); it moves no Decided point, since the
  handover is the holder's offer (Kim, 18 November) and the meeting's verdict is a case's existing outcome. A close call (`decision`).
- **Advice is an offer the head answers**, not a vote: principle 16 keeps the act the head's; the weight by opinion and standing is the
  design's (Traits, Opinion; Standing).
- **The grudge is derived, not a memory**: it follows a state (the custom's time passed, the holder holding) and ends with it.
