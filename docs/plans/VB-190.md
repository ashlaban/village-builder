## 0. In brief

- **Water is the road, and winter is the season of travel and of heavy carrying.** On the forested island of the journey scale a
  boat joins the villages that have a landing, and where it is cold enough the ice joins everyone for a few weeks a year: at four
  medium islands the pairs of villages within 12 hours go from 35 to 61 on foot to 42 to 109 by boat and 63 to 101 over the ice of a
  hard northern winter (section 2). Between the two lie the weeks of the freeze-up and the break-up, when neither a boat nor a
  foot crosses water: an island is then alone.
- **Climate is a world's latitude, chosen when the world is made,** with the map's north colder than its south. The references
  stay on the middle world, where the thaw and the frost fall where they do today, so no target moves by it.
- **The cost grid changes on at most six dates a band a winter,** known in autumn and taken from a due-queue. A table of hours is
  what people know of a normal season; only the route actually walked reads the day's ground (section 6).
- **A boat lies where it was left.** There is no search that mixes walking and rowing: a journey is a walk to the boat, a leg by
  water, and a walk on, and the table by boat is landing to landing, searched once a village. This is the hard rule, and it also
  bounds the cost.
- **Carts need a way; sledges need snow.** A hand cart keeps to paths and open ground, an ox cart to lanes, tracks and open
  ground. A sledge goes wherever snow lies and carries half again an ox cart's load, so a head keeps timber and stone for the snow.
- **Built ways follow feet.** A track, a causeway and a bridge are facilities built only along worn ways, where the counted
  crossings pay for the work in five years by the alderman's reckoning; a custom says who mends them. A way over a household's land
  needs its head's yes (Kim's decision 22).
- **Boats alone gather nobody:** the people within 3 hours of the median village do not change by boat (38 to 58), far from the
  second town mock's 160 to 320 households. Reach by water widens the day's journey and the 12-hour circle, not the village's own.
- **Twelve tasks** (section 7), none ready tonight: each waits for the journey scale's build (VB-780), the season's weather
  (VB-354), the facilities' registry (VB-361) or the sea's rule (VB-436). VB-083, VB-115 and VB-721 are rewritten and wait for
  them; VB-097 is confirmed, its trigger measured by the wrap-up; VB-722 is closed here (section 8).
- **Every number is a starting value.** The climate's figures are from memory and unchecked; the reach figures are a scratch
  script's at year 0, good as a pointer.

## 5. The design

The text is in `design.md`, Climate and travel beyond walking; this section gives the reasoning the tables there leave out.

### 5.1 Climate

- **A world has a latitude,** one of four climates chosen on the generation screen: southern (57 degrees), middle (59, the
  default), northern (62), far north (65). It is not drawn from the seed, so every world of today is the middle world and no
  island changes. The references (founders, populous) stay on the middle world: principle 6 names them, and a target is not
  moved by adding a colder world beside them. Other climates are reported with no target.
- **The map's north is colder:** 0.015 degrees a row, 3 degrees over the medium map, read in three bands (thirds of the map).
  This is a compression, as the distances are: 200 rows are 30 km at the land's scale, which in the world would be a quarter of a
  degree. Without it "colder further north" would hold only between games. Bands, not rows, so that a date changes a third of the
  map at once and the dates stay few.
- **The winter's dates** are computed once, on autumn day 1, from the band's curve and the winter's warmth (the season's draw of
  VB-354), and put in the due-queue (Rules for cost, 4). Six at most a band: frost (snow lies, the ground and the marsh bear,
  lakes and rivers skin over and close to boats), lake ice bears, the sound shuts to boats, sound ice bears, the thaw (no ice
  bears, snow goes, the ground is soft), open water and firm ground.
- **The growing year** is the band's too: VB-354's thaw and first frost become the band's dates, and later a field on high ground
  reads a later thaw by a lapse of 8 degrees over the land's height. On the middle world they fall where VB-354 put them (the
  thaw on spring day 1 to 4).
- **The wetness of a place** exists already: `w.hm`, a tile's moisture from the rain the seed's wind brings and the water of its
  valley (VB-342). VB-721 (draining) reads it; nothing new is stored.
- **A slow cooling over centuries** is not built (question 7): the weather varies about a normal that stands still.
- **Seed as a strain fitted to its band** (VB-722) is not built: the evidence gives no size for it, the bands of one map are a
  degree apart, and the case that matters is seed from abroad, which is the ship's. The finding is handed to VB-188.

### 5.2 The ground by season

| From | The ground | Boats |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Frost | Snow lies: a tile with no lane costs 1.3 times its cost; marsh bears and costs 1.5; sledges run and carts are laid up | Lakes and rivers closed |
| Lake ice bears | Lake tiles and every river tile cost 1 on foot, with no slope: a river needs no ford | |
| The sound shuts (half the frost that makes it bear) | | The sea closed in that band |
| Sound ice bears | The sea within 3 tiles of land costs 1 on foot | |
| The thaw | No ice bears; snow goes; soft ground costs 1.3 where no lane is; sledges stop, carts stay laid up | Still closed |
| Open water, firm ground | As in summer; carts run | Open |

- **Deep snow and frozen marsh** are what make winter ways differ from summer ways: the dear ground of the journey scale is
  marsh at 5, and frozen it is 1.5, so a winter way runs straight over mire and lake where the summer way goes round. Lanes stay
  trodden (Worn paths, 6: Agreed).
- **Nobody sleeps on the ice:** a journey's leg stops at the shore if it cannot cross before the day ends, and a date takes
  effect at 4:00, when nobody is out. So no pawn is on water when it opens.
- **The year's dates differ from the normal's by a game day or two;** a traveller who planned by the winter table and finds the
  ice not bearing goes round or waits, as travellers did.

- The first three loads and paces are Decided or Agreed (12 Nov) and unchanged. The rest are Proposed, from memory and unchecked:
  a sledge on a snow way was held to carry half again to twice a cart's summer load; a rowing boat of 5 m carries three or four
  people or some hundreds of kilograms; a boat of 12 m with four oars two to four tonnes (halved, as the carts' loads were, for
  bulk).
- **What a cart needs underfoot** is the one rule of this slice that makes ways matter to a farm: today a cart crosses pathless
  forest at the walker's cost. A steep step is refused by its slope factor, the number the route search already has. A cart's
  crossing wears a tile as three walkers do, so the carted way becomes the lane the ox cart needs.
- **Heavy hauling waits for the snow** by the head's choice, on its own estimate of the hours (principles 14 and 16): timber,
  stone and firewood from the far wood. On the southern world no head waits, since no snow comes in a normal winter.
- **The break-up** is when a load goes on the back: no sledge, no cart, no boat.

### 5.4 Boats and landings

- **A landing** is where a village's boats lie: the shore tile cheapest by land from the green, within 2 hours. A household's
  fishing boat keeps the shore tile it was built on. 10 to 20 of 22 to 30 villages have one (section 2).
- **A boat is a vehicle** (target-v2's record): it is at a shore tile or under way with a rower aboard, and holds people and goods.
  It is the fishing boat that exists, given a place that can change; no second small boat is invented.
- **A journey by water** is a walk to the boat, a leg by water, and a walk on. The boat stays at the far shore until it is rowed
  back, and is not fished from meanwhile: the head weighs the catch lost. A move (a bride, a servant) is rowed by a member of
  either household who rows home again; if nobody can, the move waits.
- **No ferry yet.** A boat that lies at a crossing for anyone's use, and what is given for the crossing, is a service between
  households: it waits for the standing deals of slice 5 and is named in "Not yet".
- **The large boat** is begun by a head whose loads waited for a boat, and needs four who choose to row. It is the first vehicle
  one household cannot work alone; who builds them is reported by the wrap-up (principle 13).
- **The ship** (slice 8) finds: landings, the days of open water by climate (about 4 game days fewer in the far north than on the
  middle world at each end of the year), the large boat as the island's own carrier to the harbour, and the vehicle record.

### 5.5 Built ways, passage and upkeep

- **Three kinds** (principle 7: key kinds): the track, the causeway and the bridge. A road with a made surface and a street are
  the town's and wait for one. A blazed trail (VB-447, from VB-445's plan) is the rung below the track and is that plan's.
- **Where: along worn ways only.** The worn way is the desire line and the survey: nobody lays out a road. The alderman reckons,
  from the crossings counted on a tile and the loads that went on the back for want of a way, what a stretch would save in a year,
  and proposes the one whose saving over 5 years most exceeds its building hours. For a bridge the saving is the detour by the
  nearest ford, for the destinations the village's ways lead to across the river. The meeting votes; the alderman carries it out.
- **Upkeep** is a custom, `wayDuty`: the village's work days (the default, since `sim/workdays` exists), lots by farm (the
  Swedish *väglott*: each farm a stretch in proportion to its land, viewed at the court), or the builder's.
- **Passage on foot** is read where a tile lies, not by who walks, so that one compiled ground serves every walker: a field in
  crop and a yard are crossed by nobody, the holder included, but along a way; the practice `passage` says whether standing hay
  may be crossed. A grid a household would be 200 grids.
- **The right of way** is asked of each holder as an offer and answered by its head's own estimate of gain and loss and by what
  it owes the asker. A refusal bends the way or stops it.
- **Later:** a toll as a standing deal; standing from a public work (the bridges magnates built and marked with rune stones);
  the Crown's roads. Named, not built.

### 5.6 Reach by mode and season, and what the town's plan handed over

1. **Regions and market potential** stay reports. They are shown three ways, on foot, by boat and over the ice of a normal
   winter, at 3, 6 and 12 hours; a household's estimate of a green names the mode it reckoned with (VB-813).
2. **Reach as the lever on makers:** the wrap-up reports the people within 3 hours of each village by each mode against the second
   mock's figures. This plan's measure is that boats and ice leave that circle where it was (section 2).
3. **A day there and back** stays the bound, in hours. By boat 18 to 30 pairs within 6 hours become 21 to 37; the boat is away
   for the day. By sledge over ice the bound holds for the weeks the ice bears.
4. **The third mock** (VB-776) has not run. Nothing in this plan rests on a town.

**Over the sea** (Decided, 10 Nov, as closed until this slice): an act between villages needs a way by land, or a boat that one
of the two households has, or ice that bears. VB-813 rewrites the row.

**The gathering on a cold island.** Winter days 1 to 3 fall in the freeze-up. A custom of the culture, `gatherDay`, gains a second
option, midwinter (winter days 8 to 10), which a northern culture starts with. This moves a Decided date for those cultures and
is Proposed (question 8).
