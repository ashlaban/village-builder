# VB-444: the journey scale (begun 16 November 2026)

The plan of VB-444, written in batches in a session with Kim. This file holds Kim's answers, the measures and the decisions as they
are made; `design.md` is written from it when the numbers after are in. The measure is `docs/plans/VB-444-geo.js`, a throwaway
script, not game code: `node docs/plans/VB-444-geo.js $PWD populous medium 3,7,11,42`.

## 1. Kim's answers (16 November 2026)

1. **A reach is a judgement, not a radius.** Whether a tile is worth working is to be the household head's judgement, part of its
   plan: if the head judges the travel time worth it, the tile is worked. Until that is built the reaches stay as they are, in
   steps, read as what they are: a distance. So this plan changes no reach of daily work; it lists them (section 3) and writes the
   task that replaces them.
2. **The forest's spread by seed is widened**, to reach from about 30% to about 60% of the land, about half at the median seed.
3. **Populous seats 800 as a target** and does not fail where the island holds fewer (as built on 15 November). So the scenario
   clears no forest, and the generator is not asked to keep meadow for it: a forested island may seat fewer.
4. **No nightly-scale runs for the plan.** The geography is measured at every seed and map named; years are run on the small map
   only, at two or three seeds; growth on medium is left to the nightly run after the implement tasks.

## 7. The thresholds between villages

The design's table asks for its thresholds "to be set again": they were set at 3 tiles an hour and have been a quarter as long
since 12 November. **Decided here (Deciding without Kim, 8 and 3): every threshold keeps its hours, and none is rescaled.**
Each is argued from the day, not from the map: a day's walk is the 12 hours from 6:00 to 18:00, there and back in a day is half
of it, a visit's days are the legs it takes. Those are physical facts of the walker's day; the scale changes the island under
them, which is what Kim asked for (15 November: travel felt fast and nobody was a stranger). Rescaling the hours with the speed
would hand the old island back. What is rewritten in `design.md` is the counts the rows quote.

Row by row:

- **There and back in a day, 6 hours** (claims on empty farms, `CLAIMS.farmHours`; work done from home). The median village on
  medium has 1 or 2 villages this near (2 to 9 today), and 2 to 8 villages of 22 to 30 have none (0 to 4 of 19 to 23). An empty
  farm is claimed by its own village or the one next door. Kept.
- **A day's walk, 12 hours** (service, `SERVICE.hours`; "villages this close mix as neighbours"). The median village has 2 to 4
  neighbours on medium (4 to 10 today) and 2 to 6 on small; one or two villages an island have none, as today. The design's
  "a median of 4 neighbours a village" becomes about 3. Kept.
- **A long trip**, more than 12 hours: on medium, half or more of a village's island is now this, where today most villages had
  the whole island within 24 hours.
- **Out of reach, 67 hours** (section 6): 16 to 37 pairs by land on three medium islands of four, none on small. Kept: it is the
  longest visit the design costs out, 13 days.
- **Ease of mixing, 2 × exp(-hours / 8)** (the round's choice of a wife; the tie to the culture's centre, `TIE.hours`). The
  formula stands. What it gives: the nearest neighbour, at 2.6 to 2.9 hours, weighs 1.4 (it is 1.6 today at 1.4 to 1.9 hours),
  against 2 within the village; all a village's other villages together, as a sum of exp(-hours / 8), weigh 1.8 to 2.9 at the
  median village on medium (3.0 to 6.3 today), so about half. Marriage turns towards the village and its neighbour, and a bride
  from two days off becomes rare; more couples will wait for a match within a small pool. Whether the pool is then too small is
  for section 8's years to show (marriages, the never-married) and, past that, for the tuning run: **`TIE.hours` and the match's

  6.0), which is what would give today's weight back. Not changed here: 8 hours is "two thirds of a day's walk", not a number
  fitted to the island.
- **A visit's days, 2 × ceil(hours / 12) + 1**: unchanged, and already in legs of a day. The nearest neighbour is 3 days of a
  household's winter; the median pair on medium, 14 to 37 hours, is 5 to 9.
- **The round's other acts** (an orphan's service home in the nearest village, guardians and kin within walking reach): rules of
  nearest and of reach, which follow the table with no threshold of their own.
- **Over the sea**: unchanged; no act between villages with no route by land until slice 10.

Close call, surfaced: keeping every threshold. Another agent could have widened the match's scale to 12 hours at once, to keep
the marriage market as large as it is; this plan lets the island shrink the market first and measures it, since a village that
marries its neighbours is the geography Kim chose.
