Two findings shape the plan.

1. **A stint has no bite yet.** Limiting a household's grazing changes nothing the player or the pawns can feel, since overgrazing costs
   nothing. A custom that stints needs the consequence first: a commons that is eaten down gives less (section 4, VB-709). Without it the
   custom would be a number on a page (against principle 8, mechanisms not prescriptions).
2. **Two of the five candidates need a thing that does not exist.** Sharing hay from common meadows needs common meadows, and the alderman
   (VB-314) who makes and shares them (target-v2 trace 7.1, step 2). Hunting rights need almost nothing to compare (one wild grazer,
   little meat). They go last.

## 2. The candidates

For each: the options, the history (medieval Nordic; where I am unsure I say so), the default, the malleability (1 = easiest to change,
the scale of `design.md`, Custom dimensions), what it acts through, and its cost. Costs per custom: the daily drift of attitudes grows with
options, and VB-184 priced five new practices at 1.5 ms a game day at `medium-ref`, so **about 0.3 ms a game day for a custom of 3 options**;
the rule it feeds costs what its section says. Counters are named; times are left to the nightly bench.

### 2.1 Grazing on the commons: `graze` (a voted custom; the main one)

*What it decides:* how many animals a household may put on the village's outland pasture (its commons).

| Option | Meaning in play |
| --- | --- |
| `byLand` (default) | The village grants each household a stint, in cow-equivalents, in proportion to the land it holds in the village (fields and meadows, as tiles). A landed household's stint grows with its land; one with no land has none |
| `open` | No stint. Any household of the village grazes anywhere on its outland, as today; so does a stranger (a herd of another village), so far as it can walk |
| `meeting` | The autumn meeting (through `sim/decisions`, VB-313) allots the stints each year in proportion to the head each household winters (what its hay supports), cut to the commons' capacity; the alderman (VB-314) carries it out |

*History.* In medieval Sweden the village's commons (*byamark*, *utmark*, and the *allmänning* beyond) were used by right of a holding:
the landscape laws and later the village ordinances tie the right to the farm, and the division of the infield by the sun (*solskifte*)
gave each farm a share of the commons in proportion to its share of the infield plot (I am fairly sure of this link, less of the exact
formula, which varied by province). Stinting is the standard account of how such commons were kept (Ostrom 1990, already cited in `design.md`,
Animals), and village by-laws punished overstocking with fines. A fully open commons, with no limit, is the pre-pressure state, where land was
plentiful, so `open` is a real option for a young village with free land; `meeting` is the by-law village, where the village court (*byastämma*)
set the numbers. The Danish and Scanian *byalag* did stint by the plough-unit; the Nordic details differ and I do not claim more.
*Default:* `byLand`, because it follows the land share the task asks for, matches the historical right of the holding, and gives the landed
class its edge (principle 1).
*Malleability:* 0.4. It touches every herd-holder and ties to who holds land, so it changes less easily than the inheritance customs of
the middle (0.45 to 0.65) but is no law of the land (0.12 to 0.3).

*How a stint works (mechanism, not a prescription).* A household whose herds are larger than its stint takes only the stint's worth to the
commons: the herder's pasture load on the commons is `min(head, stint)` (the load counts cattle 1, sheep `herding.sheep`; the stint is in the
same units, HERD.hay's cow-equivalents, so no new table). The rest stand at the farm, as a herd without a herder does: half milk and a stray
risk. A household can bring them back by grazing its own meadows' aftermath (existing rule) or by selling or slaughtering at the autumn count.
Nothing forbids more animals; the commons just does not feed them.
*The commons' capacity,* for `meeting`, is the sum of the grass values of its outland pasture tiles times a tunable (`HERD.commons.cap`, a
cow-equivalent a tile at full grass), refreshed at the autumn meeting. For `byLand` the stint is that capacity times the household's share
of the village's tiles in use.

*Acts through.* `sim/grazing` (`grazable`, `pasture`, the pressure it adds), a household field `hh.stint` written only by `sim/grazing`
(recomputed yearly, at the autumn meeting, by the village; a plain number, no right yet); when VB-311 lands, the stint is recorded as a
`graze` right whose holding is the village's outland and holder the household, `basis: 'custom'`, term one year, granted by
`LandRights.grant` with the village as grantor (target-v2 4.3); `hh.stint` becomes the right's cache. The meeting's option goes through
`sim/decisions` (VB-313). The custom is a `DIMS` entry `grazing` read through `optionOf(st, vid)` like `commons`; its phrase goes in `SAID`;
`sim/leanings` gains its interests (landed with big herds: `open` or `byLand`; landless with a cow: `meeting` or `open`; small herds:
`byLand`, since it shields the commons they use).
*Cost.* Entities at `medium-ref`: 211 households, about 200 villages' outland plots. Yearly: one pass over a village's households and its
farms' fields and meadows (tens of tiles), spread by village across the meeting steps (Rules for cost 5): a counter `stintHouseholds`.
Daily: nothing, the right is read where the pasture is chosen (each `pasture` call, at most once a week a household) as one lookup against
`hh.stint`; no new loop over tiles. Attitudes: 0.3 ms a day.

### 2.2 A tenant's share

Under `byLand` the right belongs to the holding. VB-311 splits a holding's `own` and `use` and creates tenancies. *Options:* (a) the
share goes with the **use** right: a tenant grazes what the farm he works is entitled to, and the owner, who works nothing, none; (b) with
the **owner**, who may graze (or let) it even where the tenant works the land; (c) split by the rent terms (the contract decides). *History:*
in Sweden the commons right was the holding's, exercised by whoever worked it (*landbonde* and tenant alike; the *jordägare* did not
put animals on it, although a lord's tenants' rights are in dispute and I am not sure of the lord's claims); so (a). *Proposed (a)*, on the
holding. It is also the only one that needs no new state: the `graze` right is one of the user's rights. A question for Kim only because it
decides who gains from owning land that is let out (closer to principle 13 than to a cost).

### 2.3 Winter stocking: `stocking` (a voted custom; the small one, first)

| Option | Meaning in play |
| --- | --- |
| `hay` ("to the hay"; default) | The autumn count keeps what the hay, with the 1.1 margin, feeds: as today |
| `herd` ("keep the herd") | The count's margin is below 1 (first value 0.8): it expects to stretch the hay (a lean ration, straw, browse, the animals thin by spring), so it slaughters fewer and the herd is larger and thinner |

*History.* Autumn slaughter (*slaktmånad*) down to the fodder was the textbook rule, but the habit of keeping more than one could feed, and
the animals coming out in spring so weak that cows had to be lifted to their feet, is well known from Swedish and Danish farm history
(the "lifting" is attested to my recollection; I am not sure how general it was in the Middle Ages, where the sources are thin). The
mechanism is what gives it a price: lean days cost next summer's milk (existing), and a herd unfed two days running loses a head (existing).
*A bad winter punishes `herd` and a good one rewards it,* with no number set for the outcome (principle 8).
*Default:* `hay`, as today (no change to behaviour unless a village changes). *Malleability:* 0.6: a habit of the farm, copied from
neighbours, easily changed; its interest pull: the stock-rich and the ambitious prefer `herd`, a household that has lost animals prefers `hay`.
*Acts through.* `sim/byre` `autumnCount` (the margin it multiplies by) reads the village's option through `optionOf`: one table, `HERD.margins`
`{hay: 1.1, herd: 0.8}`, tunable. A household's head does the counting and so chooses (principle 16): the custom is the norm he follows, and
a later task may let a head's traits and what he foresees (principle 14) differ from it. *Cost:* none beyond 0.3 ms a day for the custom.
The count already runs once a household a year.

### 2.4 Herding by farm or by turns: `herding` (byahjord; a voted custom)

| Option | Meaning in play |
| --- | --- |
| `farm` (default) | Each household sends its own herder to its own pasture, as today |
| `turns` (*byahjord*) | One household a day (a rota by farm, in the order of the village's farms; weighted by the head each keeps) sends a herder who takes all the village's herds to one pasture; the others send none |

*History.* Village herds under a rota (*byahjord*, *vall* by turns) are well attested in Swedish villages, but mostly in the early modern
period and in nucleated villages with common fields; in the Middle Ages, in sparse farm country, each farm herded its own, and summer
farms (*fäbod*) were another system altogether (not here). I am unsure of the medieval extent; it is a plausible custom and a good test
of the cost of shared labour. A rota freed most of a village's children from herding for most of the summer, which is why it spread.
*Default:* `farm`. *Malleability:* 0.3 (a change of the village's way of life; bigger for villages with 3 or more herding households, none
for a village of one).
*Mechanism.* The herder walks the farms' yards to gather the herds (each herd is led from its byre, so nothing teleports) and back in the
evening; the herds graze one pasture the village picks (the commons' best, by `pasture`'s rule over its outland, not by one house). A
household that does not send the herder this day sends nobody, so the labour of a village's herding falls to about one herder a day.
*Acts through.* `sim/grazing` (`herd` kind: today per household; the kind's `site` and `draw` become the village's on a turn day, and
`driveOut` takes the village's herds), a village rota field (`village.rota`: the next farm, a plain number, written by `sim/grazing`), the
herds' `herder`/`herded` fields as now. *Cost:* work falls: one herder a village-day instead of one a household-day; the walk to gather
costs one more small bfs a day a village. Counter `rotaVillages`.

### 2.5 Hunting rights: `hunting` (a culture-wide practice at first)

| Option | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| `free` (default) | Anyone may hunt on land that is not under a house, field or meadow, as `sim/deer` has it |
| `landed` | A household may hunt only on land its village holds or its own; no trespass on another village's outland |
| `leave` | Only with the village's leave, a grant `hunt` by the alderman, for a season |

*History.* Free hunting for all on the commons and on one's own land is the old Nordic rule as the landscape laws have it (hunting was one of
the rights of the free; I am fairly sure, not certain, about the law texts). Royal and noble reservation of game, *jakträtt* held by the Crown
over big game, grows in the later Middle Ages and is mostly the 16th and 17th centuries; for the setting here the stricter options need an
established Crown or lord (slice 8). *Default:* `free`. *Malleability:* 0.6 (low stakes, easily changed).
*Why a practice, not a custom first.* There is one wild grazer and a household's yield is a few rations; a voted custom would cost 0.3 ms a
day for a rule that nobody votes for. `PRACTICES` in `src/culture.js` is the lighter kind (a culture's rule, no drift); it can become a
voted custom once the stock of deer or the claims of villages make villages differ.
*Acts through.* `sim/deer` `huntGround` (add the right check per tile), `LandRights` (a `hunt` kind added to `RightKind`, a close call, section 6).
*Cost:* one lookup per candidate tile of a hunt ground; nothing daily.

### 2.6 Sharing hay from common meadows: `hayShare` (later; a practice)

| Option | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| `work` | Each household takes the hay it mowed (the common meadow is mown by those who work it) |
| `lots` | The meadow is shared in strips drawn by lot each year (*lotter*, as the Swedish *ängslotter*), each household mowing its strip |
| `herd` | The hay goes to the village barn and is shared by the head each household winters |
| `need` | The alderman shares it first to the households short of hay |

*History.* Common meadows mown in strips drawn by lot (*lottning*, *tegskifte* in some regions) are attested in Nordic villages; sharing by
work done is the default of a common work. I am unsure that a village barn shared hay by need in the Middle Ages (poor relief came with
the church and the parish, slice 8). *Default:* `work`. *Malleability:* 0.5. Needs the village's own meadow (a project of its meeting,
as the common field is), the alderman, and a village hay store carried by those who fetch it (the hard rule). **Not before VB-314** and
a common-meadow routine like `sim/commons`. Sketched as a task, not scheduled.

## 4. The bite: what overgrazing costs (VB-709), so a stint means something

*Proposal.* A cow's summer milk depends on the grass she grazed yesterday: milk a cow in milk is `HERD.milk` times a factor
`f = clamp(1 - HERD.herding.bite * pressure / H.full, floor, 1)` read from `grazeOf(st, pasture)` where pressure is today's per tile
(the closed-form `w.graze`). `H.full` (100) is already "full" in pasture choice, so the bite starts where herds already move on. Lean
days of winter already lower milk the same way. A herd that grazes a crowded commons gives less milk, so a small farm has a reason to
want a stint, and a big one has a reason to spread out (more pastures, later byahjord, or shifts to other tiles).
*Why not also change forest spread or wear land.* The existing coupling to forest spread stays; a second one would be
a different decision (a pasture that turns to heath is Later in the design).
*Reported before and after:* milk a cow and total milk by year, households' hungry days, at populous small seeds 3, 7, 11, 42, 15 years,
and founders tiny seed 7. A new tunable `HERD.herding.bite` (first value 0.3, at most five runs, values tried and results in the task file).
*Cost:* none beyond the existing daily milk loop (one array read per herd; `w.graze` read exists).

- `village.rota` (the next farm's index; plain number) for `turns`.
- `HERD.commons` and `HERD.margins` tunables in `src/sim/rules.js`, not saved state.

## 6. Decisions I made (Deciding without Kim; the reason with each)

1. **`graze` is a `graze` right granted by the village under the custom** (the task's own first question). Target-v2 4.3 already lists
   `graze` in `RightKind` and the task's Goal states it; the grant is the village's act (principle 16: an alderman carries out the
   meeting's allotment). Before VB-311 the stint is a plain number, the right wraps it later. Not a question: it extends a system
   (principle 4); the design's rights row says grazing rights come with this plan (Proposed).
2. **Customs, not prescriptions** (principle 8): no custom sets herd size, milk or hunting yield directly. The stint limits what the
   commons feeds; `stocking` sets the count's expected margin and the existing lean days and starvation do the rest; the rota sets who
   herds and the walking and grazing do the rest.
3. **Stints are in cow-equivalents** from `HERD.hay`, no new table (principle 3: parameters with a physical meaning: a sheep draws a fifth
   of a cow, as it eats).
4. **A household with no land has no commons right under `byLand`**. It is the custom's historical consequence and gives the landed class
   its weight (principles 1 and 13: elite is what the rules leave over); the village's other ways (`open`, `meeting`) are the answer.
5. **Closing a village's outland to strangers** under `byLand` and `meeting`. Taking in outland is already "never another village's land"
   (Decided, 1 Nov, review-claims); grazing is open today only for lack of a rule. Enforcing it (principle 11) needs no question; a herd of
   another village on the outland is trespass, reported as a log line and a border grievance later (VB-297).
6. **Hunting and hay-sharing start as practices** (culture-wide, no drift), voted customs later. Cost principle: 0.3 ms a day a custom
   is spent where villages differ; I could not see them differing for hunting yet. A close call (`decision` note).
7. **A `hunt` right kind is added to `RightKind`** rather than reusing `gather`: hunting is named in the design's candidates, takes a
   living stock, and `fish` already has its own kind. A close call.
8. **`herd` margin 0.8 for `stocking`** is a first value (principle 3), tuned in at most five runs in VB-711.
9. **Tenants:** (a), the share goes with the use right, is my proposal; it is also in section 7 because of what it does to landlords.
10. **No chosen order beyond section 3** among slices: VB-709 and VB-711 have no dependency and can be taken any time; VB-710 follows
    VB-311.

## 7. Design choices for Kim

(Each with options, the proposed answer and a one-line reason; Kim's answer of 16 November below each. All eight are the proposed answer.)

1. **Which customs, and how many voted?** Options: (a) five voted customs now (graze, stocking, herding, hunting, hay share), about 1.5
   ms a game day at `medium-ref`; (b) three voted (graze, stocking, herding) and two culture practices (hunting, hay share) that become
   voted later; (c) graze only. **Proposed (b).** Reason: the three differ between villages and shape the herd; the other two have no
   second option anyone would vote for yet, and each voted custom costs about 0.3 ms a day.
   **Kim, 16 Nov 2026:** (b): three voted customs (graze, stocking, herding); hunting and hay sharing start as culture-wide practices, voted later.
2. **Does overgrazing have to cost something, so that a stint matters?** Options: (a) yes, a crowded pasture lowers the cows' milk
   (VB-709), with forest spread unchanged; (b) yes, also the pasture wears to heath (a bigger system, "Later" in the design); (c) no, the
   custom only decides who may graze where (a stint then changes nothing). **Proposed (a).** Reason: it is the smallest mechanism that
   makes `graze` mean something; (c) would be a number on a page; (b) is another slice's work.
   **Kim, 16 Nov 2026:** (a): a crowded pasture lowers the cows' milk; forest spread unchanged; no wear to heath.
3. **Whose share when a holding is let: the tenant's or its owner's?** Options: (a) the right goes with the use of the holding (the
   tenant who farms it); (b) with the owner, who may graze or let it; (c) split by rent terms. **Proposed (a).** Reason: the commons right
   was the holding's, used by whoever worked it; (b) turns a landlord into a graze lord with no work, which only a decision of Kim's
   should make.
   **Kim, 16 Nov 2026:** (a): a let holding's share of the commons is the tenant's; it follows the use right.
4. **What does a household with no land get on the commons under `byLand`?** Options: (a) nothing (its way: `open` or `meeting` to
   change it); (b) a cottager's right to one cow-equivalent; (c) nothing, but the village may lend a stint (a custom later). **Proposed
   (a).** Reason: matches the right of the holding and the landed class's weight; the other two customs are the village's remedy, and a
   household may fail (principle 1).
   **Kim, 16 Nov 2026:** (a): under `byLand` a household with no land gets no stint; `open` or `meeting` is the village's remedy.
5. **Who herds the village herd under byahjord?** Options: (a) by turns of the farms, in a rota weighted by head kept; (b) a hired
   village herdsman, paid in grain from the village store by the alderman (needs the `hired` basis and VB-314); (c) both, as the
   custom's two options. **Proposed (a) now, (b) later as a third option.** Reason: turns need nothing the slice lacks; a hired
   herdsman is a new office and wage (a new verb for both the player and every pawn, principle 16).
   **Kim, 16 Nov 2026:** (a) now: turns of the farms weighted by head kept; (b), a hired herdsman, later, as a third option.
6. **Stocking: how thin may "keep the herd" go, and who bears it?** Options: (a) the count's margin 0.8 (below 1, expects the hay
   stretched), the cost falling on the lean-day and starvation rules as built; (b) as (a), and the herd may be fed from the village
   store's grain in a bad winter (help for animals); (c) only a count-down of the oldest head, never thin. **Proposed (a).** Reason:
   needs no new feed; (b) opens animals as claimants on the village store (principle 14 estimate, loans), and (c) would be the same as
   today.
   **Kim, 16 Nov 2026:** (a): the count's margin 0.8; the lean-day and starvation rules as built carry the cost; no feed from the village store.
7. **Open grazing for strangers.** Options: (a) a village's outland is closed to other villages' herds under `byLand` and `meeting`,
   open only under `open`; (b) always open as today (no border rule); (c) closed always. **Proposed (a).** Reason: Decided 1 Nov, no
   claim on another village's land; the custom `open` is then a real choice and it starts a quarrel (VB-297) only where villages
   meet.
   **Kim, 16 Nov 2026:** (a): closed out under `byLand` and `meeting`, admitted only under `open`.
8. **Slice and order.** Options: (a) VB-709 and VB-711 now, interleaved (no dependency); VB-710 after VB-311 in slice 5 if it lands
   in time; herding, hunting and hay share to a later "animals" slice; (b) all in slice 5; (c) all after slice 5. **Proposed (a).**

## 11. Phase two (16 November 2026): what changed from the draft, and the numbers decided

Decided by `AGENTS.md`, Deciding without Kim, 3 (a first value with a meaning, to be tried in at most five runs by the task that builds
it), unless it says otherwise. The task files and `design.md` carry these values.

1. **The bite's formula** (section 4) is corrected. As drafted, `1 - bite * pressure / full`, milk fell from the first hour of grazing: a
   lone herd loads its own tile to `downAt` (300) in its week on it, so every herd would have stood at the floor, crowded or not, against
   the draft's own criterion ("no change where a pasture is not crowded"). The bite starts where a herd moves on:
   `f = clamp(1 - bite * (pressure - downAt) / downAt, floor, 1)`, with `HERD.herding.bite` 0.3 (twice a herd's week on a tile costs 30%).
   A formula that computes something other than it says is a fix (Deciding without Kim, 3); a close call on its shape, noted as a `decision`.
2. **The bite's floor**, left open in the draft: `HERD.herding.floor` 0.5, the milk of a herd with no herder (`HERD.herding.milk`), so
   that taking the herd out is never worse than leaving it at the farm.
3. **The stint's unit**: section 2.1 named both `herding.sheep` (0.25) and `HERD.hay` (a ewe 0.2). It is the pasture load, cattle 1 and a
   sheep `HERD.herding.sheep` (0.25), since a stint limits what the pasture bears.
4. **The commons' capacity**, `HERD.commons.cap`, left open: 0.25 cow-equivalents a tile at full grass. A cow out 9 hours for the 27
   grazing days of a year lays about 365 units of pressure on her tile and its neighbours (9 × (1 + 6 × 0.25) × 27); spread over four
   tiles that is about 90 a tile, under the 100 at which a tile counts as full in pasture choice. An estimate: a `tuning` note.
5. **The rota's weight** (section 2.4), left open: a farm herds one day for each `HERD.rota.per` (2) cow-equivalents it keeps, at least
   one, before the turn passes; `HERD.rota.least` (3) farms with herds for a village to herd by turns.
