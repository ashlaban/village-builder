Time on the land

Calendar · after weather forecast maps, the cone, small multiples, the inset track:
Scales to / strains at

Scales to: one household's week on its own land. The scrubber reads well for what the land will look like: the barley turning stooked, the cut edge's cone widening past day 7, Erik's track ending in a striped blob. Naming the fence day by dragging it onto the scrubber, and seeing the barley's edge fall back and the displaced arrow, is the best moment: cost appears where it lands.

Strains at: comparison across days (A shows one day at a time; you must scrub and remember). B fixes comparison but each multiple is ~320 px wide: icons survive, ghosts and pips are crowded; the reverse (today big, strip of six) is too small to read. C's ribbons are 6 px ticks: a pattern, not a plan. Stress: 6 dated items on the green and Norrgård collapse into "+n" pips, and 19 farms' fence days are lines into one tile; three interrupted tasks sit as stray signs that never move with the scrubber. Season targets and the budget found no place: they went to the margin. The six parts of the day, terms and authorship do not show at all.

Verdict: the review is right in the main. Time can live on the land for consequences in place (a field's progress, where people will be, what a named day displaces), not for the schedule. Keep a forecast scrubber as a lens of the time strip (scrub the strip, the land previews) and the cone for estimates; the plan itself needs the strip.
