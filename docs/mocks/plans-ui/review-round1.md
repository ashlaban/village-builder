# Round 1 review (Fable, 25 November): land, primstav, threads

An independent review of the three first mocks against `docs/plans/four-scales/README.md` section 3. Its findings, condensed;
the decisions are the owner's.

## Keep
- land: one sign at the place of work with the workers' dots under it; the selected member's cut-and-resumed route as numbered
  steps; the season shield with "-8" on the barley field.
- primstav: the three bands of the time axis (binding, Per's estimate, beyond the horizon), solid turning striped at the estimate's
  end; the levy window as one hatched bar with "name a day"; the stake lighting with its bar; **the remedy list with each option's
  cost** ("Add a hand: Nils, off firewood; wood target 10 h behind"), the most valuable element of the three.
- threads: one selection, one chain, the rest dimmed; the "displaced" edge with its cost (Erik's fence day -10.5 h to the barley);
  the "vs" of budget against target; the why-ladder as a card anchored at the thing.

## Dead ends
- The hearth ring (covers the busiest tile and its neighbours; 8 px glyphs; 8 members ~75 px radius; impossible at 20 households).
- The stave's wood and notch pictograms, and the stave standing on the green (covers what it links to).
- The "no place on the land" column: most reasons have no tile, so the long arrow is the common case.

## The grammar
- Frame = horizon is the weakest channel (day and dated cannot be told apart at map size). Collapse to three frames: round (doing
  now), square (planned or dated, the day as its number), shield (target or aim); let a time axis carry the horizon elsewhere.
- Whose hours: ochre vanishes on rye, stubble and yard; ochre against red-brown is a red-green confusion pair; the hatches read as
  mud at 40 px (demanded and taken look the same). Encode the owner twice (hue and a rim or shape cue); a breach as a mark (a torn
  edge), not a texture.
- The badge mixes state and author: an authored step at risk needs both. Give the author its own channel, the corner to state.
- The number has no unit (29.5, 2.5, -8, 9/20, day 6, a count). One meaning per frame: square a day, shield hours short, signed.

## Decisions underneath (the reviewer's recommendations)
- Time lives on one thin strip docked at the bottom (the design's 3.7), collapsed to a line for the selected body; the stave's bands
  kept, its wood dropped. The land shows where and what is wrong.
- The day belongs to the pawn; the farmstead shows an aggregate (levied hours against budget, a count of problems).
- Modes by question, not horizon: Look (why), Plan (strip + map, drag to assign), Build, Muster and Ledger (tables, honestly
  panels). Zoom on the strip changes the horizon.
- Decision points: the "at once" and "the hour" kinds on the pawn or icon, slowing time; dated ones in a tray ordered by deadline,
  a tap flying to the place with the remedies and the default ("the rule will: add Nils").
- One mark per tile as aggregation: the most urgent thing with a count pip; the callout lists the rest.

## Directions not yet tried
1. The tray and fly-to: a deadline-ordered rail of decision icons; the land shows only now and problems.
2. The pawn-carried day: each pawn wears its 12 hours in the six parts with the levy line; clustered pawns merge into a household
   stack, a village into a stack of stacks.
3. The muster table as a first-class mode, the alderman's view first.

## The biggest risk
The map is being asked to be the schedule. What makes the design complex (the six parts, terms, the ladder, authorship, deadlines)
is temporal and relational, not spatial, and most reasons have no tile. Decide that the land shows state and problems and one honest
time surface carries plans; test icons at true map size on a 20-household scene.
