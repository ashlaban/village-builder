## Architecture and design

The shipped application is a single generated HTML page. `build.py` assembles
simulation modules in manifest order and presentation scripts in page order.
The simulation uses seeded random streams, plain serializable state, commands
for actions, a rules registry, work counters and deterministic golden checks.
The PixiJS presentation reads simulation state and acts through the simulation
API. Page scripts share a closure, so their ordering is an implicit dependency.

The target has five layers: world/kernel, material/land, social, agency and
read-only derived views. It is a migration target; its proposed interfaces are
not permission to replace the existing design. The strongest current boundary
is the owner table and its declining allowance for writes outside owners.
The principal remaining risks are those shared writes, late-game work that
scales with history, and long moving-round/midnight steps. Current modules are
more granular than the target; merging them merely to match its module count
would not itself improve the architecture.

The design is a medieval Nordic household simulation whose player eventually
inhabits a pawn. Family succession, changing customs and food production drive
it. The painterly hex world and inspectable people support that intent.
The v0.4 design deliberately leaves later slices to their plan tasks; proposed
goodwill, player control and household planning must not silently become new
rules during a refactor. Preserve the existing contact-based ties here; the
change to familiarity belongs to VB-127.
