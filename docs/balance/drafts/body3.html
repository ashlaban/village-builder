What holds the game in balance

Nine properties to tune toward, and the levers that act on them. This draft is grounded in four cards: hunger to mobility, population growth, the surplus that supports towns and an elite, and goodwill beside silver. Three more cards are seeds and are not drawn yet.

What draft 3 shows

  • The split of the surplus is the main dial. Eaten, taken, feasted, sold, rotted: each outlet feeds a different part of the map. Feasting feeds goodwill, sale feeds the town, taking feeds the elite, and rot feeds nothing.
  • Today only two outlets and the sink work. Food is eaten, a little is brewed, and the rest rots. That is why a surplus of 1.1 to 1.6 supports nobody.
  • Silver thins goodwill by competing for grain. Nothing takes goodwill's place among kin. A tax in silver makes farms sell what they would have feasted.
  • Help is the gate to both roads up. Where kin help falls short, a household either borrows (debt, then land) or takes a patron's gift (goodwill, then work and votes). Both end in land concentration.
  • Extraction still has no brake of its own. R1 tightens itself, B5 opposes it only at the land step and slowly, and the floor is the one hard stop.
